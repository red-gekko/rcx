EPISODE - Joy to the Just
	To most, the hot Californian sun was an oppressive god. Peeled skin and cooked flesh; dried crops and tired men; chapped lips and hot rashes - that was its worship, its liberty.
	Logan rolled his shoulders as the orange tyrant crawled behind the Sierra Nevadas; he enjoyed it. The peel. The cook.
	Feeling. Something he held onto, as hard as he could.
	Not the healthiest coping mechanism; but it was his, and his alone.
	Bucey nickered at the approaching settlement, as Logan reached forward and stroked his neck.
	"Good boy..." he smiled softly.
	"Evenin', mister." he nodded as he passed a townsman, half-sleeping at the entrance.
	"Hm? Oh, hey mister." the man nodded. "Welcome to Hope, San Joaquin."
	"Hope?" Logan grinned. "We could all use some of that, bub."
	"Indeed. Um... who's your friend, there?"
	The gagged man slung over Bucey's rear wriggled in protest.
	"He's a bad fella." Logan chuckled. "Where's the Sheriff's office?"
	"Down by the saloon, right next door. Can't miss it." the man pointed. "Sheriff McGillis insisted he be stationed there to keep an eye on the drunkards."
	"Smart man." Logan nodded. "See you around."

	The door swung open, and McGillis looked up from his newspaper.
	"Well, I'll be." he cocked an eyebrow. "Is that Ronnie Smalls?"
	"In the flesh." Logan grinned. "Gift wrapped and everythin'."
	McGillis motioned for his deputy to take Smalls away, and then extended his hand.
	"Sheriff Nolan McGillis. And you are?"
	"Jim Hawkins." Logan smiled, taking it and shaking.
	"Pleasure to meet you, Mr. Hawkins. So, I'm guessing you a bounty hunter?"
	"What gave it away? The tied-up outlaw, or the fact I sauntered into this dump and came straight here?"
	"The dry wit and attitude, mostly." McGillis grinned. "You lookin' for work, Mr. Hawkins?"
	"Matter of fact, I am." he nodded. "I'm pretty good at what I do."
	"But what you do ain't very nice." McGillis snarked. "Alright. Wesley Donovan - real piece of work, him. His gang have been robbing stagecoaches like it's 1659."
	"Sounds to me like I need a stagecoach, then." Logan stroked his chin.
	"You wanna use yourself as bait?"
	"Poisonous bait. Best kind." Logan chuckled. "You got one I can borrow?"
	"Yeah, matter of fact, I do." McGillis nodded. "Johnny Freeman, down by the workshop. He's a jumpy little fella on account of his history, but he'll fix you up a wagon no trouble."
	"Thanks." Logan bowed his head. "See you around, sheriff."
	"I sure hope you will, Mr. Hawkins." McGillis smiled. "Donovan's gang are savages - don't underestimate them."
	"Hey, nothin' wrong with a little savagery." Logan grinned, his teeth showing.

	"Johnny Freeman?" Logan called out, as he entered the cluttered barn.
	His ears pricked up, and heard Johnny's heart race.
	"Hey, easy. I ain't here for trouble."
	"H-h-hello?" Johnny asked, trembling. "A-are you with Smitty?"
	"Who the hell's Smitty?"
	At that, Johnny relaxed immediately, and rushed over.
	"S-sorry about that, mister. Johnny Freeman, at your service."
	"Jim Hawkins." Logan smiled, shaking his hand. "Sheriff McGillis told me you'd be able to fix me up a coach? I'm tryin' to draw out this Donovan character."
	"Donovan?! Mister, are you crazy?"
	"A little." Logan grinned. "Can you?"
	"Y-yeah, 'course." Johnny nodded hurriedly. "I'll get her ready before the evening."
	"Thanks." Logan smiled. "Hey... who's Smitty?"
	"He--he's a bad man, mister Hawkins." Johnny shivered. "He's the son of my old master."
	"Master? California was a free state." Logan tilted his head.
	"That didn't stop Master Calhoun from bringing us along with him from Texas during the Gold Rush." Johnny nodded awkwardly. "Smitty was a bushwacker during the War, and he never forgave me for havin' the audacity to be free. I been sharecropping for him ever since."
	"That ain't right." Logan growled. "Where's Smitty now?"
	"Probably the Saloon. He's always in there, when he ain't bullyin' or beatin' me."
	"I'll be back for my wagon in the mornin'." Logan nodded, heading off.

	Smitty and his boys cheered, as their fifth whiskeys of the night were clinked and downed.
	The saloon went silent, as the doors swung open. The floorboards creaked on Logan's entrance.
	"Evenin', mister." he nodded at the barman. "I'll have a whiskey. Actually, make it two."
	"Comin' right up."
	"Hey, stranger." Smitty called over, joining him. "You new here?"
	"It would seem so." Logan nodded curtly. "You are?"
	"Smitty. Smitty Calhoun." he said, extending his hand.
	"I've heard of you." Logan scowled. "Now, you wanna listen up. You're gonna leave Johnny Freeman alone, you hear?"
	Smitty scoffed. "Oh, you're one of them bleeding-hearts, ain't ya? Where you from, boy? New York?"
	"Canada." Logan grinned.
	Smitty and his boys laughed - a whiny, rattling sound. He put his hand on Logan's shoulder.
	"Listen here, Canuck. I don't know how things work in Limey Country, but over here? You don't talk that way."
	Logan growled. "Take your dirty hand off me, kid, or I'll make you eat that bar."
	"Hah! Kid? Boy, I'm older than you! Go on - threaten me again." he grinned, putting his free hand on his revolver.
	Logan flashed his teeth, and then--*SLAM!*
	He wrapped his arm around Smitty's shoulder, and slammed his head mouth-first into the bar. Then he raised him up, and slammed again.
	Smitty collapsed to his knees, his teeth hitting the ground one by one.
	"Shit, hellfire!" the bartender gasped, dropping down in fear.
	The first of his boys came with a knife. Logan gripped it blade-first, and drove his elbow into the man's throat. Then, he shot his boot up another's chin, and finally, a haymaker sent the third man sprawling.
	"HEEEEY!" McGillis roared, storming in. "WHAT IN TARNATION IS GOIN' ON DOWN HERE?!"
	"Sheriff." Logan grinned. "These boys jumped me outta nowhere. Threatened me. Ain't that right?"
	Silence.
	"I *said,* ain't that right?"
	Murmurs of agreement came from the bruised, bloodied men.
	"Is that so?" McGillis scowled, walking over. "Mr. Hawkins... let me be clear. You can stay here tonight because I need Donovan swinging. But you start anything like this in my town again... it'll be you up on my gallows."
	"Oh, me? Sir, I'm a real church boy." Logan chuckled. "You won't hear any more trouble from me."
	"I'd better not." McGillis nodded. "Alright, everyone, show's over. Bed time."
	"Yes, sheriff." the patrons whispered, walking off to their homes.
	Logan rolled his shoulders, and smiled devilishly. He hopped onto Bucey's back, and they trotted downstream. There, he pitched his tent, and curled into a ball.
	
	They were itchy. Itchier than normal.
	They *hated* that they weren't being used.
	Logan sighed, and closed his eyes - then, his fists.
	*SNIKT!*
	He grimaced, as the bone claws cut from his hands. He reopened his eyes, and looked down at the bloody blades. Then he shifted them back in, and watched intently as the cut flesh knitted itself back together.
