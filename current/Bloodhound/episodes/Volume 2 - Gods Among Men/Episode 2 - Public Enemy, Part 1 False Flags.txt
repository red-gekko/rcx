VOLUME - Gods Among Men
EPISODE - Public Enemy, Part 1: False Flags
============================================================

SCENE - Metro Gassing

	Drackhill was, to most, an icon of urban rot. Nothing exemplified it more than the Underhill; that labyrinth of tunnels, storm drains, basements, and smuggling routes that snaked under the city.
	However, amongst the dirt and decay, there was the rare concrete worm that moved with light and life.
	The Drackhill Subway System.
	At the Redbridge Central Terminal, some trains pulled in while others raced off. Flocks of humans mobbed around, moving towards their destinations.
	Lena was seventeen, and she hated it here. She hated the crowds, the smell, the way the lights flickered like they were powered by hamsters. But it was cheaper than the bus, so there she was - pressed between a man in a suit who smelled of too much cologne, and a woman with a pram that took up half the carriage.
	She checked her phone. Two stops to go. She could make it.
	The train slowly screeched to a halt. The doors hissed open, and the usual tide of people flowed in and out. Lena stayed put, her eyes on her screen.
	Then, she heard commotion up ahead.
	"Sorry, sir - there seems to be an issue with the gates." a ticket officer said unsurely, as he tried and failed to get them to open.
	"Come on! I'm gonna miss my train!" a commuter on the entry side begged.
	Lena's eyebrow cocked, as she waited for the train to pull out. It didn't.
	Not just hers, either. Trains were pulling in quicker than they were pulling out, it seemed. The station was unusually crowded. There were a lot more bodies than normal.
	Two figures moved through the maintenance tunnels beneath the station. They were wearing sunglasses, but beneath the tint, black veins pulsed around their eyes.
	"Timer's set." one of them reported.
	"Confirmed. Sixty seconds."
	They moved quickly, exiting on the platform, and moving.
	Behind them, a canister with a clear, colourless contents clicked into place.
	Then, it drained - right into the ventilation system.
	The aircon units hissed, as gas spilled out of them.
	Train doors opened, and refused to close. The drivers cocked their eyebrows in confusion, as the controls rejected them.
	Then, the choking started. Small, at first - the kind most brushed off as a winter cold.
	Lena coughed.
	It started as a tickle in her throat, something she could ignore. Then, it got worse. Her chest tightened, and her breath came in short, sharp gasps.
	She looked around, and saw that others were coughing too. The man in the suit was clutching his chest. The woman with the pram was on her knees, her baby beginning to stir.

	"I'm just saying." Trey argued, waving a chip for emphasis. "If you're going to put pineapple on a pizza, you might as well put bananas on it too. It's the same principle."
	"It is absolutely not the same principle." Bea countered, grinning. "Pineapple is a fruit that works with savoury. Banana is a dessert fruit. I mean, they're both gross, but for different reasons."
	"I add pineapple to pizza, just to upset the Italians." Amy snarked.
	"Oh, I do that. I snap my spaghetti in half before boiling it, as a big fuck-you to Mussolini." Connor grinned, sipping his coffee.
	The gang laughed around their table, as Raj took their empty plates.
	"We'd better think about heading off, soon." Chloe began. "I've got lectures in an hour."
	"Oh, lectures, shmectures." Bea grinned.
	"I didn't know you were studying Yiddish." Trey quipped.
	"Oy, vey..." Connor chuckled, leaning back. Then - his ears pricked up. Ever-so-slightly.
	Noise. Beneath their feet.
	Screaming. Choking. Crying.
	Dying.
	"I, uh... I'll be right back." he said quickly.
	"Something wrong?" Chloe asked, concerned.
	"Hm? Yeah, just--need the loo."
	"They don't have bidets here, by the way." Trey grinned. "I miss them already."
	Connor forced a half-hearted laugh, and rushed out to the back. He pushed through the bathroom door, locked it behind him, and closed his eyes. He let out a breath, and his civilian clothes rippled, melted, and reformed. The red hoodie darkened to black. The trenchcoat unfurled from his shoulders. His mask crawled up his face, and when he opened his eyes, they burned a familiar crimson. He squeezed out of the bathroom window, and dived out.

	The metro entrance was chaos. People were streaming up the stairs, coughing, crying, clutching each other. A few had collapsed on the pavement, gasping for air. Sirens were already approaching in the distance.
	"Shit." he gasped, getting a whiff of the gas.
	Odourless. Invisible. And yet, he felt his mouth go numb already.
	Shit. Nerve gas? Shit!
	Below, the unlucky people who hadn't managed to move quick enough were collapsing, and going still, as their lungs seized up.
	Connor's eyes darted around. He had to be quick.
	Then, a sound cut into his mind.
	The pushchair next to Lena fell, and the baby rolled out. He moved with a speed he wasn't even aware he could manage, and rushed to the infant, cradling him.
	No.
	The baby was still. His heart had already stopped. He was just... too small.
	Connor pounced, to the mother. Gone, too.
	A wheeze, and a faint heartbeat shook him from his stupor.
	Lena choked, and Connor squeezed her hand. His eyes blazed, and he looked around, as quick as he could.
	People were dying. People were dying, and he wasn't saving them.
	"Come on... where are you?" he snarled, frantically searching.
	There. Just beneath the platform.
	No time for subtlety.
	Biomass shifted from Connor's flesh into his arms, and thick, hammer-like fists calcified. He brought one gauntlet down onto the platform floor. Then another. Then another.
	Finally, he broke through. Beneath, he could see the compromised ventilation system, as nerve gas poured into it. The canister still had plenty left - enough for everyone in the station, and then some.
	No. Not if he had something to say about it.
	He took a step toward it, and his legs nearly gave out. His vision swam. The gas was affecting him - slower than a human, but it was affecting him.
	Fuck. Fuck.
	He dropped to his knees, then forced himself back up. One step. Another. His body was already working overtime, repairing the damage to his lungs, his nervous system, his eyes. But it wasn't fast enough.
	He reached the canister. His hands shook as he gripped it, and his palms morphed, producing a thick, black tar that spread over the metal like living ink. He forced it into every gap, every seam, every crack.
	Finally, mercifully, the hissing stopped.
	His legs buckled, and he collapsed beside the canister, his eyes dimming.
	Darkness took him, like it did so many others.
	Except, unlike him - they would never come back.
	As his brain flickered into coma, one feeling gnawed on his hearts.
	One he hated that he was getting used to.
	Survivor's guilt.

	The firefighters arrived first. They'd dealt with gas leaks before, but nothing like this. They tightened their gas masks, and moved in.
	"Christ..." one of them breathed, looking at the rows of the dead. "What kind of monster does this?"
	"Focus." their chief ordered. "We need to check for survivors."
	They moved through the platform, checking pulses, calling out for anyone still alive. Most weren't.
	"Sarge!" one called out. "Got a survivor here. He's in a bad way!"
	"On it!" his superior nodded, rushing over. They pulled out a stretcher, and helped carry him up.
	"Holy shit!" another gasped. "Chief! I, uh... I found the source."
	"What is it? Some kind of bleach spill?"
	"No sir, it's..." he shuddered. "You wanna see for yourself."
	The chief rushed over, and looked down into the broken hole in the platform floor. There, he saw a man in black and red kevlar, sprawled out and unbreathing, next to a gas canister.
	"It's... it's him, sir."
	"Jesus..." the chief crossed himself. "Is he alive?"
	"I don't know. His chest isn't moving."
	"I was there, you know." the chief whispered. "At the barn in Blackmarsh. I had nightmares about that; right up until the flyover collapsed. Then I saw him again - and he saved people. And now... he died to save even more."
	"ARMED POLICE!"
	A squad of officers, clad in tactical armour and gas masks, stormed in. They raised their shotguns in the air, and moved towards the platform.
	"PS Kyle Dent, Counter-Terrorism." their leader barked. "Is that the source?"
	"Seems so." the chief nodded. "Jack Osborne - fire chief."
	Dent didn't shake his hand. Instead, he aimed his gun at Connor's still body.
	They approached the prone figure, rifles raised. One of them kicked his arm. No reaction.
	"He's dead, sir."
	"Make sure."
	Osborne stepped in front of them. "The hell you are. This man just saved hundreds of lives!"
	"Step aside, or we'll arrest you for obstruction, and aiding and abetting a terrorist."
	"What?!"
	Dent raised his weapon. "Last warning."
	And then - a noise. A ragged, rattling breath. The Werewolf's eyes snapped open, burning a brilliant, furious red.
	"SHIT! TAKE HIM DOWN!"
	The bullets slammed into his hide, and he moved like a cheetah. Within nanoseconds, he was on the officers, and shoved them out of the way in the same manner one clears a desk.
	Connor stood over them, chest heaving. He looked at Osborne, who nodded once.
	He nodded back, then turned and fled into the tunnels.

	Connor crawled back in through the bathroom door, morphed into his civvies, and unlocked the stall. He cleared his throat, and forced himself to look presentable. Then, he re-entered the cafe.
	"There you are! What took you so long?!" Chloe gasped, rushing over.
	"Sorry - I don't think last night's curry agreed with me." he quickly covered. "What did I miss?"
	Trey shuffled awkwardly. "There, um... there's something going down at the metro station."
	"Oh, shit. What happened?" Connor asked, subdued slightly.
	"No one knows. Cops and firemen have gone in." Amy shrugged. "Anyway, I need to get to work - can you give me a lift?"
	"Yeah, no worries." Connor nodded. "I'll see you guys tonight, yeah?"
	"Yeah, looking forward to it." Chloe said, brushing his arm.
	He nodded, before heading to the car.

	"So... what happened?" Amy asked, as they both shuffled into their seats.
	"I don't know. Some kind of nerve gas attack... I..."
	"Hey..." she whispered, as his eyes welled up. "You did your best."
	"Tell that to everyone who just died. Tell that to their families. Tell it to..." he choked, his hearts aching.
	"To?"
	"A... a baby. Was one of the victims." he finally admitted.
	"Oh, Connor..." she said softly, hugging him. "I'm so sorry... it's not your fault, okay? It's not."
	"It is! It's my responsibility to save people, and I--"
	"Did you do everything you could?"
	"Well, yeah--"
	"Then it's not your fault." she said firmly. "You wanna make things better? Let's start by finding out who did this."
	"Yeah. And then... I'm going to make them pay." he growled. He grabbed his phone, and unlocked it - before it started ringing.
	"Oh, hey Shannon." he began. "I was just about to call you. I'm guessing you've heard about the gas attack by now?"
	"Connor..." she said worriedly. "You need to see this."
	"See what?"
	"Check the news. Channel One's doing a broadcast now."
	Connor tapped his screen, and swapped over to his web browser. There, he quickly found the newsreader speaking.
	"...confirmed that the attack on Drackhill's Redbridge Terminal was carried out using a military-grade nerve agent. At least forty-seven people are confirmed dead, with dozens more injured. The attack was claimed by the vigilante known as the Werewolf--"
	"WHAT?!" Connor barked, his eyes aflame. Amy leaned in, her skin going pale.
	The screen switched to a video. It was him. Or someone who looked exactly like him. The same costume, the same mask, the same burning red eyes. But the posture was wrong. The voice was wrong. When he spoke, it was with a cold, measured cruelty that made Connor's stomach turn.
	"People of Drackhill. You have worshipped me as a hero. You have called me a protector. But I am done protecting the inferior. I have watched you, judged you, and found you wanting."
	The image leaned closer, and Connor could see the details now - the mask was slightly off, the eyes not quite the right shade of red. But to anyone else, it would look identical.
	"No more. Listen and obey."
	Connor gasped at the words - the Commandant's words. They cut like a knife.
	The video ended. The newsreader returned, her expression grim.
	"The city government has declared a state of emergency. We go now to Mayor Norris..."
	"The cowardly attack by this despicable vigilante terrorist reveals what he's always been - a killer, a monster. Him, and those who have protected, promoted, and supported him, will be routed out from our society! Mark my words - the Werewolf will face justice for this crime!"
	Connor stood frozen, the world around him dissolving into noise and static.
	"They framed me." he whispered.
	"I know. The video's a pretty obvious forgery, but the news is just running with it. They've also replayed your interrogation of Becker - they're presenting a narrative that you're a crazy terrorist."
	"It's gotta be Norris, right?" Amy cocked an eyebrow. "The election is in a couple of days - he's gonna use this to his advantage."
	"Yeah, but... a gas attack, right here in the city? That's gotta leave plenty of threads." Shannon nodded. "I'll start digging - but you might wanna do some investigations of your own."
	"Norris is dead." Connor growled. "He killed all those people, and--"
	"Connor." Amy said firmly. "You can't kill him. Not yet. We need proof he was behind it first, otherwise you're just making him a martyr, and telling everyone that he's right."
	"Agreed. I'm sorry, but... you need to hold off on the Rage, just for a moment."
	Connor hesitated. It would be so easy. Kill, kill, kill...
	"Fine..." he whispered at last. "But when it comes... I'll savour it."

SCENE - Checking in on Lena

	"You've been staring at your phone for five minutes." Sparky noted.
	"I know." Connor sighed. "I'm just... trying to figure out what to say."
	"To who?"
	"Judi." he rubbed his temples. "I need to know how things are in A&E. But... well, she might have reservations about working with a wanted 'terrorist,' now."
	"Just call her." Sparky nudged his hand. "You won't know until you try."
	Connor took a deep breath, and dialled.
	It rang three times before she answered. She didn't say anything.
	"It's me." Connor said quietly. "I know you've seen the news."
	A long pause.
	"I have. I... I don't know what to say. The whole city is in lockdown. They're saying you killed dozens of people. Children."
	"I didn't." his voice cracked. "Doc, I swear to you, I didn't do this. I tried to stop it. I sealed the canister. I almost died."
	Another pause. He could hear her breathing, slow and deliberate.
	"Why should I believe you?" she asked. "After everything... why should I trust you?"
	"Because I'm asking you to." Connor said simply. "Not demanding. Not threatening. Asking. You've seen what I do. You've seen the people I've saved. Am I someone who would gas a train full of civilians?"
	Silence. Then, a heavy sigh.
	"No." Judi admitted. "But that's not what the law says."

	"I'm sorry. I'm sorry for putting you in this position. But people are going to die if I don't stop whoever did this. The attack today was just the beginning - I can feel it."

	"There was one." Judi finally said, her voice barely above a whisper. "A girl. Seventeen. She was... she was calling out for you. Before she lost consciousness. She kept saying 'Werewolf,' over and over again."

	"Yes. You know her?"
	"I remember." Connor sighed. "What's her name?"
	"Lena. Lena Novak. She's in the ICU. Her mother is on her way - she's caught in traffic, but she's on the phone with her now."

	Connor scaled the hospital wall, and slipped through a window, his boots morphing to muffle his footsteps.
	The corridor was empty, for now. He moved quickly, keeping to the shadows.
	Lena's room was at the end of the hall. Through the glass, he could see her - a young girl, pale against the white sheets, an oxygen mask over her face. Her eyes were open, staring at the ceiling. A phone lay on the bed beside her, the screen still lit up.
	He pushed the door open slowly. Lena's head turned, and her eyes went wide.
	"W-what..." she stammered, her voice muffled by the mask. "No... no, please..."
	"I'm not here to hurt you." Connor said quickly, raising his hands. "I swear."
	"You... you did this!" she gasped, trying to push herself up. Her arms shook with the effort. "You killed all those people! You!"
	"I didn't." Connor said, his voice cracking. "I know you don't believe me, but I didn't. I was there - I tried to stop it. I sealed the canister."
	Lena's breath came in short, panicked gasps. Her heart monitor beeped faster.
	"I remember you." she whispered. "You... you were on the platform. You looked at me. You..."
	"I tried to save you." he nodded. "Please, you have to believe me."
	Lena stared at him for a long moment. Then, slowly, she relaxed back against the pillows.
	"I need to know what you know. What happened before the gas came?"
	"I don't remember much." she admitted. "Just... people screaming. And then I couldn't breathe. And then... nothing."

	"What do you mean?"
	"I have... abilities." he said carefully. "I can see memories. If you let me, I can look at what you saw - maybe find something that can help me find the people who did this."
	Lena's eyes widened. "You want to... go into my head?"
	"I know it sounds scary." Connor said quickly. "And I understand if you say no. But I think you saw something - maybe without even realising it. Something that could help."
	Lena hesitated. Her heart monitor beeped steadily.
	"Will it hurt?"
	"No." Connor shook his head. "It might feel... strange. But it won't hurt. And it'll help me make sure this never happens again."
	Another long pause.
	"Okay." she whispered. "Do it."
	Connor reached out, and gently placed his hand on her forehead.
	The connection was immediate. He felt her fear, her confusion, her exhaustion. And beneath that, a flicker of hope - faint, but still there.
	He pushed deeper, into her recent memories. The train. The crowds. Sudden panic.
	Choking. Falling. And then...
	There. Behind Lena's knowledge of poetry. Just next to her favourite ice cream flavour. And just before the attack. Two figures, moving on the platform. Sunglasses. Black veins around their eyes.
	Super-Agents.
	The link wasn't one-way. Lena watched as Connor caressed the baby. She saw her own face choke, as Connor squeezed her hand. She felt him frantically try to stop it. Stop the dying.
	He pulled back, and Lena gasped, her eyes snapping open.
	"What... what did you see?" she asked.
	"Two men." Connor said grimly. "They're the ones who did this. They went into the tunnels just before the gas was released. They're connected to the conspiracy I'm fighting. And I'm pretty sure the mayor is too - which means I'm getting closer."
	"You... didn't do it." Lena whispered, feeling the memory resurface.
	"I really didn't." Connor confirmed.
	Lena closed her eyes. A single tear slipped down her cheek.
	"The baby..." she whispered. "His name was Thomas. I heard his mum call him that. He was... he was so small."
	Connor's hearts ached.
	"I'm going to find them." he vowed. "The people who did this. And I'm going to make sure they never hurt anyone again."
	Lena nodded, and he slipped out the window.
	He was halfway down the wall when his earpiece buzzed.
	"Vanguard." Shannon began. "There's something happening."
	"What? What is it?"
	"The Calder Crossing. Someone's blown it up."
	Connor's blood ran cold. "What?"
	"There are cars in the water. People screaming. It's... it's chaos. I've called Spartacus - he's en route."
	"I'm on my way." Connor snarled. He dropped to the ground, and ran.

SCENE - Bridge Collapse

	Fires were visible at the struts of the bridge. Smoke billowed out from detonation sites, while cars washed into the Calder.
	Connor and Sparky landed hard on the riverbank, their eyes scanning the chaos.
	"Okay, here's the plan." Connor began. "You take the bridge, I'll take the river. Your job is to rescue anyone trapped in their vehicles. I'll try and rescue anyone underwater. You got that?"
	"Got it." Sparky barked, ready for action. "Let's do this."
	Connor dived into the water, swimming straight for a sinking car. Inside, he saw a family, panicking.
	He reached the car, and ripped the door off. The father inside screamed, and pushed himself away.
	"No! No, please!" he begged, shielding his children. "Don't hurt us!"
	"I'm not here to hurt you!" Connor shouted over the rushing water. "I'm trying to save you!"
	"You killed all those people! You gassed the metro!" the mother shrieked, clutching her baby.
	"That wasn't me!" he barked, reaching in and grabbing the father's arm. "Look at me! If I wanted you dead, you'd be dead! I'm getting you out!"
	The father hesitated, his eyes wide with terror. But the water was rising, and his children were crying. He nodded, and Connor pulled him free, then the mother, then the children.
	He pushed them onto the riverbank, and dived back in.
	He repeated the action on another car - this time, an elderly woman who screamed and clawed at his face as he pulled her free.
	He didn't flinch - he just carried her to shore, and went back for more.
	Meanwhile, Sparky dragged wounded people from their cars. He heard a scream, and rushed over - a young man was hanging on the edge of the bridge. Sparky grabbed the man's arm with his mouth, and gently pulled him up.
	The man stood, and looked at him, shocked. Then, his face twisted in fear.
	"Get away from me!" he shouted, stumbling backwards. "You're one of them!"
	Sparky whined, and stepped back, his ears flat.
	He shook himself, and returned to the wrecked cars. There were still people to save.
	Connor surfaced, gasping for air. He'd saved everyone he could find in the water. Now, he climbed onto the riverbank, dripping and exhausted.
	A woman was trapped in her car on the bridge, screaming. Connor scaled the struts, and approached her.
	"No! Stay away!" she shrieked, trying to roll up her window. It was already broken. "I know what you did! I know what you are!"
	"I didn't do this." he said firmly. "I'm trying to save you. Please, let me help."
	She stared at him, her breath coming in short, panicked gasps. Then, she looked at the drop below her, and at the fire spreading towards her car.
	"Please." Connor repeated, reaching out his hand. "I won't hurt you. I promise."
	She hesitated. Then, slowly, she took his hand.
	He pulled her free, and carried her down to the riverbank. She collapsed onto the grass, sobbing.
	"Thank you." she whispered. "I'm sorry. I'm so sorry."
	"It's okay." Connor nodded, already turning away. "Stay here."

	No sooner had Connor finished saving people before the sirens arrived.
	A convoy of police vans screeched to a halt at the edge of the bridge. Armed officers poured out, their weapons raised.
	"STAY WHERE YOU ARE!" one of them shouted. "HANDS WHERE WE CAN SEE THEM!"
	Connor raised his hands slowly, his eyes dimming. "Easy. We're not here for a fight."
	"SHUT UP!" another officer barked. "You're under arrest for the bombing of the Calder Crossing, and for the murder of forty-seven people at Redbridge Terminal!"
	"That wasn't us!" Connor snapped, his eyes flaring. "We just pulled people out of the water! Ask them!"
	"We've got eyewitnesses who say you were planting charges on the bridge!" a third officer shouted. "Now get on your knees, or we open fire!"
	Connor's jaw tightened. "Please." he said, softer now. "Don't do this. I'm not your enemy."
	"ON YOUR KNEES! NOW!"
	The lead officer raised his hand. "Last warning!"
	Then, a new sound. A low, mechanical hum, growing louder.
	Three drones descended from the sky, slicing through the air. They were sleek, black, and armed - the same Eidolon model that Paladin had deployed months ago. The CT drones.
	These weren't Paladin's. They were painted with the insignia of Drackhill Police.
	"Deploying counter-terrorism drones." the lead officer said into his radio. "Targets confirmed."
	The drones formed a triangle, their weapons trained on Connor and Sparky.
	"You've gotta be fuckin' kidding me." Connor snarled.
	"FIRE!"
	The drones unleashed their guns.
	Connor threw himself down, his biomass surging to form a ballistic shield on his forearm. The bullets slammed into it, each impact ringing through his bones.
	"Spartacus!" he barked. "Drones are yours! Cops are mine!"
	"Got it!" Sparky pounced onto the bridge, his claws finding purchase on the twisted metal. He launched himself at the nearest drone, his jaws clamping around its rotor. It sparked and shuddered, and he ripped it free, sending the drone spiralling into the river.
	Connor charged the police line. The officers opened fire, but he was already moving - a blur of black and red that tore through their formation. A punch to the jaw. A sweep of the legs. A slam into the side of a van.
	"STAY DOWN!" he roared.
	One officer tried to raise his weapon. Connor grabbed it, bent the barrel in half, and tossed it aside. A second officer swung a baton at his head. Connor let it hit, and turned, eyes aflame.
	"Don't! P-please! Don't kill me!"
	*THUMP!*
	The man clutched his bruised gut, and folded like a deck chair.
	The second drone fired a missile. Connor dived, and it detonated against the bridge, sending shrapnel flying. He rolled, came up, and morphed his crossbow. A bolt punched through the drone's central processor, and it dropped like a stone into the Calder.
	The third drone retreated, hovering above the river. It was almost smarter than the others - it kept its distance, tracking Sparky's movements.
	Sparky was faster. He pounced from strut to strut, zigzagging across the bridge, until he launched himself at the drone. His jaws clamped around its undercarriage, and he ripped it free. The drone spiralled, and crashed into the water.
	Silence.
	Connor stood there, chest heaving, surrounded by groaning officers. His costume was torn, his mask cracked, and blood seeped from a dozen wounds.
	Sparky limped over, his fur singed, and pressed against his leg.
	"You okay?" Connor asked.
	"Yeah." Sparky panted. "You?"
	"I'll live."
	The lead officer was still conscious, sprawled against the side of his van. He stared up at Connor, his eyes wide.
	"Don't... don't kill me, please!"
	"Why not?" Connor snarled, raising the man by the throat. Rage boiled inside him - one he was suppressing for too long.
	"Please! Please!"
	"You were trying to kill me." Connor growled lowly. "You shot me. Sicced drones on me. Blew me the FUCK UP! AND YOU HAVE THE *BALLS* TO BEG FOR MERCY?!"
	"CONNOR!" Sparky barked. "Put him down, now."
	Connor turned, locking eyes with his packmate. Then, he released his grip.
	He stumbled along the bridge, as his muscles seized with exhaustion.
	Then, his legs gave out.
	He collapsed, his body finally succumbing to the damage it had taken. The last thing he saw was Sparky's yellow eyes, wide with panic, before the darkness took him.
	Sparky tried to grab him, but his own body was failing. He collapsed beside his packmate, and the two of them slid into the water.

	Hours passed.
	The first light of dawn was beginning to creep over the horizon when Connor's eyes flickered open. He was lying on his back, half-submerged in mud, the taste of river water and blood in his mouth.
	He groaned, and forced himself to sit up. Beside him, Sparky stirred, shaking mud from his fur.
	"You okay?" Connor asked, his voice hoarse.
	"Yeah, I'm fine." Sparky stumbled over. "Are you?"
	"I'll live." Connor groaned, lying back down. Hot blood seeped from his nostrils. "Fucking cops... you'd think after everything, they'd cut us some slack."
	"Maybe one day." Sparky quipped, curling up next to him.
	"Who the fuck blows up a bridge?" Connor chuckled, wincing in pain.
