VOLUME - Wolves Among Sheep
EPISODE - Law and Disorder
============================================================

Connor stepped into the Singing Horse late at night, nodding to Pawel. The dim light barely masked the bar's wear and tear, but it felt familiar - like home, if home were a sketchy nightclub. Sparky trotted dutifully at his heel.
In the back-office, Tommy sat at his desk, while Trey stood.
"Look, Trey, you're a good kid... you sure you wanna do this?" he asked.
"Connor's a good kid too, and he's one of your top enforcers now." Trey retorted. "Besides, after the other week - the Skinners don't respect whether or not you're actually in. So, no harm, no foul?"
"Harm. Foul." Tommy sighed.
"Look, my dad - he's worried about the mortgage. I just... we need the extra cash. You know how fast I can move bits."
Tommy chuckled. "That I do... alright, fuck it. Pick up from Mo's place tomorrow at 8PM, drop off in the usual place in Bristol. Don't be an idiot - take the long route. And for Christ's sake, don't use your own car."
Trey cheered silently. "You won't regret this."
"Make sure you don't either." Tommy warned. "Believe it or not, I don't actually want you kids to get arrested."
With that, Connor knocked on the door.
"Right on cue. Come in." Tommy gestured.
"Connor! Have a seat." he motioned. He put down the paper he was working on, and smiled.
"Trey?" Connor asked.
"Hey, bro!" Trey smiled, scratching the back of his head. "Guessing this is my cue to leave, right? Top secret, and all that."
Tommy cleared his throat. "What's he talking about, Connor?"
"I told him how the Werewolf came to visit the other week." Connor explained, twitching his eye slightly. "How we're helping him fight the Skinners."
Tommy leaned back, nodding subtly. "Well, Trey - I'm sure you understand why that needs to be kept on the DL."
Trey nodded. "Of course, *boss.*"
"Boss?" Connor cocked an eyebrow, before nodding in realisation. "Be careful, yeah bro? You need a bodyguard, you know where I am."
"I could say the same." Trey chuckled. "Let me know if any sexy vampires show up."
Connor smirked, and took a seat.
As Trey turned, he saw Sparky traipsing in.
"Oh my goodness! Whosagoodboyyy? You are!" he cooed.
"No, you are!" Sparky chuckled, taking his seat by Connor as Trey left.
"Someone's been a busy boy, eh?" Tommy grinned.
"Hah, you could say that..." he nodded.
"Don't sell yourself short. A little birdie told me that a certain Werewolf killed Donovan." Tommy smiled, pouring both a drink.
Connor chuckled, as the pair clinked their glasses. "That nickname's really growing on people, eh? Oh, speaking of..." he motioned downwards. "This is Sparky. The 'wolf' to my 'were.'"
"Hello, boy." Tommy smiled. "Damn, he's a big one!"
"He can speak. And he has my powers." Connor continued nonchalantly.
"Jesus... he can speak?"
"Only to me. Perks of being a superhuman." Connor chuckled.
Tommy grinned. "With Donovan dead, the Skinners are in disarray. But..." his expression turned serious, "We've been seeing more of those... black-veined freaks."
"Super-Agents." Connor said, sipping his drink. "That's what I've been calling them. Mutants who've taken a serum like the one they gave me. Donovan was one - so was that freak who came for me a couple months ago. But they're weaker than me - I'm not sure why."
"Maybe what they gave you kills most people, so they made a weaker, safer version?" Tommy pondered aloud.
"Maybe. The more pressing question is, where the hell are random Skinners getting their hands on super-serum?" Connor tilted his head.
"Either way, you've been fighting the good fight. Where do you get your info from?"
"I have a guy - a friend in the KGB. He's been feeding me intel on these freaks. There's this... cabal."
"Go on?"
"The Skinners are stooges for this company - Gambit Security Services. Gambit is a front for Paladin PMC. And Paladin is a front for the Optimates."
"Who?"
"The cabal. The conspiracy." he sighed. "An international cartel of the ruling class. They're real pieces of work - and somehow, they're involved in whatever happened to me."
"Holy shit... " Tommy nodded. "Well, speaking of shadowy money, is there a reason an IT consultancy firm based in the Bahamas has been sending us cash?" Tommy asked, eyebrow raised.
"Killing Hughes wasn't only for the sake of justice. MI5 had a price on his head, and the KGB contracted me to take him out."
"And they're not part of the conspiracy, then? I'd've thought MI5 would be the first to join up..."
"It's... complicated." Connor shrugged. "The Optimates officially don't exist - which means that they can't give outright orders. The guy who contracted me wanted Hughes gone."
"Well, good to hear it. With that money, I've managed to reopen the repair shop, double wages across all our owned firms, and expand into other sectors. With the Skinners getting stronger, we're being seen as the only defence. Shops and pubs are coming to us for protection before we even ask them." Tommy smiled.
"Well, that's what the Hoods have always been about, eh?" Connor cocked an eyebrow. "I should probably do a few shifts. But hey, been busy."
"By the way... the police suspect you're involved with us. Well, not you, the Werewolf... but still. There's this detective that came round - I've got history with him. DI Henry Naismith. Supposedly, a stand-up guy - for a copper." Tommy said pensively.
"Receding brown hair?" Connor tilted his head.
"That's the one."
"He shot me in the face last week. Prick." Connor chuckled.
Tommy grinned. "And you busted his ribs. Plus, you know... you can heal shit."
"Hmm... I have an idea." Connor pondered.
"What?"
"The Optimates thrive in the shadows... you can't win a secret war against them." Connor stared into the distance. "But maybe... if I lead the investigation towards them..."
"Connor?" Tommy said, unsure.
"First things first - I'll have a talk with this 'Naismith'. I'll plan a strike against Paladin, and make sure he's there to see it."
"Smart move." Tommy nodded. "I won't lie, Naismith's close to a crisis of faith. Show him irrefutable proof of these freaks, he might just turn for you."
Connor smiled. "Call me if you need me. If these bastards hit us, I'm the one who can hit back."
"You've got it." Tommy nodded. "See you around, comrade."
"You too." Connor grinned. He turned and left, with Sparky lazily following.
"You can go to bed, you know." Connor smiled.
"I know." Sparky yawned. "I like spending time with you."
"Aww. I like spending time with you too, bud." Connor smiled warmly.
He pulled out his phone.
"Hey Verdict." he began. "Need some intel. Do the KGB have the ability to access police personnel files?"
Ivan audibly grinned. "Well, not *legally,* but we *are* spies. Name?"
"Naismith. DI Henry Naismith."

Naismith was in his home - a small terraced house. He woke up to his alarm, with a big, fluffy St. Bernard curled up at his feet. He sat up, yawned, and stroked the dog.
"Good boy." he said gently. The old dog raised his head, smiled, and went back to sleep. Naismith looked down at his alarm clock. It was 5AM.
He headed downstairs and made himself a coffee. He was wearing a dirty white T-shirt and boxers.
He drank his coffee, then headed into the bathroom. He noticed his stubble was threatening to grow into a full beard. He sighed, sprayed some deodorant, and got dressed. He gave his dog a stroke.
"I'll be back later, Max. I'll take you out for a walk, yeah?"
The dog smiled softly. Naismith headed to the kitchen, checked the dog door was still working, and filled Max's bowls with dog food and water. He sighed, aching somewhat, and headed outside to his car. He entered the car, sighed again, and closed his eyes.
With that, Connor leaned forward from the back seats.
"Morning, Detective." he said in a deep, gravelly voice.
Naismith reached for his gun, but Connor drew his crossbow, and pushed it into Naismith's temple.
"That would be very foolish." he growled. Naismith nodded and released, holding both hands in the air.
"Gun. Now." Connor barked. "Any sudden moves, this bolt gets coated in brain."
Naismith nodded. "Okay. Okay. I'm doing as you say."
He pulled out his handgun, and passed it to Connor. Satisfied, Connor put it down by his side.
"There." he said. "Now we can talk like grown ups. Never liked guns, anyway."
"What, not visceral enough a method of killing for your tastes?" Naismith snarled in an accusatory tone.
"Hm? No, I just don't want you to shoot me in the face again. Besides, it's the opposite." Connor chuckled grimly. "With your hands, it's much easier to subdue someone without killing them. You can be deliberate. Guns... it's hard to shoot someone non-lethally. The way I see it, if I use my hands, I make sure everyone I kill, I did it on purpose. My conscience can be clean."
"A clean conscience?! Did you see the man you mutilated at the Hog and Hounds? He's gonna spend the rest of his life in a care home, eating through a straw and shitting into a bag, because of *you!*"
"After he shot me in the face. People have a habit of doing that, it seems. Tell me, Detective - would you be in control of yourself after that?"
"I wouldn't be anything, I'd be dead - because I'm not some freak of nature!"
"Oh, I'm a freak all right - but nature had nothing to do with this." Connor chuckled. "Someone else did. And to find out who... I need your help."
"What?!" Naismith laughed incredulously. "That's a new one. Never had a criminal come to me for help."
"Naismith." Connor interrupted. "I read your file. Seems you're well known as the office rat - you've arrested 5 other officers in your career. And, you made it to Detective Inspector, which means you're clever. And cherry on top - you came through good on the Hughes job. So, a clever man with morals. If you can't see there's something bigger going on, no one can."
"I see two things - right and wrong. The law." Naismith inhaled. "And criminality. And I know which side I'm on. What I want, what I support, is the law. Rule by order. You... you're an anarchist. An insane malcontent."
"Well, I'm definitely a malcontent." Connor chuckled. "And I couldn't agree more - there's right and wrong. Black and white. Where you are wrong..." he continued, his mask hiding his smirk, "Is which side the law's on. The law is the arm of power. And power has a name. Optimates."
"What are you talking about?!" Naismith sighed, exasperated.
"No. If I tell you, you won't believe me. You need to see it for yourself." Connor replied. "I'm going to attack somewhere tonight. I won't tell you where, so you don't crash the party too early - but it's in Marsh Road. Have yourself and your people on standby, and listen out for sounds of a fight. It'll begin at 10 PM. Try not to shoot me in the face again."
Naismith shook his head. "Why would you tell me that?"
"So you can get there on time. And see the truth first-hand." Connor nodded.
Naismith thought for a moment. "It sounds like I'm doing your dirty work for you."
"No. You're responding to a threat from a serial killer, right?" Connor interjected. "A known, wanted criminal told you where he plans to strike. So, you have a duty to arrive on the scene, no? Also, you kind of owe me for trying to kill me last week. And besides - there's some Super-Agent bodies in it for you."
"Super-What?!" Naismith asked incredulously.
"Super-Agents. Not like me - the men I've been killing. You would have seen loads by now." Connor explained clinically. "They have black veins on their eyesockets. Donovan was one. Super-Agents are humans who have been given a serum that changes them, somehow. You've been sending their bodies to Vitalis, right?"
"How do you know about that? Our work with them is top-secret..."
"Hello? Anarchist malcontent?" Connor jeered. "Safe to say, I know you've been working with Vitalis. And there'll be more to send them tonight. Are you in, or not?"
"Fuck you." he growled. "I don't work with criminals, let alone ones that can carve up a young man's face!"
"What?"
"Barrett! Connor Barrett! Let me guess, you already forgot about the poor lad, didn't you?!"
Connor paused, before speaking again.
"Okay, fine. I went a little overboard with him. But he betrayed me. Besides, he's fine now."
"'Overboard?!' Are you shitting me?!"
"Why are you so upset, Detective? Because I hurt traitors... or because you used him, and it backfired?"
Naismith looked away, his eyes pained.
"Ah. That's why you came at me on your own, when I killed Donovan. That's the real reason, isn't it?"
"Shut up."
"Oh, I'm sure you told yourself plenty of good lies. But deep down... you couldn't stand to be the reason anyone else got hurt. It *sickened* you."
"I said SHUT UP!"
Connor grinned. "There. That fire. That's what this city needs. You know that, as well as I. Now, I'm gonna go and get ready to kill some people. You can get a little bit closer to the truth as a result, or not."
Naismith sighed. "...fine. I'll be there."
Connor breathed a sigh of relief. He hopped out of the car.
Naismith rested his head on the steering wheel, and sighed again.

The gang had gathered around Trey's again for the afternoon - a rare moment where they were all free from work or university - or, in Connor and Sparky's case, a brief repose before their killings that night. Trey had fetched lunch, and they all enjoyed a nice chilled time in.
Or, it would've been chilled, had Bea not decided to put the local news on.
"Are you fucking kidding me?!" she snapped at the TV. A debate between the two frontrunners for the mayoral elections - Helen Ward of the United Socialist Party, and incumbent Alan Norris of the British Unionist Party - was playing.
"I've heard this all before." Connor shrugged. "Though, to be fair, it's my natural instinct to spit at anyone with the word 'Unionist' in their description."
Trey laughed. "I like him." he said sarcastically.
"Trey, he wants to make sodomy illegal again." Bea replied, shocked and disturbed.
"Yeah, grand. I wanna feel dirty when I do it." Trey jeered. The gang erupted in a mix of laughs and groans of disgust - most at the same time.
"So... okay, don't judge me, but I know fuck all about politics." Amy prefaced in her characteristic tact. "What does the mayor actually do?"
"Currently, fuck all." Connor snarked. "As I understand it, they're elected by the public, and they decide who gets what job in cabinet - which is how they set overall, valley-wide policies. Then, the local councils - like the borough councils in the city, or the town councils out in the valley - are in charge of most day-to-day services, as well as actually implementing those policies."
Bea nodded. "That's basically it. The big problem is that the mayor and the councils are so separate - the mayor can't implement policies directly, while at the same time, the councils can't really hold the mayor accountable. The disconnect between the two branches of government is a big reason why nothing ever gets done. London has the exact same problem with its setup - we're in the awkward halfway between a regular county and a devolved government. But even London has an assembly."
"I'm guessing Ward wants to change that?" Trey grinned. "After all, she seems to be psychically stealing all her policy ideas from you."
Bea chuckled. "She's an advocate for valley devolution. But in the meantime, she wants councils to elect representatives to an advisory committee, to help bridge the gap."
"How come we have a mayor for the whole valley?" Chloe asked, egging Bea on. "I'd assume their jurisdiction would be Drackhill City, but you're saying their authority extends out as far as Brynmore."
"I'm not sure. One of the weird quirks of the valley, I guess." Bea shrugged.
"...why are you all looking at me?" Connor tilted his head, as a swathe of grinning faces turned to him.
"We're long overdue a history infodump." Amy snarked.
Connor rolled his eyes, laughing. "The mayoral office started out as the chief executive of the city as a whole; the boroughs were governed by magistrates, while other parishes had their own leaders - Danwich and Mercy Isle had Bishops, Angleport, Wickermast, and Pencroft had Mayors of their own; while Rexton, Bromney, Polwynn, and Blackmarsh had Barons - and all of them swore fealty to House Calder. But, after 1309, the Mayor took over the duties of the Earl, and then during industrialisation, most government functions got centralised in the city. So, the mayor was the de facto administrator for the whole valley; when the Local Government Act 1974 got put in place, they formalised the position."
Bea smirked, and pressed play.
"It's high time we stopped talking so much about 'Human Rights' and began taking seriously our Human Responsibilities. To each other, to our families, to our children." Norris said in response to a question about his views on abortion.
Bea paused it, infuriated. "What the hell kind of answer is that?!"
"That was fucking sinister..." Amy chuckled.
"'Enough about this 'human rights' rubbish, it's time to talk about human wrongs!'" Connor mockingly mimicked, to roars of laughter.
Bea unpaused it, watching Ward's response.
"Are you---Did I hear that right? Sorry, are you saying 'Enough about human rights, let's talk about human wrongs!'?!" Ward asked, bewildered.
The gang immediately roared "Oiiii!" in unison, while Connor took a bow.
"Psychic thief!" Trey giggled. "She robs the policies off Bea, and the rhetoric off you!"
"And for my next prophecy..." Connor grinned, before Amy kicked him in the rear.
"Didn't see that coming, did ya?" she jeered.
"Little shitbag..." he chuckled, sitting back down.
Chloe smiled. "It's good to see Bea in her element again." she whispered, while Bea shouted at another line from Norris.
"Yeah..." Amy nodded. "Good to see her hanging out with us all again, too."
"It's because Charlie's not here." Chloe said, hiding a scowl. "He's away for the week, thank God."
"Do you want me to look into him, a little?" Connor tilted his head.
"No, it's fine. I'm just... nah, it's fine." she shook her head back.

The moderator moved the debate to the audience questions section, as Bea leaned in, listening intently.
"My question is to both candidates." one person began. "As you're both aware, following the Local Government Act 2013, this will be the first election in which the responsibilities of the Police and Crime Commissioner will be folded into the office of mayor. What changes will you make to the Drackhill Valley police force after the election?"
"I would start by removing the restrictions placed upon the police by the political correctness mob, and reinstate Stop and Search." Norris nodded.
"Of course, Mr. Norris's answer is to reinstate a racist policy that damaged community trust." Ward snarked. "I would start by commissioning a full anti-corruption review of the force, and ensure moving forward that transparency and accountability are a core part of policing in this valley."
The moderator nodded. "Next, the man in the brown jumper with glasses."
"Thank you." he rose. "As mayor, you are responsible for the entire valley area, not just Drackhill City. What are some policies of yours that relate to those outside the city?"
"My opponent seeks to--" Norris began.
"He's asking you what *your* policies are." Ward grinned.
Norris cleared his throat, as the crowd murmured in chuckle. "I will... the BUP will cut council tax for rural areas, to relieve the awful burdens placed upon farmers."
"The same council taxes that spiked under your party's leadership." Ward quipped. "You *do* know the mayor doesn't directly control council tax, right? At most, we can affect the mayoral precept."
Subdued murmurs of laughter danced across the crowd, as Ward took a breath.
"I intend to implement free bus services on both sides of the valley, so that rural communities are able to connect with each other more efficiently. I also plan to refurbish Dunmere Railway, and bring back the ferry services from Angleport and Rexton, so you don't have to go all the way to Caldermouth just to take a trip to Brynmore."
"Next please, the woman with the red jacket and glasses."
She stood. "Increasingly, people are concerned about the vigilante attacks perpetrated by the so-called 'Werewolf.' Despite this, the police have been unable to identify a suspect, and some people even seem to support him. What are your plans to deal with this situation?"
"Vigilantism on our streets cannot be tolerated." Norris scowled. "This 'Werewolf' is a terrorist, and should be treated as such. Apprehending him will be my top priority after the election."
"It's clear that the violence is unacceptable." Ward nodded. "However, the police investigation has failed precisely because the PCC and the Chief Constable have refused to investigate the so-called Skinner gangs. It's not just the Werewolf that needs to be stopped - but the wider conspiracy he is clearly entangled with."
Connor nodded, smirking slightly. He made a mental note to include Norris as a suspect.

The debate ended in an official 'tie', but Ward had clearly emerged the victor. Bea was grinning wildly.
"Anyway... thanks for putting up with me there." she said shyly.
"Bea, I'm mates with Connor. Compared to his rants about the 'British Bastards,' this was tame." Trey quipped, chuckling.
"Feck yous." Connor snarked back.
Bea laughed. "Anyway... what's up with you guys? Any news?"
"Well, um..." Amy began. "I've applied for a new job."
"Oh my god, that's great news, hun!" Bea cheered. "Where for?"
"A dog shelter. It's like a mix of rescue and daycare." she explained, trying and failing to contain her excitement.
Connor looked on with pride. "Think you can take Sparky?" he quipped.
"Think they can take *you?*" Sparky hit back, half-asleep.
"Shut up, lazy-bones." Connor snarked.
"Eat my tail." Sparky winked, flopping his head back down.
"God, he's got such good banter for a dog." Trey grinned, hearing only the barks and whines, but getting the gist nonetheless.
"I'm really proud of you." Chloe nodded warmly. "It sounds like a really good opportunity."
"Well, I've not exactly got it yet." Amy admitted. "But here's hoping."

That night, Naismith and Nina sat in the car, parked up in Marsh Road. Nina was sipping a coffee cup, as Naismith looked out the window, deep in thought.
"So..." she began. "Do you want to tell me why we're here, sir?"
"I got a tip off from a CHIS I've been working with." Naismith shrugged. "Supposedly, the Werewolf plans to hit somewhere here tonight. We're always arriving late to these, so... if we get there as it happens, maybe we can catch the bastard."
Nina smiled and nodded. "It's a shame we couldn't get armed backup."
"Yeah... budget cuts." Naismith sighed. "Dorsey's putting all the pressure on us to catch this guy, but he's not giving us anything to work with..."
"Between you and me, sir... I think Dorsey was appointed Chief Constable because he knows how to please the politicians, rather than any actual competence."
She anxiously awaited Naismith's reaction, scared of seeming unprofessional. Naismith chuckled, causing her to relax.
"You got that right..." he replied, grinning. "Anyway... we've just gotta sit on our arses until something goes down. So, in the meantime..." he shrugged, "Uh... tell me about yourself?"
Nina chuckled. "You need to brush up on your interrogation technique, sir."
He laughed. "Yeah, small talk was never my speciality."
She paused for a moment, thinking. "Well, I live on my own - unless you count my cat, Georgie. What about you?"
"Same here. Just me and my big ol' Max." he smiled warmly. "Are you a local?"
"Yeah, born and raised here." she nodded. "My parents moved here in late 80s, just before I came along."
"Ah, the 80s." Naismith chuckled fondly. "I barely remember them. Good music, at least."
"What about you, sir? You grew up North, I'm guessing?"
"Yeah, up near Leeds. Moved here in '98."
"Any reason you moved?" she asked.
"Eh. I was fed up with things up there, and wanted a change of scenery." he shrugged. "So, you grew up here, then?"
"Yeah, that's right." she replied. "I was born in '91. Then, in '96, my mum got pregnant again."
Naismith nodded, before realising. "You mean..."
"Yeah." she shuddered. "She wasn't one of the ones who died, but she was pretty badly affected. Aneurysm. She's still alive, in a care home. Sometimes, she does little things that tell me she's still in there, somewhere. My new little brother... he didn't make it."
"I'm sorry." Naismith shook his head sadly. "Did they ever find out a cause?"
"No, still nothing. There was a big stink in 2012, some major inquiry or something." she trailed off.
Naismith awkwardly cleared his throat. "So, um... what about the rest of your family?"
"My dad's a deadbeat. Haven't seen him since I was little - he bailed after Mum became ill."
Naismith nodded sympathetically. "My old man was a drinker - a really mean drunk, to boot. My brother would always suck up to him. He and I don't talk anymore - around about the time I joined the police, he joined a gang of racist thugs. Heh, I suppose it's not like the police up there *aren't* a gang of racist thugs. Anyway, it just so happened that I was called out to respond to his gang harassing folk. That was my first assignment after I'd completed my AFO training, and... he had a shotgun on him. I ordered him to put it down, and he didn't comply."
"Oh, god." Nina shuddered.
"He's alive. Double-tap to the gut. And he had a beer-belly even bigger than mine." Naismith grinned. "After that, I decided to move down here. Took the detective course, and here we are."
Nina smiled. "So, what made you want to be a police officer?"
"I don't know." he shrugged candidly. "The cliche answer is that I wanted to be a better man than my dad and my brother - but the truth is, it was a job - or at least, that's how it started. I wanted to make a difference, sure, but I learned early on that nothing really changes."
He chuckled. "Sorry, I'm playing up the cynical detective act again. How about you?"
She laughed, then paused for thought. "I'm not sure, to be honest. I think I wanted to make a difference too. But yeah, I'm learning that it's not that simple. I thought this placement on Serious Crime would let me learn a lot, but so far, no one wants me on their case. Instead I get sent all the paperwork, while the boys in the squad are busy elsewhere."
Naismith sighed. "That's probably why you're stuck with me. Assign the newbie to the has-been."
"I'm not sure about that..." she chuckled. "Besides, this vigilante case - it's been a wild ride."
"Only downside is, I don't think anything you'll learn from it will apply to any normal cases." Naismith snarked.

At that, both were startled by a cracking sound in the distance.
"Gunshots." Naismith snapped into action. He started the car and drove toward the source. Nina put on her bulletproof vest.
"Control, this is Oscar Charlie 5-2." she said to her radio. "Gunshots heard on Marsh Road. Investigating now."
"Received, 5-2. Backup ETA is 19 minutes. Proceed with extreme caution, disengage if needed."
They arrived at the warehouse where the showdown was taking place, and jumped out of the car, pistols drawn.
"ARMED POLICE!" they both shouted, only to put their guns down in horror.
The scene that unfolded before them was beyond comprehension. They saw Connor, costumed up and battling a man... no. Not a man. A giant of a man, easily 8 foot tall, and built like an ogre. Every swing of his fists sent shockwaves through the air.
Connor moved like water, agile and precise. He ducked under a punch, tearing a hole through the wall behind him. His breath was steady, but there was a bestial cunning in his eyes as he calculated his next move.
"Come on, at least *try* and hit me." Connor quipped, slipping beneath another swing.
The Giant snarled, swinging again, this time with even greater fury. Connor barely had to move to avoid it.
"Temper, temper. Ever considered anger management?"
"RAAAAGH!"
This hit made contact, and Connor sprawled backwards.
Nina stared in horror, as Naismith opened fire on the Giant. The bullets hit his chest, but to no effect.
The Giant barely flinched. Naismith cursed under his breath and switched tactics, trying to find a weak spot.
The Giant turned, its eyes flashing. Before it could charge, though, a crossbow bolt buried into its clavicle.
"Hey, big guy! Word to the unwise - focus on the guy who can *actually* hurt you." Connor called out. He ducked another punch, grinning.
"C-c-control, v-vigilante suspect currently engaged in melee with--with..." Nina stammered into the radio.
"Repeat, 5-2, what is the situation?"
"A-a Giant! There's a fuckin' Giant in here!" Nina shrieked back.
Out of nowhere, a second figure leapt from the shadows - Sparky, clad in his own costume. He landed on the Giant's shoulder, teeth sinking into his flesh with a sickening squelch. The Giant howled in pain, momentarily distracted.
"Repeat, 5-2. Did you say 'Giant'?" Control asked incredulously.
Nina ignored it, and shot the Giant. The bullet glanced off, and buried into a wall. The Giant turned, enraged, and launched a crate at her.
Before it could hit, Connor dived in, taking the impact. He stumbled to his feet, dazed.
"Oi! Eyes on me, you oversized bastard!" Connor shook his head, trying to clear the dizziness.
With that, a loud boom filled the warehouse. From above, a figure emerged from the shadows - another soldier, this one dressed in full kevlar and crouching in the rafters like a spider in its web.
Connor spat a little blood onto the floor, and straightened up.
"You know, I've met some pretty stupid people in my time." he said, wiping his mouth. "But you're really setting a new standard."
The Giant bellowed, and lumbered towards him.
"There it is. Come on, then!"
Above them, Ivan steadied his rifle, and without hesitation, fired.
The bullet flew through the air with deadly precision, and with a thwack, it slammed into the Giant's chest. It punched through the thick muscle and bone as if they were made of paper, sending the Giant stumbling back with a roar of agony.
"Khoroshaya devochka, Isabella." he whispered, blowing his rifle a kiss.
