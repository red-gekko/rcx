VOLUME - Wolves Among Sheep
EPISODE - Cold Comfort
============================================================

Chloe pulled her hoodie tighter around herself. Specifically, it was the red one Connor had gifted her weeks ago, after saving her from a Skinner ambush.
It had gone dark since she'd taken it, but still - it reminded her of him. Helped her feel safer. Alas, it did little to settle the knot forming in her stomach. Something felt off.
Sensing his packmate's unease, Sparky brushed up by her leg, and pushed into her.
"Hah, thanks buddy." she smiled.
As they walked, an older woman sneered at them both.
"That thing should be on lead and in a muzzle."
Sparky whimpered slightly, hurt by the words and reminded of his restraints in the lab. Chloe, noticing his discomfort, stroked his head warmly, and they walked on. They arrived at the cafe, greeted by Bea who ran up to them and hugged her.
"Hey, girl! How was your run?"
"Haha, heya - yeah, it was good, wasn't it Sparky?" she said, to which Sparky barked affirmatively. With that, they sat down at the table, where Charlie was waiting.
"Hello, Chloe. It's been a while." he said, holding his hand out for a handshake. Chloe shook his hand curtly.
"He is not safe. Do not trust him, Chloe. If you only ever hear me say one thing, let it be this." Sparky warned, growling.
"Oh - is he not friendly?" Charlie asked, looking afraid.
"Oh, he's friendly - just very vocal, aren't you, boy?" Bea explained.
"Bea, do not trust him. Get away from him. He smells-no, he *reeks* of bad vibes." Sparky continued, heard only as whines to the human audience. "The moment he saw Chloe, I could hear his blood flow faster - not the way Connor's does, but the way the Super-Agents do when they see us. He was enraged. He stinks of fear and anger. Do not - Bea, I repeat - do NOT trust him."
He begged whatever gods may exist to grant his friends the ability to hear him - just this once.
"He might need toilet - come on, Sparky, let's go outside." Chloe said, noticing his unease - and feeling it herself.
"Yes, that might be for the best." Charlie nodded.
Sparky stared Charlie down - his eyes bored right into the back of Charlie's, as if trying to telepathically get the message across.
"I will kill you. You lay a finger on any of my friends, and I will kill you." he thought.
"Sparky, come on." Chloe said, firmer this time. Sparky huffed one last time, and followed.

"Well, that could have gone better." Charlie began. Bea looked at him, blushing shyly.
"Sorry... he is friendly, I don't know what's gotten into him today. He's probably just tired." she explained.
"Still..." Charlie replied, trying to come off as meekly as possible. "It's a shame he's not been trained, really. Did you say that was Chloe's dog, or her friend's?"
"Well, it's Connor's dog - he's our friend." Bea replied. At the mention of another male friend, Charlie's pupils constricted, but he relaxed his demeanour before Bea could notice. "I mean, he's very well behaved - Connor has trained him well, especially for a wolfdog."
"He's a wolfdog?!" Charlie carried on, almost pleased. "That's awful! He belongs in the wild, not in a city. It's a real shame that he's kept here, truly. I'm sorry your friends don't see that."
Bea gulped. Charlie's appeal to her sense of justice was working - by warping it. Were anyone else to say this, she'd launch into a fiery rant on how Connor adopted Sparky from a terrible situation, and the bond the two of them shared was unquestionable. But coming from Charlie, who had always known her weak spots, the thought of challenging him was anathema.
"Well, I suppose you're right..." she began shyly. "It is a shame he's not among his own kind. But, well, I can't fault Connor for that, Sparky had a hard life before Connor adopted him. Maybe that's what was wrong - maybe it's your cologne, or your clothes, or just something that reminds him of that." she said, searching desperately for an explanation that didn't include the words "Sparky doesn't like you."
Charlie slumped into his chair, looking saddened. "Oh... I'm sorry. I didn't realise it was my fault..."
"Oh no, no it's not!" Bea said, verbally backpedalling. Charlie smirked out of her sight - she took the bait. "It's really not your fault... you're right, Connor should have trained him better." she nodded, not quite believing it - but for the sake of Charlie, she believed that she believed it.

Chloe and Sparky were outside. Chloe was squatted to face Sparky eye-to-eye. She sighed and stroked him.
"Hey..." she began. "I didn't like him either."
"He's bad news! Please, you have to believe me." Sparky pleaded.
"I know, buddy." she whispered, not hearing him, but deep down, understanding he was trying to warn her. "You saved me, Sparky. Do you remember? When I was walking home from work and those bad men attacked me. You and Connor saved me. I will always trust your instincts."
She stroked his cheek.
"I won't let anyone hurt our pack." Sparky reaffirmed. "I will protect us."
Chloe smiled softly. "Look, we'd better go back in. Just... play it cool, okay? Let me ask him some questions, get to know him better - don't worry, I won't fall for his lies. I'll learn how badly Bea has fallen for him, and then we can start thinking of solutions." she explained. Sparky nodded in agreement. Chloe was bemused by just how deeply he understood. Satisfied, they returned.

"Sorry about that!" She said chipperly. "He just needed a poop is all."
Sparky put on a smile, not wanting to give away his hatred of Charlie any further.
"Aww, poor buddy." Bea said, smiling and stroking him. Sparky laid down at her feet, and nodded at Chloe, as if to encourage her to begin.
"So... Charlie." Chloe began. "How have you been since school?"
"I've been good, thank you Chloe." Charlie answered. His charms just came off as sleaze to her. She suppressed her disgust, and smiled.
"And you're studying here at Drackhill?" she asked.
"Yeah - I could have gotten into Oxford or Cambridge, but I decided I'd rather get away from the pomp of those schools."
Chloe stifled a smirk at such a blatant lie.
"That's so humble of you." Bea smiled. "Chloe and I were much the same - we were even poached by the Oxbridges, but we both decided we'd rather a more local, less toffish school." she went on.
Charlie smiled, pleased that she fell for it. Chloe bit her tongue.

"Speaking of, how's everyone else from that club of yours?" she asked. "The one you guys had back in school."
Charlie's nose twitched slightly - she hit a nerve there.
"I am trying to look humble to her." he thought. "And here you are, ruining that."
Bea hesitated, her gaze flickering between Charlie's smooth smile and Chloe's eyes. She knew something was off, but she couldn't quite pinpoint what it was.
"I can't say I kept in touch." he said curtly. "It was good fun, but the elitism of that club was truly terrible."
Satisfied that she'd found his weak point, Chloe decided to press on. Charlie may have been a skilled gaslighter, but Chloe was a better deducer.
"Oh, I agree." she nodded, hiding her satisfaction. "I remember when Bea and I tried to join. If I remember rightly, I was rejected because of my hearing." she said, this time unable to stop the corner of her mouth raising slightly.
"Oh, and they rejected me for my vitiligo." Bea added. Chloe felt a slight change in the atmosphere - only slight.
"Yes, well, I would never have turned you both down. It's a shame, really - I was unaware their discrimination went that far." he said, choking back his annoyance. Bea smiled, believing him.
"That's good to hear." said Chloe, swallowing her scepticism.
Charlie's jaw tightened. He'd lost the last exchange, and he knew it. Time to change the subject - and he had just the thing.
"Actually, speaking of people being treated badly for no good reason," he said, as if the thought had only just occurred to him, "That monster 'Werewolf' shot me last night."
"...did he?" Sparky tilted his head. "Huh. Nice one, Connor. Shame it wasn't a real gun. Stupid vow..."
"He... shot you?" Chloe blinked. "He doesn't use guns."
"Well... he did shoot that Chapman guy in the hands. And there's that sniper he works with, too." Bea shrugged. "I--I'm grateful for how he saved us, but... I can't believe he'd just attack Charlie like that."
"Yes, I was telling her about it earlier. It was terrifying." Charlie nodded.
"You... look well for someone who got shot." Chloe snarked. "Normally, if the Werewolf wants someone harmed, they're *harmed.*"
"I can demonstrate, if you like." Sparky snarked to himself. "Ugh... if you could hear me, Chloe, you would've found that funny."
Charlie gestured vaguely at his chest. "It wasn't a normal bullet - more like a pellet. Slam rounds, someone called them."
"Mhm." Chloe sipped her coffee. "Last time I checked, the Werewolf doesn't attack people for no reason."
"Chlo..." Bea pushed softly.
"I was attacked in the street." Charlie said. His voice soft and wounded - the type that made you feel like you were the one who'd done something wrong. "I don't know why. Maybe he mistook me for someone else. But I thought..."
He stopped himself, and looked at her with a kind of disappointed sympathy that made her skin crawl. "I thought you'd know more than most how harmful victim blaming can be."
Chloe felt her jaw tighten. She took a breath. Then another.
"That's not what I said." she began, keeping her voice level. "I said the Werewolf doesn't shoot people for no reason. There's a difference between assuming someone deserved it and asking why it happened."
Charlie's smile didn't falter, but something behind his eyes shifted - reassessing her. "Of course. I'm sorry - I didn't mean to imply anything. I'm just a bit shaken, is all."
"I'm sure you are." Chloe said absently.
Sparky jumped up, and licked her cheek softly.
"Hah... hey buddy." she cooed.

"Anyway..." Charlie cut in. "I was saying to Bea - it's a shame Sparky is living in the city."
Chloe tilted her head. "How do you mean?"
"Yeah, what do you mean?" Sparky asked, trying not to growl.
"Well, he's a wolfdog, isn't he?" Charlie explained. "He belongs with his own kind, surely."
Sparky nuzzled into Bea's leg, and put a paw on Chloe's foot. "I'm with my own kind, thank you very much." he grumbled.
Chloe hid her anger. He'd found her nerve.
"He's okay." she countered. "He has a good life with Connor. Honestly, Sparky's such a loveable goofball, I doubt he has the heart for killing his own prey."
Sparky chuckled to himself.
"Well, he's still a wild animal." Charlie said, trying to press harder on the nerve. "At the least, wouldn't it be prudent to keep him leashed and muzzled?"
At the word 'muzzle,' Sparky flinched reflexively, before scowling under the table. Chloe tensed.
"Well, his recall and obedience are second to none. When I walk with Sparky in town, there is speeding traffic and rodents and god-knows-how many distractions for him, but he sticks to my heel and trots at my pace. If I stop in the street without warning, Sparky will come to a halt as well. I'm not going to leash him if he doesn't need it, and I certainly won't put a muzz--anything on his face."
"Um... we don't use the 'M' word." Bea said gently. "Sparky gets upset by it."
Charlie smiled deviously. "I see. Well, I'm glad he obeys you so well." he said, satisfied he'd rattled her cage.
Chloe looked down at the wolf - her green eyes peering into his yellows.
"See why I hate him?" he whispered. Part of her understood.
She looked up at Bea, who was looking at Charlie and smiling, absolutely smitten, and oblivious to the battle of wits being played. Not willing to let him have the last laugh, Chloe decided to hit him again.

"So, Charlie... you're studying business, is that right?" she asked.
"Yes, that's correct. Business and accounting." he replied, correcting her slightly.
"Oh right - fascinating." she said, feigning interest. "What sort of thing do you study there?"
"Well, all sorts really - there's a great deal of mathematics involved - nothing too tricky." he bragged, trying to make it sound easy to him.
"And what else do you study there?" she goaded. "Any sort of psychological studies?"
"Yes - we study marketing, advertising, etc - how to get people to want what we have."
Chloe smirked, like she was playing chess, and she'd just Checked him. "So, how to manipulate people, basically." she replied nonchalantly, before sipping her coffee.
"Chlo..." Bea said, uncomfortable with that remark. Charlie was unable to contain his upset.
"No, not at all. Marketing is in no way about manipulating people. If it were, I wouldn't study it, because I am not manipulative. There's actually a lot more to it than that. It's not..." he ranted before catching himself.
"Checkmate." she thought to herself, grinning.
"It's alright, I'm only kidding." she said aloud.
Charlie slumped slightly, recognising his loss.
"Anyway, I'd best be off." Chloe stood. "Connor and Amy should be all moved in, now - I'll drop Sparky off."
Bea smiled, relieved that the confrontation had ended. "We'll all have to go round there and do a housewarming party."
Chloe nodded, smiling. "That would be good - I'll arrange something with Connor. Something where we can all meet up." she said, intentionally stressing the group nature of any future activity. She knew what gaslighters did, and she wasn't going to let it happen here.
With that, she bade them both farewell, and her and Sparky left.

Once they were both clear of the cafe, Charlie turned to Bea.
"So... I don't mean to speak out of turn, but that was really rude." he said.
Bea turned back to face him, shocked. "Really? Why?"
"Well, it seemed like she was trying to push my buttons, you know?" he answered, trying to turn his loss into a win - and now that Chloe wasn't there to challenge his narrative, he prepared to reframe it into an excuse to isolate Bea further.
"She kept talking about the club, even though I think I clearly said I didn't keep in touch with them. She decided to try and blame me for getting assaulted last night. She got very, very defensive just for me voicing my opinion on the dog. And then, she tried to make me out as some kind of manipulator. I just... it's a shame she doesn't like me." he said, pretending to be upset.
"Oh, no, I'm so sorry... you're right, that is really rude." Bea nodded, conflicted.
"I don't want to disparage her - I know she's your best friend." he feigned a sigh. "It just hurts me that your best friend can't accept me. And it saddens me that your friend wouldn't accept your boyfriend... I just feel such a close connection to you, is all. When I'm with you, my heart skips a beat. You're all I think about..." he put on all his best charms. Bea melted.
"I... I'm so glad you feel the same way." she admitted. Charlie grinned, glad his tactics had paid off.
"I hoped you felt the same... and, well, I want the best for my girlfriend. That includes wanting her to have friends who will accept the person she cares about." he replied.
Bea nodded, her pupils dilating with infatuation. "You know... I've kind of had a crush on you since we were in school."
"You have?" Charlie replied, feigning surprise. "I... I can't believe it. I always had a crush on you too!" he lied through his teeth. Bea melted again.
"I..." she began.
"Bea..." he whispered. "I think... I think I'm in love with you."
"Charlie..." she said, softly. "I... I love you too."
They kissed, and Charlie grinned like a devil.

Over by the Riverside, Connor and Trey carried boxes into the unit.
"That's the last one." Connor smiled.
"Woop woop! Now we have to unpack them..." Trey groaned.
"It's cool, I'll unpack." Connor began, but Trey cut him off.
"No, you won't." he chuckled. "You'll leave them in their boxes, saying 'oh I'll unpack them next week' until the world ends."
Connor laughed. "Okay, fair."
The pair began unpacking.
While working on assembling a sofa-bed in the basement for Connor to sleep on, Trey piped up.
"Bloody Amy. Leaving the hardest jobs for us, eh?"
"Well, we can't have her breaking those acrylic nails, can we?" Connor snarked back.
The two finished putting it together, and sat down on it.
Connor smiled warmly. He rose, and began unpacking a box onto a desk. It was full of half-finished antique clocks.
"What's all that?" Trey asked, confused.
"I, um..." Connor hesitated. "I've been playing around with these clocks I got from Molnar's. Thankfully, they weren't damaged in the fire."
"The antique shop in Caldermouth?"
Connor nodded.
"Nice." Trey smiled.

With that, they heard a knock on the door.
Connor answered, and Sparky jumped up, giving him licks all over his face.
"Hi Connor! Missed you!" he barked happily.
"Hah, hey buddy!" Connor smiled. Sparky rushed over and pounced on Trey too.
Chloe walked in. "Hey! How's it going?" she asked, hugging Connor. Connor smiled and hugged her back.
"Great! We're almost done unpacking." he replied. "Wanna see the place?"
"Sure!" Chloe smiled. They entered.
"Wow!" she said upon seeing the basement. "This room's about as big as your flat!"
Connor chuckled. "It's making me agoraphobic."
She grinned. "Need a hand unpacking?"
"Nah, nearly done. Just this last box, actually..." Connor said, picking up a small cardboard box. He opened it, and unpacked it - he found old electronics, a novelty tie, and a small jewellery box.
"I am *fascinated* by the way you organise things, bro." Trey laughed. Connor chuckled.
"It makes sense in my head!" he faux-protested. He opened the box, and revealed a bejewelled necklace, with ash-grey gems. It glittered and sparkled in the light. Next to it sat a small silver coin.
"It's beautiful." Chloe commented, smiling warmly.
"Suits you." Trey teased.
Connor laughed. "The grey brings out my eyes, eh?"
"Stunning, darling." Trey quipped, wafting his hand and putting on the voice of a camp fashion designer.
Connor and Chloe laughed. Connor looked at the necklace again.
"So... where did you get it from?" she asked.
Connor didn't answer for a moment, before shaking himself out of his stupor. "Sorry?" he asked.
"Oh, I--I was just wondering where you got it from?" Chloe repeated, shyly.
"Oh. It, um... it was my gran's." Connor replied absently.
"How about the coin?" Trey pointed into the box.
"Oh - 200 Algerian dinar." Connor shrugged. "Souvenir."
"You've been to Algeria? When?" Trey tilted his head.
"Oh, uh-never." Connor shook his, quickly finding an excuse. "I, uh, found it on the ground. Polished it up and whacked it in the box."
Chloe smiled, holding it. "Oh, wow - it's a 50th anniversary special. That's a great find."
Connor beamed, and pulled out a photograph from the box - of an old woman holding a baby.
"Is that your grandma?" she asked, cautiously.
"Yeah. That's her with me." he replied, smiling sadly. "Thank god it wasn't damaged."
He looked back at the necklace.
His vision shifted abruptly to the sight of a car caught in a blazing inferno. Screams and death rattles were audible. A very young Connor crawled towards, before faltering.
"Connor!" Trey's voice echoed. Connor took a sharp intake of breath.
"You okay? You spaced out for a second."
"Yeah, I'm... I'm fine. Sorry, memories." he explained. He put the necklace back in the box.
"You wanna talk about it?" Chloe asked, smiling sadly.
"Hm? Nah, I'm... I'm fine." he shook his head. "Anyway... are yous two hungry?"
"Am I about to have a world-famous Barrett Bacon Sarnie?" Trey grinned.
"They are good, aren't they?" Chloe giggled.
Connor smirked, and headed to the staff kitchen. Chloe and Trey sat down in the break room.

Sparky trotted up to Connor's side.
"Hey bud! How was your run?" he asked, trying not to sound too loud.
"It was good!" Sparky replied.
"Good lad." Connor grinned. His look turned conspiratorial, and his voice went to a whisper. "We're out tonight, by the way. Ivan just sent me the location. Super-Agents in Wickermast - should be easy killings."
Sparky nodded. "By the way... I need to tell you something."
Connor gestured at Sparky, as if to say "I can't reply much in front of them, but tell me."
"We met with Bea and that Charlie." Sparky explained. "I don't like him. There is something seriously off about him."
"I know." Connor whispered, almost inaudibly - only Sparky's super-hearing picked it up.
"He kept trying to make Chloe angry. And Bea seemed different, too. As though he was controlling her. Is he an Esper?"
"No, just a prick." Connor nodded. "We'll protect Bea, don't worry."

Meanwhile, Trey and Chloe struck up a conversation.
"You had one of those sandwiches before?" Trey asked.
"Yeah, it was about a month ago. After I got attacked outside my work..." she began, shuddering slightly, before brightening. "I stayed round Connor's and Amy's that night. The morning after, he made me those sandwiches."
Trey smiled comfortingly. "You stayed round Connor's?" he asked.
"Oh, uh--yeah, well, I just... after what happened, I felt safer staying with him and Sparky, you know?" she blushed.
"Nice." Trey smiled. He looked over at the kitchen shiftily, before turning to Chloe. "So, um... you like Connor, yeah?"
Chloe blushed even harder. "Oh, I mean, yeah, I like him, I mean, we're friends, I..." she stuttered shyly.
Trey chuckled. "It's alright. But, FYI - the feeling's mutual." he said coyly.
"Oh, I..." Chloe stammered, smiling.

Connor entered with the sandwiches.
"Bone apple teeth!" he chuckled. The group laughed.
"Oh, before we begin..." Trey began.
"What now..." Connor faux-groaned. Trey laughed.
"I brought you a moving-in gift. I've left it in the garden next door."
The group headed outside, where the sun had now set. They entered the motel's garden through the back gate, and Trey opened the box, to reveal a firepit.
"Hah, thanks bro!" Connor smiled.
"Wanna give it a go?" he asked.
"Sure, unless it's too cold?" Connor shrugged, turning to Chloe.
"That's what the fire's for, no?" Chloe grinned.
Connor smiled, and the boys set up the pit quickly. Trey chucked firewood in, before lighting it.
They took up chairs around the fire. Sparky snuggled up at Connor's feet.
Chloe pulled out her fingerstick from her bag. She pricked her finger, and then unzipped her hoodie to inject her insulin. She carefully observed the boys reactions - and relaxed when she realised they couldn't care less.
They began to eat their food.
"Thank god you guys were able to stay here." Chloe smiled. "If you need anything..."
"It's cool." Connor nodded gratefully. "At least we don't have to pay rent."
"And the basement's a nice upgrade." she chuckled.
"Hah! Yeah, you've got that right - keep this up, I'll have those three private islands in no time." he smirked. "Anyway - how's work been?"
"Weird." she grimaced. "They had me doing an autopsy the other night. It was... some sort of giant."
"I heard rumours about that." Trey nodded. "The Werewolf fought an ogre, or something. Well, different to the other time, at least. This one wasn't blue, or something."
"More or less." she shuddered. "It was... an experience, I'll say. They were so... different to most humans."
Connor awkwardly scratched his hands. "Um... changing the subject, how was your run?"
"Yeah, it was good!" she smiled, thinking back to her chat with Sparky.
"Where'd you go?" Trey asked.
"Wyrmwood." Chloe answered, covering her mouth as she chewed her sandwich, before chuckling. "Is there a reason everywhere in Drackhill sounds like an Elven kingdom?"
The boys grinned. "You know why it's called Drackhill?" Trey asked.
Chloe shook her head.
Connor leant over the fire, preparing to tell the tale.

"Once upon a time, this land was a rolling steppe, shadowed by a great, black mountain; known a the Draigpens. Inside, there was a silver city, home to a clan of subterranean monsters, called Bockas. Most Bockas are of the Gwidden type - a friendly, if mischievous type of Fae. The ones here, though - they were Bocka Dhu. They were a greedy folk - they'd sneak out from the mountain at night and steal from the villagers nearby. In Fae culture, this was practically heresy - the Fae had binding taboos on interfering with mortals who had not broken their rules. In fact, the Bockas used to have a contract with the mortal villagers; the humans would give them offerings, and they would protect them from disease. The Bocka Dhu, however, just took what they wanted with nothing in return. But one day, their king got too greedy, and robbed a Dragon's nest - Dragons were like Gods to the Fae. The Dragon was displeased with this insult, and so he said to the Bocka King;

"Thou hast taken what was mine,
Wretched Bocka with fire-eyes,
Midnight shall mark thy doom.
Stand before me, or,
Let thy clan be swept away,
Like leaves in the wyrd-wind."

The Bocka King was disturbed by this proclamation. He loved his clan, but knew he would die should he face the Dragon. So, he devised a cunning scheme...
The King approached the chief of the human villagers, and gave them an offer - all the gold and silver in the mountain, and in exchange, the humans would slay the dragon.
No human was brave enough to accept the offer. Until finally, a young man stood, and agreed.
That night, the Dragon descended upon the peak of the mountain. The young man stood defiantly. The Dragon was not amused.

"Thing of flesh, I know thee not.
My quarrel is not thine.
Leave these lands,
Or feel fire's bite.
Thy fate is nought to me,
Scurrow back to thy folk!"

The man stood firm, and drew his blade. The Dragon could not help but be impressed. The two fought, and the Dragon took to the sky, holding the young man in his hand.
The young man was brave. He took his blade, and plunged it into the Dragon's throat. A mighty roar shook the foundations of the land.
Seeing this, the Bocka King rejoiced.

"Look, my people, and see man's folly!
Tricked by promises of wealth and fame.
Such simple fools, who bask in the light!
Thralls to the Earthborne, as is proper.
His death brings riches beyond measure.
Let our cry ring out - Long Live the Bockas!"

Hearing this, the man was enraged at the Bockas' deception. As the Dragon fell to the ground, he turned his blade, steering the Dragon towards the mountain.
It hit the peak, landing right on the Bocka King. The mountain was shattered, leaving the valley underneath to run into the sea. The old mountain was named the Wyrm's Rock, and the new valley was called the Draca Hills.

The hero's body had been torn down to specks by the explosion. But seeped in magma and dragon blood, and the screaming souls of a thousand Bocka Dhu, he rose again. He pulled his blade from the peak, like Arthur did with Excalibur. It, like its master, had been reforged; now glowing with magical energy and coated in obsidian. The tribes united under his shadow, and as draconic wings unfurled from his back, he soared upwards, blocking out the moon."

Finishing the tale, Connor looked up at his friends. Chloe was looking at him, almost dreamily.
"Or at least, that's the legend, anyway." he cleared his throat.
"You missed your calling as a storyteller." she grinned. He chuckled.
"I love that story, man. Except, I can never remember the hero's name." Trey stroked his chin.
"Traditionally, you call him Pendrinn. When the Saxons came over, they changed it to Wulfric Wyrmsbane." Connor explained. "Most tellings of the story have him become a guardian spirit of the valley. Supposedly, it rains when he flaps his wings."
"Well, the bastard needs to stop flapping his wings. It's been pissing it down lately." Trey grinned.
"I love old folklore like that." Chloe smiled. "You have to wonder, who comes up with that?"
"Well, it's not so far fetched." Trey began. "This valley has an unusually high amount of silver and gold ore."
"And fossils." Connor added. "In the Wyrmrock caves, they've found loads of weird fossils. Mostly, they look like animals - but some also look like people. Except... weird. And also, the fossils were found in the same parts - meaning they're the same age."
Chloe leant back, surprised. "That's insane. How is that not bigger news?"
"Well, I saw a documentary on that." Trey replied. "They reckon there was some disease that caused these deformities in the valley."
"I heard about that. Didn't that same doc also prove that Wyrmrock *was* just a foothill to a much bigger mountain here that blew up?" Connor asked.
"It's a weird one. They proved that there was something similar to an asteroid impact 50,000 years ago that created the valley - but, the valley doesn't look the way it should do from something like that. They're not sure if it was a really weirdly shaped asteroid, or if the mountain's structure caused the unusual valley.. One of life's great mysteries." Trey grinned.
"The age of the story is also weird." Connor added. "Like, the fact that it references an ancient mountain being destroyed by an aerial impact, *and* we're pretty sure it predates Celtic settlement in the region - so it's possible that the story started out from the oral traditions of a tribe who actually witnessed the impact. Then the Celts come over and add Bockas to the mix, and then the Saxons come over and change up the names... it's pretty cool, when you think about it. There's even a theory that Pendrinn coming back from the dead to watch over us was a product of Christianisation. And, to boot, House Calder claimed lineage from him - though that's obviously dubious."
"House Calder?" Chloe tilted her head. "You've mentioned them before, right?"
"Former Lords of the valley." Trey explained. "Descendants of the Caledwyri tribe - Pendrinn's people. "So... okay, explain it again, but this time, like I'm a baby." Tommy grinned.
"It's... put simply, it's a magnetic lockpick." she explained. "As in, if you press it against a magnetic lock, it delivers a current strong enough to unlock it for a moment. The current fries it though, so you'd need to take it back here for me to repair it and make it usable again."

Tommy grinned. "That's perfect. Disciples won't know what hit 'em."
She smiled. "Um... would the Werewolf need any?"
"He can lift cars above his head - I think he'll be fine."
At that, the door opened.

"Speak of the devil." Tommy chuckled. "Alright, mate?"
"Hey." Connor nodded, clad in his costume and holding a laptop and a backpack. "Oh, hi, Shannon!"
"H-hi!" she waved awkwardly.

"It's encrypted - but there are some files I need access to."

"Right here." Shannon smiled, pulling the device from her pocket. "I can take a look, if you like?"

"That's okay!" she cut in. "I've been helping out, around here."
Connor paused. "You've been hacking for us?"
"Yeah! Tommy said we needed a tech specialist, so... I'm your girl!"
Connor's gaze turned back to Tommy, squinting slightly. "Um... Shannon, give us a minute, if that's okay?"
"Uh--yeah, sure, no problem." she stammered, rising and leaving.

"What's the problem?" Tommy asked.
"The 'problem' is that I didn't send her to you to get involved in... our world." Connor sighed.

"She's well aware of the risks, Connor. It's her choice."

"While I'm here... can we talk about Riverside?"
"Of course. How are you and Amy finding the place? And the dog, of course."

"Location-wise, it's perfect - being right by the river means we're slap bang in the middle of the city. I can patrol easier from it, plus the basement is good for stashing my stuff... and I've got plenty of money from the Hattab and Hughes jobs, but it's tied up to avoid suspicion. I can't withdraw the money directly, but I can spend it on assets."

"It makes the most sense." Connor shrugged. "And my work there is the perfect cover. Speaking of - I'm gonna buy the motel next door as well. Turn it into housing for me and Amy - the Hoods can launder money through it, as well."
"Look at you, little businessman." Tommy grinned. "Alright - deal."
Connor smiled, shook his hand, and headed back outside.

Shannon was sat at a desk, browsing through the now-unencrypted files.
"Oh, hi." she said nervously. "Is everything okay?"
He smiled softly. "Yeah, it's fine. Just... this is really dangerous, you know? Working with us--with *me...* it puts a target on your back."

And if I can do just a little something to make your job easier, to take just a little bit of weight off your shoulders - then that means I'm helping you save other people just like me."
Connor sighed, but his eyes betrayed his pride. "When we last met, you said you were studying computing - not that you were a hacker."

Connor smiled. "You're sure you wanna do this?"
"I'm sure. You can help people by beating up bad guys and saving the day. I can help by doing *this.*"
He thought about it for a moment, and then nodded.
"Well then... welcome to the team, Shannon."
"Glad to be here." she smiled warmly. "By the way, do I get a cool codename too?"
"Maybe later." he grinned.
She paused, awkwardly. "Um... you don't have to answer."
"Oh, Christ, here we go." he grinned.
"Are you Connor?"
His hearts skipped their beats. "Um... what?"
"N-nothing." she shook her head. "It's just, you guys have the exact same voice, and... he darted off yesterday to go and help with a drive by that you showed up to stop..."
He sighed, and unmorphed his mask. "Lips. Sealed. Okay?"

"So, how are we--ah." Tommy came in. "So much for secret identities."
"She figured it out." Connor shrugged.
"Sorry - it's a bad habit. When I see a thread, I can't help but pull on it..." she shrugged. "Don't worry - it's only because you use your real voice with me. That badass scary Werewolf voice? No-ones guessing your identity from that."
"...I thought my real voice was kind of badass." Connor sighed meekly.

It was well past midnight, now. A stone's throw away, Trey sat wide awake at his bedroom desk. One hand was working on his laptop, the other nervously tugging on his dreads.
"I must be crazy." he thought.
But it all added up.
He looked down at the pseudo-document he'd been writing.
He triple-checked news reports, and the dates they were written.
The truth was undeniable.
