SCENE - Ceilidh with Chloe/Warehouse Massacre, Part 1

	The knock at the garage door was hesitant - three quick taps, then silence. Connor looked up from the engine block he'd been tinkering with, wiping his hands on a rag.
	"Come in!" he called out.
	The door creaked open, and Chloe poked her head through. She was wearing her black gym outfit underneath his red hoodie. Her hair was tied back in a messy bun - to his pride, it looked like she wasn't trying so hard to hide her hearing aids. A gym bag hung from her shoulder.
	"Hey!" she smiled. "Hope I'm not too early?"
	"Nah, perfect timing." Connor tossed the rag aside, and gestured for her to come in. "I was just finishing up."
	She stepped inside, looking around the workshop. The lights were dim, most of the space taken up by stacks of tyres and machine parts. Sparky was curled up on an old armchair in the corner, snoring softly.
	"Hey, buddy..." she whispered, not wanting to wake him.
	Connor chuckled. "He's out cold. We were up late last night."
	"A big walk?"
	"Something like that."
	He grabbed a water bottle from the workbench, and took a swig. "You ready to train?"
	"Born ready." she grinned, dropping her bag by the wall.

	The depot sat at the end of a dead-end road, surrounded by chain-link fence and rusted shipping containers. A single floodlight buzzed overhead, casting everything in a sickly orange glow. Beyond the fence, the city hummed - distant traffic, the occasional siren, the ever-present patter of rain on corrugated metal.
	Inside, a dozen Disciples were gathered around a card table, laughing and drinking. Their bikes were parked outside, chrome glinting in the dark. Crates of stolen goods lined the walls - electronics, cigarettes, and a few kilos of cocaine stacked in the corner like an afterthought.
	"Another round, lads!" one of them cheered, slamming his glass down.
	"You're gonna bankrupt us, Terry." another grinned, dealing the cards.
	"Fuck off. I'm on a winning streak."
	The rest of the gang laughed. Another sparked up a joint. The night was young, and they were untouchable.
	One turned up the radio.
	"I love this song, man." he grinned, before singing along.
	"Every breath you take,
	And every move you make,
	Every bond you break,
	Every step you take,
	I'll be watching you."

	The gym room at the back of the garage had changed since she last saw it. The punching bag was clearly new, replacing the old cracked leather of its predecessor. Soft mats covered the concrete floor, and a few old weights were stacked in the corner.
	"Alright." he began, rolling his shoulders. "Let's start with a warm-up. Stretches first."
	Chloe nodded, pulled off her hoodie, and began to limber up. She followed his lead, stretching her arms, her legs, her back.
	She smiled, and finished her stretch. He walked over to the punching bag, and gave it a quick tap. "Let's see that form again. It can take a while to get the hang of--"
	She stepped up to the bag, and took her stance. Feet shoulder-width apart, weight balanced, hands up by her face.
	"Huh." Connor chuckled. "Well remembered."
	She smiled, and threw a jab, then a cross. The bag swung slightly.
	"Again. Harder."
	She repeated the motion, putting more force behind it. The chain creaked.
	He circled her, studying her movement. "Now, mix it up. Don't just go for the same two punches."
	She nodded, and began to flow. A jab, a feint, a hook to the side of the bag. Then, a low kick, followed by an elbow strike.
	Connor watched, arms folded. A small smile crept over his face.
	"That's really good. Your footwork's come a long way."
	Chloe paused, catching her breath. "Yeah?"
	"Yeah. I mean, you already had a knack for kicks, but that was impressive."
	He stepped closer, and gently adjusted her guard. "Just remember - keep your hands up. You drop them when you get tired."
	She blushed at his touch, but quickly regained her composure, and nodded, absorbing the advice. "Got it."

	Outside, a shadow dropped from the shipping container onto the gravel below. Silent. Measured. The figure moved along the fence line, keeping low, boots crunching softly on the wet ground.
	Then, the lights went out.
	"Oi, what the fuck?!" Terry barked, looking up.
	"Power cut?"
	"Fuckin' council bastards. Someone check the box."
	One of the men - a skinny, younger one with a nervous twitch - grabbed a flashlight and headed for the door. He stepped outside, shining the beam into the dark.
	Nothing.
	He took another step.
	*CRASH!*
	A stack of pallets went flying, scattering across the yard. The kid spun around, his heart racing. The beam caught nothing but shadows.
	"Who's there?!"
	Silence.
	He swallowed, and crept forward. The gravel crunched under his boots. The rain picked up, drumming on the containers.
	Then - a humanoid shape.
	"Oh, SHIT!" he screamed, stumbling backwards.
	He bolted back inside, slamming the door behind him.

	"What?! What is it?!" Terry demanded, rising from his chair.
	"The Werewolf! He's out there!"
	The room erupted. Men grabbed weapons - knives, crowbars, a couple of handguns. The braver ones moved toward the windows, peering out into the dark.
	"I don't see anything."
	"He's fuckin' there, I'm telling you!"
	"You're high, you idiot."
	"I'm NOT!"
	*BANG!*
	A shipping container door slammed shut somewhere in the yard. The sound echoed through the depot like a gunshot.
	"COME ON THEN, YOU LUPINE CUNT!" one roared.
	"...'lupine cunt?' That's new." another chuckled.
	"Shut up!"
	"Lads! Shh!"
	*CRASH!*
	Another noise - glass shattering, this time. One of the floodlights exploded, plunging the yard into deeper darkness.
	"It's 'im! It's gotta be!"
	"We ain't scared of you!"
	"...I'm scared of him."
	"Kyle! Shut up!"
	Terry grabbed a shotgun from behind the bar. "Right. We go out together, we find this prick, and we fuckin' end him."
	"You sure that's a good idea?"
	"You got a better one?"

	"Alright. Let's try some sparring. Go easy on me, yeah?" he grinned.
	She chuckled. "Prepare to eat dirt! In a--in a second. I'd better take these out." she began, removing her hearing aids and glasses.
	"I can still hear, but things are muffled without them." she said, slightly louder than usual.

	"Nice. But you're still telegraphing."
	"I'm what?"
	"Your left shoulder drops before you strike. It's a tell."

	"Practice. Or just hit harder and faster so it doesn't matter."
	She laughed, shaking her head. "You're a terrible teacher."
	"I'm a great teacher. You're just a slow learner."
	She lunged at him, and he dodged, grinning.
	"Come on... you are planning on hitting me, right?"
	Another lunge, and another dodge.
	"Okay, when I said 'go easy...'"
	Then, a third lunge---no. A feint. She spun quickly, catching the back of his knee. He dropped down, and looked up with pride.
	"There it is." he smiled warmly. "Turns out you just need prodding."
	She laughed, offering a hand. He took it, and rose.
	They circled each other, both grinning now.

SCENE - Ceilidh with Chloe/Warehouse Massacre, Part 2

	Outside, the rain was falling harder now. The yard was a maze of shadows and rusted metal. The Disciples fanned out, flashlights cutting through the dark.
	"Stay close." Terry muttered.
	"I don't like this."
	"Shut. Up."
	They moved past the shipping containers, past the stacks of pallets, past the dumpster. Nothing. No sign of anyone.
	"Maybe he fucked off?"
	"Maybe."
	*CLICK-CLACK!*
	The sound was unmistakable - a shotgun being readied.
	Every man froze.
	*BANG!*
	The first shell tore through Terry's chest. He flew backwards, crashing into a container, his weapon slipping from his fingers. Blood sprayed across the wet gravel.
	"SCATTER!"
	*CLICK-CLACK! BANG!*
	A second shot took another man in the throat. He collapsed, gurgling, hands clawing at the wound.
	The rest ran. They didn't know where they were going - just away. The figure in black kevlar moved through the yard with terrifying precision, cutting off escape routes, herding them like sheep.
	*CLICK-CLACK! BANG!*
	A third dropped. A fourth. The depot echoed with gunfire and screaming.
	Three men made it back inside, slamming the door behind them. They were pale, shaking, drenched in rain and blood.

	"What the FUCK?!"
	"That's... that's not him." one of them whispered. "He doesn't use guns."
	"Yeah he does! I heard a story that he started using slam rounds."
	"DO THOSE LOOK LIKE FUCKING SLAM ROUNDS TO YOU?!"
	"Then who the fuck--"
	"I don't know! I don't know!"
	They scrambled for cover, overturning tables, grabbing whatever weapons they could find. The lights were still out. The radio was still playing.

	"Every single day,
	And every word you say,
	Every game you play,
	Every night you stay,
	I'll be watching you."

	*CRASH!*
	The skylight shattered. A figure dropped through, landing in the centre of the room. Before anyone could react, the bullpup was raised, and two shots rang out.
	The first man dropped. The second followed a heartbeat later.
	The third - the skinny, young one - pressed himself against the wall, trembling, tears streaming down his face. His flashlight was still in his hand, and he aimed it at the figure.

	She was tall. Lean. Dressed in black kevlar that hugged her frame like a second skin. Her face was obscured by a mask, but her eyes - her eyes were wrong. Black veins crawled from the sockets, pulsing faintly.
	"Please..." he whispered. "Please don't kill me."
	She tilted her head. Reloaded.
	*CLICK-CLACK!*
	"PLEASE!"
	*BANG!*

	It had been ten minutes now, and Chloe's arms were starting to ache. But she wasn't giving up. Not yet.
	Connor threw a combination - jab, cross, gut punch. She blocked the first two, but the third caught her, sending her stumbling back.
	"You okay?" he asked, concerned.
	"Yeah, fine." she smiled. "That was faster than I was expecting."
	"You're not hurt, are you?"
	"It takes more than that, Mr. Barrett." she grinned.
	He relaxed slightly, and stepped back, giving her space. "Remember - breathe."
	She nodded, and they resumed. Chloe was more careful now, conserving her energy. She threw a jab, then a cross, then ducked under Connor's counter.
	"Nice dodge!"
	"I'm learning."
	She threw a kick - a roundhouse aimed at his ribs. He caught it on his forearm, absorbing the impact.
	"Okay, that was actually really good."
	"'Actually?'" she raised an eyebrow, grinning.
	"It was really good." he corrected. "Textbook. You've been practicing."
	"Maybe a little." she blushed, pushing a strand of hair from her face.
	He smiled. "I'm impressed. Seriously."
	She ducked her head, suddenly shy. "Thanks."
	They stood there for a moment, the silence comfortable. The punching bag creaked softly as it swayed.
	Connor noticed her tugging at the collar of her top, her face flushed.
	"You're overheating." he noted carefully. "I've got a spare top, if you want something looser?"
	She hesitated. "I... yeah. Maybe."
	He headed downstairs, and quickly morphed one up - a soft, white tee, as comfy as he could manage. He folded it, and headed back upstairs.
	There, she was nervously gripping her long sleeves.
	"You okay?" he asked gently.
	"Y-yeah, I'm..." she stammered. "Um... if I get changed, can you promise me you won't ask any questions?"
	He tilted his head, but nodded.
	She forced a smile, took the shirt, and disappeared into the small bathroom at the back of the gym.
	Connor stood there for a moment, uncertain.
	Chloe pulled off her top, and looked into the mirror, before closing her eyes and pressing her forehead against it.
	She opened them again, and looked down at her arms.
	Those. Not the only things she wanted to hide from the world, from him - but one of the biggest ones.
	She swallowed a lump, and pulled the shirt on. It was comfy and cool, hanging loosely around her frame. It reached down to her elbows - but her forearms were still terrifyingly bare.
	She steeled herself, and opened the door.
	Connor turned his head, and caught her. She held her arms crossed over her chest, hands gripping her elbows, as though she was trying to make herself as small as possible.
	His eyes flickered - just for a second - to her forearms.
	A network of scars. Some clustered, some lone. Some thick, some as thin as the razor that made them.
	He looked away immediately.
	You promised. You promised.
	"So..." he began, clearing his throat. "The, uh... that shirt looks good on you."
	She didn't answer.
	The silence stretched. Too long. Much too long.
	"Um... not that your old one didn't, I mean. Anyway, shall we continue?"
	"I'm sorry." she whispered.
	"What? No, Chloe, you don't--"
	"I--I should go." she said, turning toward the door.
	"Wait! Wait." he said quickly.
	She stopped. Her back was to him, and her shoulders were trembling.
	"I've... been there." he began.
	She turned, just slightly.
	"I don't scar easily, and--it was long enough ago that they've faded." he explained.
	Not a lie. He hated that so much of what he said nowadays needed to be qualified like that - never true, just... not false.
	"I... I'm so sorry, I didn't--"
	"It's alright." he smiled softly. "I'm not trying to say I know exactly what you've been through, but... I'm not gonna judge you. Just like you won't judge me."
	She hesitated, before walking slowly towards him.
	"I--Thank you." she whispered. "I'm sorry, I--I didn't mean to make a big deal out of it. It's just..."
	He cocked an eyebrow. "You're tired of having to explain your entire mental health history to anyone who sees them?"
	"...Yeah."
	"I promised I wouldn't ask questions." he nodded. "If you *do* wanna talk about it, that's completely fine."
	"Do you wanna talk about yours?"
	He shrugged. "I don't need to. And I'm not saying you do - I'm saying *if* you do, we can."
	She relaxed a little, and took a shaky breath.
	"How's my form?" she finally asked. "Am I getting better?"
	"You're doing amazing." he smiled proudly. "You've really got a knack for the kicks. We need to work on your defence, but that'll come with practice."
	"At this rate, you'll have to teach me ceilidh, won't you?" she quipped.
	He cocked an eyebrow.
	"You know... because we joked that you'd... teach me if I got better at sparring, so..." she awkwardly shrugged, fumbling with her hands.
	Connor grinned, and pulled out his phone. A few swipes of his fingers, and a lively fiddle tune filled the room.
	Chloe giggled shyly, and took his hand. He crept onto his tiptoes, and she copied.
	"The basic step is like this - one, two, three, and turn. Got it?"
	She tried to mimic him, stumbled, and laughed. "Nope, not got it."
	"Here." he said, placing his hand on her waist to guide her. "Step, step, step, turn. Better?"
	"I think so?"
	They began to move together, slowly at first, then faster as she found the rhythm. The fiddle picked up tempo, and Connor spun her - she gasped, nearly fell, and he caught her, one arm around her waist.
	They froze.
	Her face was inches from his. Her breath was warm against his cheek. Their eyes locked, and they held for a moment.

	Then, as if the gods themselves were playing tricks, the door swung open.
	Amy stood in the doorway, a bag of takeaway in one hand, her keys in the other.
	"Yo, meathead. Quit fucking around in here, I've got dinn--"
	Her mouth opened, then closed, then opened again.
	"Oh." she said. "Oh, I'm--I'll just..."
	She backed out, pulling the door shut behind her.
	"Fuck..." she muttered, walking away quickly. "Nice going, Amy."

	Inside, Connor and Chloe had sprung apart.
	"I should--I should probably go." Chloe said, her face bright red.
	"You don't have to."
	"No, I--it's late, and I've got work in the morning, and... I had a lot of fun." she smiled.
	"Me too." he beamed. "Drive you?"
	"There's a bus." she nodded. "I'll see you tomorrow night?"
	"Yeah. Trey's jonesing for a pub crawl." Connor chuckled.
	She stepped toward him, and they stood for a moment. Their throats were both flexing, as if they were *trying* to say something, but it got stuck somehow.
	Instead, she went for a hug. He held her for a moment, before they pulled apart.
	"Keep the shirt, by the way."
	"You sure? I've already stolen one of your hoodies." she grinned, pulling the red cotton back over here.
	"I'm sure." he nodded. "And... I won't bring... 'it' up, but if you ever wanna talk about it..."
	She brushed his hand gently, and smiled.
	"Night, Connor."
	"Night."
	She slipped out the door, and turned out into the garage. "Night, Amy! See you tomorrow!"
	"Oh, uh---night!" she called back.
	Chloe bowed her head, and left.
	At that, Amy stepped in.
	"I am... so, so, *SO* fuckin' sorry."
	Connor grinned, sitting down. "Don't be. It's fine."
	"It's not. You two were having a moment, and I--"
	"It wasn't a moment."
	"You're hopeless." she snarked, sitting down with him. "If a girl looked at me the way she looked at you, my blood pressure would go so high I'd explode."
	"Yeah, well, imagine having two hearts, and they *both* skip a beat. Or having both of your windpipes seize up in awkwardness."
	She laughed, then leaned back against the wall.
	"You really like her, don't you?"
	"I--" he went to deny. "Yeah."
	He closed one eye, looking at her. "You're not going to give me shit about it?"
	"I mean, I *would,* but... you got beaten up by a cyborg ogre the other night, so I'm going easy on you."
	"Fuck yourself." he laughed heartily.
	"You guys out tonight?" she asked.
	"Nah... I feel guilty, but we're taking the night off for a change."
	"That's good." she smiled. "You look like you need it."
	"Aaaand we're back to insults." he grinned.
	She laughed. "No, but honestly, don't feel guilty. So, you take the night off for once. What's the worst that can happen?"
	Connor's eyes widened, as multiple concurrent apocalypses flashed before him.
	"Oi." she cut in, snapping her fingers in front of his face. "I was kidding. Honestly, there's not gonna be some psycho massacre just because you have a kip."

	The young Disciple howled in pain as he clutched his now-obliterated kneecaps. He was alive; despite his preferences in the moment.
	"Who... who ARE you?!" he begged.
	She paused, and looked at him, her bullpup aimed at his face.
	Then she reached up, and pulled off her hood-mask.
	Brown hair tumbled down, curling around her lightly-tanned face. The black veins spread across her cheeks like cracked porcelain. Her eyes - warmer than he'd expected - met his.
	She smiled. Her lips were coated in a black powder.
	"Call me... She-Wolf. And... if you see my hunk of a man... give this to him. No-one else, though... or I'll kill you."
	She scribbled a note on a piece of paper, and then kissed it, planting a tarry imprint as if in signature.
	She turned, and walked out. The radio kept playing, over the Disciple's screams.

	"Oh, can't you see,
	You belong to me?
	How my poor heart aches,
	With every step you take?"

	"If you truly love me, you'll tell me where Amy is."
