EPISODE - Monsters

It was an ordinary afternoon in 2012 - at least, that's how it began.
Chloe and Bea were at school, walking away from their last lesson of the day.
Chloe's hair was unkempt, hiding her unscarred face.
A group of girls walked past, and laughed.
"Freak show!" one of them jeered.
"Piss off!" Bea snapped back.
"What did you just say, freak?" the girl asked, approaching menacingly.
"L-l-leave us alone!" Chloe stuttered.
"L-l-l-l." the girl mocked.
Bea stepped forwards, scowling. "Piss. Off."
"Whatever. Freaks!" the girl spat, storming off.
Bea sighed. "I hate this school."
"M-me too." Chloe nodded sadly.
"I've gotta go..." Bea smiled, noticing her mother's car. "I'll see you tomorrow, yeah?"
"O-o-okay." Chloe said, smiling back.
She walked on. A sixth-former drove up past her.
"Hey there! Need a lift?" he asked.
"Oh, um, I-I'm not sure I-I'm s-supposed to..." she said, worried.
"Oh, don't worry!" he replied. "I'll drop you right off at your place."
Chloe thought for a moment. "What will my mother think?"
But then, decided her mother's approval was worthless.
"O-okay then." she nodded, entering the car.
"What's your name?" he asked.
"C-Chloe." she replied.
"I'm Francis." he said, smiling.

Chloe's eyes snapped open, and a sharp breath came through her nostrils. She closed them again, and this time took a sigh, before rising.
Most mornings, she loathed her alarm. But even still, there were some where it saved her.
She stood, before shuffling over to her nightstand. She pulled out a small stick, pressed it against her fingertip, and then pulled the trigger. A small globule of blood formed at the skin, and she tested it.
Blood sugar of 3.6 nmol/L. Not great, not terrible.
She was running late, and so rather than making breakfast, quickly opened and downed a sugary drink. She took a few tablets, and went into the shower.
Not a nice, long, comforting one, no. This was a big morning, after all.

Chloe finished drying her hair, and put on the set of clothes she'd laid out. A white button-up shirt, complimented by a black blazer, fitted trousers, and slip-on shoes. She hesitated for a moment, looking down. She ran her fingers over the constellation of scars that covered her forearms - many faded, many not. She gripped the shirt and blazer sleeves, and pulled them down.
She sat down in front of her mirror, and put a bit of makeup on - eyeliner, and faint lipstick.
Then, she hesitated, before taking the concealer. She looked at herself again, touched her scar, and sighed sadly. She placed her glasses over her emerald eyes, and closed them for a moment.
She opened a small box, pulled out a pair of hearing aids, and placed them in. She made her hair into a bun, making sure her curtain bangs were pinned loose enough to cover her ears.
Hiding. She hated that she was so good at it.

The morning sunlight peeked in through the curtains, shining down on the sleeping pair of mutants. Their flesh trembled and twisted in their sleep - as though their alien biology healed better this way. They were both half-smiling - clearly sharing a good dream.
Both had mostly healed their burns. Connor woke up, and checked his watch.
"09:49. We got back in gone 4AM. So, 5 hours to fully recover from smoke inhalation and third-degree burns. Nice."
Sparky looked up and yawned. "Is that good?"
"Hah... yeah, better than most." Connor chuckled. He rolled onto his side, and groaned.
"I'm late for work." he realised, rising.
"We'd better go. I want some food!" Sparky beamed.
Connor laughed. "You can come, if you like. You'll just be sitting around while I work though - it'll be very boring."
Sparky yawned. "After last night, I'd like a bit of boring."
Connor chuckled, endeared. "Oh yeah, you'll meet Trey, too. He's my best friend - well, aside from you, I mean."
"So he's part of our pack?"
Connor paused. "Yeah. He's part of our pack."
"Then I wanna meet him now!" Sparky yipped, rising.
Connor smiled warmly, as the tar reformed over his skin and shaped itself into comfy, yet practical clothing. He raised his red hood as they stepped into the rain, and carried on.

Chloe took a deep breath through the nose, centring herself. She walked up to the glass door, and slid it open.
"Good morning! Can I help you?" the receptionist asked.
"Hi! Um, I'm here for a job interview? My--my name's Chloe. Chloe Derrington."
"Ah, yes - just wait here for a moment." the receptionist nodded.
Chloe nodded back, swallowing some saliva - wishing she could do the same to her nerves. In short order, someone came through the stairway.
"Ah, hi Chloe! Peter Jones." the man began, offering his hand.
"Good morning, Mr. Jones!" she smiled warmly, shaking it. "Or-or is it Peter?"
"There are 5 Peters in this office." he grinned. "Everyone 'round here calls me Jones."
"Ah, well, nice to meet you, Jones." she bowed her head.
"Shall we?" he beckoned. She nodded, and followed him to the interview room.

She cleared her throat. "Well, Hox genes tell a developing body *where* it should grow a new part; and they switch off once that's done. However, in some species like Axolotls, they remain active into 'maturity,' which is why they can regenerate limbs. We've been investigating how retinoic acid can alter the positional memory involved in regeneration. The hope is, we can manipulate activation selectively, to make sure that the limb develops correctly, and doesn't regrow anything else. In theory, we could use this for organ donation too - make it so you can grow a spare heart in your abdominal cavity, to provide for donation without harming the patient."
Jones grinned. "We've been working on something similar here - but the retinoic acid idea, that's... that's worth looking into, for sure."
She smiled, trying to release the tension.
"Well, I'll cut to the chase, save us both the time." Jones nodded.
Her heart sank - she did her best not to let it show.
"Does next Monday work for you? We'll get you in two days a week - paid, of course, and there's weekend overtime if you fancy it. Professor Lowenthal will send us lesson plans so we're sure you're learning the right stuff."

He grinned. "Welcome aboard."

Sparky alternated between napping in a sunbeam, and watching the humans with fascination.

'Jog' - that was one word for it.

Sparky's tail wagged furiously. "Hello! I'm Sparky! What's your name?"
"Bea, meet Sparky." Connor smiled. "Sparky, this is Bea. She's a friend of ours."

"Aww, hi Sparky!" she cooed. "Look at those eyes! They're so expressive."
