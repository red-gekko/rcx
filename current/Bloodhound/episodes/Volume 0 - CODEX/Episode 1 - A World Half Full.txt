EPISODE - A World Half Full
============================================================

It is autumn, 2015. The world stands on the brink - undone by events decades in the making.

In 1908, President Theodore Roosevelt made the monumental decision to seek a third term. He won that election in a landslide - his Second Square Deal reforms streamlined the American government, empowering the executive. Satisfied, he stood down at the 1912 election, and Woodrow Wilson took the reins of power.
That same year, the Tunguska Event terrified locals. None yet knew of the true horrors to come.

Then, in 1914, a Serbian man shot an Austrian archduke. Almost as soon as German boots crossed into Liege, the British government announced its declaration of war. American businessmen grinned unanimously, making a fortune selling guns and munitions to the Entente.
1917 was a tumultuous year. Russia had fallen, with fighting between Socialist forces and reactionary remnants. Yet at the same time, the Ottoman Empire fell to the Arab revolt. American soldiers landed in the trenches of Saint-Mihiel, and began to turn the tide against the German juggernaut.

By 1918, an exhausted continent signed the Treaty of Versailles.
A wise man once said "The only fair deal is one where no-one is happy."
A much wiser man once said "This is not a peace. It is an armistice for 20 years."

Imperialism carried on as if nothing had happened - as if it didn't just result in an entire generation being fed into the meat-grinders.
In Ireland, hopeful revolutionaries who had been fighting since Easter, 1916 - and 800 years before then - believed they were going to win. Their hopes were dashed by chance - a string of unlikely British victories, along with the assassination of IRA leadership, ended in a smouldering Eire with the Union Jack still flying over the ashes of Dublin.
The Imperial Boomerang was in full effect - the Empire's totalitarianism abroad fostered totalitarianism at home. Strikes were outlawed, the nascent Labour Party was disbanded, and the British State - and its disgustingly wealthy owners - reigned supreme.
Across the Empire, things were no different. Egypt faced a brutal crackdown and was forced under direct rule from London; Canada had prison camps built specifically for the internment of Quebecois nationalists; the colonies of Oceania were reforged into the Commonwealth of Australasia, whose first Act of Parliament was the 'White Australasia Policy' and the 'pacification' of indigenous rebels; the combined Dominion of South Africa absorbed Namibia and Rhodesia, with criminals across the Empire being deported to the 'frontier' - the goal being the elimination of the Black Native as a sovereign force.
Perhaps most dystopian of all, for the peoples of the Raj, nothing changed at all.
In fact, the only Imperialist quagmire avoided was in Arabia. Focus on consolidation of its existing Empire meant the British had no interest in Sykes-Picot - instead viewing a unified Hashemite kingdom as a valuable proxy state. The "Hashemite Kingdom of Arabia," stretching from Palestine to Baghdad and Damascus to Sana'a, had as much independence as you could want - aside from every major corporation in the kingdom being a British, American, or French subsidiary.

And yet, as the British Empire tightened its grip on its quicksilver colonies, and as the French struggled to reconcile Liberté, Egalité, and Fraternité with Autorité, Iniquité, and Vanité, the Russian Empire was consigned to the history books. The Russian Civil War ended in a victory for the Soviet Red Army - a coalition force of Bolsheviks, Mensheviks, Orthodox Marxists, Radical Socialists, Liberals and Anarchists who had had a Damascene conversion, and more - all eventually coagulating into the Communist Party of the Soviet Union.
Then, in a mansion in Gorki in 1924, Vladimir Lenin breathed his last. He was succeeded by a troika of Nikolai Bukharin, Leon Trotsky, and Grigory Zinoviev. Josef Stalin, and his Bonapartist clique, were ousted after his disastrous performance in the Polish-Soviet war - but his cronies began the work of infiltrating the state apparatus.
The war had brought Eurasia to its knees. But thankfully, careful industrialisation, organised collectivisation, the New Economic Policies, and decentralised leadership managed to prevent a food shortage in Ukraine and Kazakhstan from becoming a full-fledged famine.

The Great Depression shattered the American spirit, but from the ashes, Senator Huey Long built a national machine with his 'Share Our Wealth' program.
President Franklin D. Roosevelt responded with his own New Deal, but the political landscape was fractured. In 1934, the shadowy 'Business Plot' to overthrow the government was exposed and thwarted by the heroic testimony of General Smedley Butler, revealing the rot at the heart of American capital.
In 1935, a son of a judge shot Long in the chest - hitting his pocketbook. Long's bodyguard immediately slew the assassin, but the Kingfish's paranoia and megalomania only grew. Convinced of a vast right-wing conspiracy out to get him - not entirely without merit, it must be said - he knew exactly what to do. The 1936 elections were looming, and he approached FDR with a choice - "Give me power, or I'll run as a spoiler."

The Democratic Party wanted nothing to do with Long's populist policies, but Roosevelt knew he couldn't win with Long in the running - so he approached him, covertly. He expected an egomaniac who would demand the Vice Presidency for himself - but instead, he fell into the traps of a shrewd political operator.
Long demanded appointment as head of the WPA, the key agency of the New Deal - and demanded that said appointment be made a cabinet position. He also demanded General Butler be made Secretary of War.
Roosevelt was surprised, but agreed hastily to the terms, leading to the resignation of his Vice President, Garner. In response, Long made one additional demand - appoint Henry Wallace as VP.
The 1936 election was won by the new administration in a landslide. America experienced a shift in politics not seen since the Reconstruction era, with increasing civil rights, redistribution of wealth, and the ascent of radical new figures.
As the Share Our Wealth platform overtook the New Deal, America began a parallel process - economic justice, and a growing patronage network, with the Kingfish at the top.

But while the USA saw exposure of fascist plots, and the USSR saw a new age of democratic socialism, the European Continent went rabid.
In Italy, the home of the word 'Empire,' Benito Mussolini's Blackshirts marched on Rome, forging a fascist model others were eager to follow. Their brutal conquest of Ethiopia, their genocidal actions in Libya, their annexation of Albania, and their puppeteering of Greece, made them seem like the kings of the New Order in Europe.
That is, until another king entered the castle. In Germany, the Nazi Party rose to power on a tide of resentment, its growth fuelled by a brutal campaign of street violence and a new, escalating terror campaign by the radicalised KPD.
Hitler was well aware of the growing resentment to capitalism, and so he used an ancient trick to Empire - blame someone else. Every rich pig in German high society was quick to blame all the problems of their robber-baronism on a secret, Jewish cabal. Anti-Semitism was the greatest tool of German capital - an imaginary group of devious financiers, obscuring the very real group of devious financiers, all swearing fealty to the Fuhrer.

The Soviet Union, seeing full well the growing fascist movements, did not sit idly by like the isolationists in Washington. It provided arms and advisors to communist proxies in the Chinese Civil War and watched Poland closely, forging a pact with France in the face of British indifference to the inevitable war.
The eruption of civil war in Spain doused Europe in gasoline. The Republican forces saw support from France and the USSR, along with tepid aid from America; meanwhile the goons of General Franco saw aid from Germany and Italy, along with similarly tepid support from Britain. At the same time, the great Polish Peasant Strike of 1937 devolved into a fratricidal free-for-all, with populist militias fighting the Sanationist regime, and infighting between the rebel forces; some gleefully accepting Moscow's support, others chafing under Soviet direction. The battle lines were drawn.
In 1938, those lines were crossed, and the spark was lit; German forces swept into Czechoslovakia and Poland, citing border incidents with Soviet clients. That same year, the Nazi regime quietly initiated Aktion T4 - the murder of the disabled, and Phase 1 of the coming nightmares.

Britain, under Chamberlain and fearing a resurgent USSR more than Hitler, declared neutrality. Backbencher Winston Churchill failed to mobilise a pro-war movement - none of his Tory peers wanted to fight and die for Russian Communists, and his own imperialist demagoguery made him no friends among the proscribed socialists.

Isolated, Paris fell within months, a puppet regime installed at Vichy. Madrid followed, with direct intervention from Wehrmacht troops crushing the remaining Republican forces.
America, under an increasingly frail FDR and a secretly-dying Butler, extended Lend-Lease to the desperate Soviets, despite the protestation of the isolationist Long. In response, German U-boats began sinking Atlantic convoys, which only enraged the American public and further wounded the Isolationist movement.
If the Kingfish wanted to keep his crown, he needed to adapt.

In the Pacific, Japan's hunger for empire only grew. Its proxy war in China, coupled with total humiliation in Khalkhin Gol, led to the sidelining of the Army, with the immense Zaibatsu corporations backing the Imperial Navy's ambitions for Pacific dominance. The army was instead ordered to focus on China, while the 'real war' would be fought at sea. Plans were drawn up for operations to begin in February, 1940.
The resentment, along with extremist racism endemic to the army, led to horrific atrocities across China. From sickening experiments in Manchuria that would make Frankenstein pale, to unspeakable brutality in Nanjing - if an alien force was looking at Eurasia, they'd see two increasingly disgusting genocides, spreading like wildfire.
While the IJA committed crime after crime, the IJN struck across the Pacific - first, seizing French territories in Indochina; then, taking Indonesia from the occupied Dutch; and last, attacking British and American holdings - culminating in its occupation of the Philippines and a sponsored uprising of Hindu nationalists in the Raj - and at the same time, a shocking invasion of Hawaii.

A vengeful Congress, shepherded by Butler, declared war on the Axis - against a U-Boat campaign in the Atlantic, and a mass-expansion in the Pacific. Long knew he couldn't fight for isolation anymore - so he, ever the masterful spin doctor, got to work re-framing the war as a continuation of his campaign against Wall Street - a fight against fat cats, at home and abroad, who would dare to threaten the American people.
The crowds went wild. The Kingfish had done it again.
And then, in June of 1940, Smedley Butler went to the doctor with complaints of stomach pains. It was cancer. He resigned from the administration, and spent his last few weeks at home with his family, praying that his work would be done.
The stresses of war, a hostile underling, and fury from Wall Street, took their toll on Roosevelt. First a small stroke, then a major one, and like Wilson before him, the President was bedbound.
Long knew the administration would be paralysed as long as FDR's heart was still beating. And so, to ensure his now-puppet Wallace could ascend to the highest office, the unthinkable was ordered. Rumours say it was Long himself who smothered the old man - but there was never any proof of foul play.

On the Eastern Front, the full might of the Wehrmacht plunged deep into Soviet territory. The conflict became one of annihilation, and the Nazi regime's genocidal ambitions escalated into the industrialised horror of the Holocaust. 6 million Jewish people, 1.5 million Roma and Sinti, hundreds of thousands of dissidents - all burned in the charnel houses of Europe. In parallel, Generalplan Ost, and Organisation Todt, depopulated Eastern and Central Europe, as hunger policies caused famine to rip through the Bread Basket, while forced labour claimed millions of lives to feed the German war machine.
Fascist forces mobbed outside of Leningrad, Moscow, and 'Fortress Crimea', but the Red Army, ever defiant, made the invaders pay for every inch. The German advance was finally, and decisively, broken in the ruins of Volgograd.

The American war machine, now ruthlessly organised by Long's shadow network, awoke. Its first great victory was the bloody liberation of occupied Hawaii. For London, however, the Raj slowly slipping from its grasp sent a message across the Empire - "They can be beaten."
A wave of mass strikes and demonstrations in Canada was the first sign of the British Empire's death rattle. The Governor-General authorised a crackdown on any revolutionary impulse within the country, leading to masses of refugees arriving in the Northern USA.
The de facto Long administration, made up of isolationists-turned-hawks, smelled blood. The American people were largely sympathetic to their northern neighbours, and so, the unthinkable happened - the execution of War Plan Crimson. American invasion of Canada.

The invasion was swift - the US Army was welcomed into Ottawa with only token resistance. To the Canadians' horror, however, the Americans decided that independence was not their destiny - instead, the Provinces of Canada became incorporated as States. The anti-Empire movement was split - some supporting American statehood, with most demanding total independence - and a not-insignificant Loyalist minority only added further fuel to the fire.
Losing Canada and the Raj sent a clear message across the Empire - Australasia was next, declaring unilateral independence; while South Africa threw out the British, and welcomed the Germans - becoming a non-belligerent Axis pact member. The African colonies broke free in near-unison, while the Caribbean saw American occupation replace British dominance. Ireland tried once again to break free, but the British, now no longer needing to station forces away from the Isles, had another idea.
Driven mad by the unstoppable collapse of its Empire, and fuelled by rabid hatred towards the birthplace of anti-Imperial action, the punishment of Ireland made the failed Independence War seem tame in its bloodshed. Having taken out its frustrations on the Emerald Isle, Britain sued for peace with the American juggernaut.
Come the 1944 elections, President Long swore his oath of office to crowds of cheering supporters. Vice President Wallace stood beside him, secretly hating his puppeteering ways.

Back in Europe, the Nazi forces floundered against the Soviet walls. Pushed back to Berlin, with uprisings across Western Europe and a looming American invasion, the Soviets decided to end this war with flair. Twice in a century, they'd faced German aggression - this time, with the crime of crimes being committed against their citizens. They were determined that the German people would never again threaten them.
Thousands of theoretical physicists had escaped the Reich and its proxies. While a few went to America or Britain, both countries' deepening authoritarianism made a different destination appeal - Moscow.
Codenamed the "Chelyabinsk Project," a new type of weapon was designed. It was clear that, as long as Berlin stood, the Axis stood with it. The Soviet army had lost millions in the war, and taking Berlin would only bleed them dry - and they needed troops to secure the fracturing Reich, let alone their Eastern flank.
On the 18th of May, 1946, a single plane flew above Berlin. All anti-air defences in the city were destroyed by successive bombing campaigns - a reverse-Blitz of epic proportion. The plane flew as high as it could, and dropped a single bomb, targeted at the Reich chancellery building, and the Fuhrerbunker beneath.
In an instant, a light beyond light engulfed the square, blinding for miles all who dared to look at it. A massive fireball swirled out, and a mushroom cloud formed above the centre of the Reich. Shockwaves ripped up the ground beneath, with famous monuments being reduced to ash, rubble, and memories.
Not since the Thirty Years' War had Berlin seen such devastation.
Hundreds of thousands of civilians were vaporised alongside the Reich. Demoralised and terrified, the remaining holdouts surrendered immediately. The leadership of the Nazi Party, who had merely 4 years prior been the kings of Europe, were missing, presumed dust.
Across Eurasia, Japan failed to fight a two-front war. With Soviet troops now free to cross into Manchuria, their army folded, as the Red Flag flew above Beijing and Seoul. Terrified of Soviet occupation, the Imperial government surrendered to the Americans.

In Washington, the end of the war with a planet-cracking bullet point led to panic among the new elite of Long's network. Wallace's clique believed that the USSR were potential allies, and that they should secure the future of the world by formalising this alliance. Meanwhile, General Douglas MacArthur, who had grown in power and influence throughout the war, wanted to steal the secret superweapon that had melted Berlin, and use it again - on Moscow.
Long, tired, pained, and jaded, couldn't hope to reconcile the two bitter foes. Realising his pursuit of high office was all for nothing, and his dirty tricks had only enabled the same hawkish technocrats to take the reins, the President wrote a note, placed it in his desk, and went for a walk into the woods, alone. He was never found.

Wallace's ascent was marked by instability, with MacArthur's clique accusing him of murdering Long. Wallace managed to organise the post-war order of Europe, and stepped down at the 1948 elections, placing the keys to the Oval Office into the hands of President MacArthur, and Vice President Robert Taft. The New Deal, the Share Our Wealth project, the age of Every Man a King, was gone. In its place was the beginning of a new war.

A cold one.

Backed by the resentful oligarchy, America began its divine mission: to contain a socialist bloc that stretched from Madrid to Seoul.
Europe fractured: France, Iberia, and much of the East aligned with Moscow, while Britain built a rival coalition of anti-communist states in northern Europe - the European 'Democratic' Union.

In 1962, the Hashemite Kingdom of Arabia fell to Ba'athist revolutionaries. Religious and ethnic tensions still smouldered underneath, suppressed by the watchful eye of the Party.
China, guided by the orthodox Wang Ming after Mao's quiet ouster, threw its lot in with the Soviets. Nixon never went to China, and as it industrialised, the Soviet bloc seemed unstoppable. Even India and Arabia quietly bowed to the communist juggernauts.

Ireland remained under British rule. Another, slightly more successful revolt, led to devolution for Connaught, Leinster, and Munster - collectively known as the Autonomy. Ulster, however, was still ruled straight from London, with gerrymandering allowing the Protestant minority to hold a majority of power.
The Troubles never ended. The Catholic provinces had substantial Loyalist minorities, imported over by the Brits after its brutal depopulation strategy of 1944. No Irish state could be born without mass-terrorism from its Loyalist subjects - a fifth column, and a hallmark of British imperial strategy: Divide and Rule.

Surprisingly, the two superpowers managed to avoid a nuclear arms race. Wallace had hobbled the early efforts of the so-called 'Manhattan' project, while the Soviets had no interest in building superweapons instead of train lines. While both powers continued research into peaceful nuclear power, and secretly maintained a tiny arsenal of weaponry, neither openly pointed global annihilation at the other.

Elsewhere in the nascent anti-communist bloc, the capitalist nations pursued reactionary politics to divide their working class. Apartheid carried on alive and well in South Africa. Australasia became a brutal police state, propping up puppet states in Papua and Indonesia. In the States, the Civil Rights Act of 1947 was still on the books, but was swiftly undermined by crypto-racist policies that restored segregation in all but name.

Come 1994, Chairman Mikhail Gorbachev was ousted in a soft coup by Marshal Dmitry Kruglov. At long last, Stalin's progeny had taken the throne. The simmering rot in the Soviet state had reached its top, and Kruglov's cronies soon enriched themselves, becoming no different to the oligarchs of America they decried.
The rest of the Soviet Bloc embraced this change, and Bonapartist leaders took power across Eurasia. In Poland, General Jaruzelski declared martial law, outlawing strikes and crushing dissent. In the Balkans, leaders like Milosevic and Boban took power in regional republics, claiming ethnic loyalty over class solidarity.
Socialists across the globe were horrified at this shift, while separatist movements and religious extremists chafed under centralised rule.

In 2002, mass uprisings in Derry escalated into the brutal "Bogside War." Loyalist paramilitaries, openly backed by the British state, bombed schools and massacred civilians. Britain's authoritarian grip only tightened in the aftermath - an inevitability of colonialism coming home to roost.
Ulster Catholics decided their time was up. They emigrated - either southwards to the Autonomy, or further out to Iberia. Many ended up using their experience in separatist warfare to aid regional paramilitaries like the ETA.

And then, in 2009, Eurasia fell into anarchy. Continued instability, growing nationalist movements, and the assassination of Kruglov by rebels caused the mighty USSR to collapse. Its satellites followed suit - Poland came under a fundamentalist Catholic Junta, the Balkan Federation collapsed into a practical race war, and China fell into a familiar era of warlord cliques - Xinjiang, Tibet, Manchuria, Qinghai, and a weakened central remnant.

By 2015, Britain and the United States stand as hollowed-out powers - right-wing kleptocracies where democracy is theatre, wealth is power, and resistance is futile.

The world is on the verge of a new, horrifying order.
Earth is a land of decaying empires and failed states, rotten to the core.

The old world is dying. The new world struggles to be born.

Now is the time of monsters.
