⚠️ Content Warning

A guide to sensitive content in Bloodhound, and why it is there.

Please read this before continuing. Bloodhound is a work of dark fiction. It deals directly and frequently with themes that many readers will find distressing. This page exists so that you can make an informed choice about whether, when, and how to engage with the work. You are not obliged to read anything that you expect to harm you.

Depiction Is Not Endorsement

Bloodhound depicts bigotry, violence, and abuse in order to critique them. The narrative does not ask you to agree with its villains, and where characters express hateful views or commit atrocities, the text positions those acts as wrong. However, depiction is not always comfortable, and the work deliberately refuses to look away from the harm it describes. If a topic is listed here, it does not mean the author endorses it — it means the narrative engages with it, often critically, sometimes graphically, and always with consequences for the characters involved.

Why These Topics Are Depicted

The central thematic concern of Bloodhound is the relationship between power, violence, and the systems that enable them. Many of the work's most difficult scenes exist to show how violence is enacted by institutions against individuals, and how survivors respond to it. Where the narrative depicts abuse, bigotry, or torture, it does so to render those experiences legible, not to glorify them. Readers who have lived through similar experiences may find some scenes difficult or even retraumatising, and are encouraged to use the episode-by-episode guide below to navigate the work at their own pace.

Content Categories

⚠ Abuse & Mistreatment

Physical, emotional, and sexual abuse is depicted across multiple storylines, including child abuse, domestic abuse, and the abuse of people in institutions or captivity. Some of these depictions are visceral. Survivors of abuse may find certain scenes particularly difficult.

⚠ Bigotry & Hate Crime

Racism, homophobia, transphobia, ableism, antisemitism, Islamophobia, and other forms of bigotry are frequently depicted, both as individual acts of violence and as systemic forces. Characters use slurs in ways that reflect the realities of the settings they occupy. These depictions are intended to critique the ideologies involved, not to reproduce them uncritically.

⚠ Torture & Body Horror

The work includes extended depictions of torture, medical experimentation, forced mutation, and graphic physical injury. Some scenes involve extreme body horror, including dismemberment, surgical modification, and grotesque transformation. These are among the most graphic sequences in the work.

⚠ Suicide & Self-Harm

Suicidal ideation, suicide attempts, and self-harm are depicted and discussed. Some scenes depict characters in acute mental distress. Readers who are currently struggling with their own mental health are strongly encouraged to seek support rather than continue reading if they feel unsafe.

⚠ War, Genocide & Mass Violence

The backdrop of the work is a century of war, ethnic cleansing, and state violence. Depictions of military atrocities, massacres, and genocide appear throughout, including in flashback and in dialogue. These are presented as horror, not spectacle.

⚠ Addiction

Addiction, substance misuse, and their consequences are recurring themes. Some scenes depict overdose, withdrawal, and the impact of addiction on families and communities.

Episode-by-Episode Warnings

The following list is intended as a navigational tool. It is not exhaustive, and some episodes contain themes that emerge gradually. Where an episode contains no significant content beyond the baseline of the work, this is noted as None beyond baseline. Severity markers are approximate and reflect the prominence and intensity of the theme within that episode.

Mild — present but not prominent, or only mentioned in passing. Moderate — clearly present and sustained. Severe — graphic, sustained, or central to the episode. None — no significant content beyond baseline.
