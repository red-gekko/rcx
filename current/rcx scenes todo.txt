Process of Mutarch invasion:
	1: Mutarch arrives on planet. Works its way up the food chain by dissecting and consuming fauna, before kidnapping and vivisecting sapient species
	2: Mutarch enthralls a few sapients as Broodkin - typically loners or outcasts. It uses them to learn more about the planet, its governance, and its weaknesses.
	3: Mutarch begins experimenting on broodkin - either by slowly adding alien traits to thralls (going from Primal>Feral>Aberrant>Malignant), or adding native traits to broodlings (going other way), creating Hosts.
	4: Mutarch creates an infectious agent, typically a neural parasite like Brain Slugs, and distributes this in locations prime for infectious spread (water sources being common).
	5: Mutarch establishes a full Hive Cluster, staffed mainly by Broodkin and Hosts. This cluster is typically underground.
	6: Mutarch creates a "Cult", a pseudo-religious movement among Broodkin to be spread to native population. Cult is often syncretic with local religions, philosophies, and ideologies.
	7: Upon engagement of hostilities with local authorities, begins full infestation process. Initially starts by mutating local fauna to use as attack dogs, while utilising Broodkin and Hosts as soldiers and double-agents.
	8: Having successfully integrated local fauna DNA with Zennelid templates, begins production of true Zenelidd bioforms.
	9: Upon successful conquest of the planet, integrates existing population into the Cult, and begins producing Leviathans to spread to other worlds.

	How vamps work:
		All Vamps are required to consume blood at least semi-regularly. A side-effect of their metabolism slowly poisons their blood, requiring them to expel it in the form of black ichor, and replace it with fresh blood. To aid in this, they are compatible with donations from virtually any mammalian blood - they can also consume blood from birds and reptiles in a pinch, though the blood of sapient beings is most tempting to them.
		Abstaining from the consumption of blood will result in hunger pangs, madness, frenzy, and death. Rarely, a vampire who abstains from consuming blood and has expelled all ichor will undergo a metamorphosis, turning them back into a mortal (though they will require a blood transfusion.)
		In addition to requiring it to survive, blood is addictive to them, due to the way paradrenaline effects vampire brains - they feel a constant need to inflict pain on others, or to consume animal flesh and blood.
		In addition, they still require normal food and drink, though their requirements for these are reduced compared to baseliners.

		Weaknesses:
			Vampires have a number of potential drawbacks. Not all will have the same ones. A vampire who has reduced sense-of-self (from giving in to the inner beast, for example) will have more weaknesses than a vampire who has maintained personal integrity.
			
			The vast majority of vamps experience severe pain and discomfort from UV light. At low levels of this condition, they will experience irritation and discomfort. Higher levels result in physical burns, irrational fear or rage, delirium, entering a coma-state, or outright bursting into flames. Vampires are naturally nocturnal, so even Daywalkers (vampires who do not suffer under sunlight) will still prefer to sleep in the day - though vampires only require 4 hours of sleep, so a Daywalker can still function well throughout the day/night cycle.
			Some unfortunate vamps can't even stand artificial lights.
			Indeed, this weakness also applies to all ionizing radiation, but UV light specifically causes more damage than most radiation.

			Many vampires are also vulnerable to certain 'charms' - physical objects imbued with spiritual significance. A Vampire's weakness to these objects is determined both by the faith of the wielder (where applicable) and the vampire's own loss-of-self (see below).

				Garlic
