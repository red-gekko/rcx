VOLUME - Gods Among Men
EPISODE - Judgement Day, Part 2 - Broken Hearts and Bones
============================================================

It was early hours of the 26th of September, 1996. 03:27 AM.
A Thursday. There was a spatter of rain, but the night sky wasn't too cloudy. Were it not for the city's light pollution, you'd be able to see the night stars with impressive clarity.
The moon was a waxing gibbous - nearly full. 24 hours before a blood moon, as if we needed any more omens.
And one man remembers all of this. Every detail. He can't forget it.
No matter how much he wants to.

Vince's car swerved into the hospital car park, narrowly missing a bollard. Without bothering to straighten into the lines, he burst out of his door, and sprinted like a blur over to the passenger side. He swung the door open, nearly taking it off its hinges, and pulled his sister Laura from the seat.
Blood and yellowish fluid coated her legs, mouth, and nostrils, while thick, black tears of ooze streaked down from her bloodshot eyes. Her skin was red, and hot to the touch.
"Vince..." she whispered through ragged breaths.
"Shh..." he replied, carrying her to the hospital entrance. "You'll be alright..."
He made it to the door, kicked it open, and screamed for help.

Just over an hour had passed.
Specifically, it was now 04:42 AM. The clouds had begun to draw in, and the rain was becoming a little stronger. The moon peeked out through the clouds, as though it was too scared to watch.
Vince anxiously paced the hospital corridors, as Laura's screams echoed in his skull. A nurse approached him, her face pale.
"M-mister Barrett?" she asked.
"Is she okay?" he hurriedly answered, rushing over.
"We... Laura appears to be suffering from systemic organ failure, and extreme hyperthermia - a condition we've been calling hypermetabolic shock, which has triggered a cascading system failure."
"What?" he asked, white as a sheet. "Wh-what does that mean?"
"In simple terms, her metabolism has spiked to unprecedented levels. She can't take in enough oxygen to keep up with the burn, causing a dangerous build-up of lactic acid across her body. Worse, she's experiencing a massive rise in body temperature - her core temp is currently 41.3°C, and rising still. Her blood pressure has also grown, which is making clots form in the lungs, and near the brain, causing a cerebral infarction - a stroke."
"Oh, God..." Vince whispered in shock. "What... what about the baby?"
"From the tests we've run, the baby isn't suffering from any of these conditions." the nurse began unsurely. "But as long as he's inside her womb, he's being exposed to the severe temperatures, and her blood chemistry is becoming dangerously acidic."
"...'he?'" Vince gasped.
A boy. A baby boy.
She'd kept the gender secret, even from herself.
She should be the one to know this. Not him.
Not him.
"Mr. Barrett?" the nurse cut in, shaking him from the fog of pain.
"Is... is there anything you can do?" h finally whispered.
"At the moment, we have two options." she began. "We can provide her with medication to help bring down her body temperature and blood pressure, giving us time to treat the underlying hypermetabolism. However, this medication... it's never been used in a live birth before. At the doses we're using, it would most likely cross the placenta... and be toxic to the baby."
Vince ran his hands through his hair.
"Our other option... would be to perform a C-section right away, and try to save the baby. But the additional blood loss, and the time it would take... Laura would most likely die."
Vince agonised over the choice for less than a second. It felt like hours.
"Save her." he nodded, closing his eyes.
"Are you sure?" the nurse asked.
"I'm sure. Save her. Please..." he begged.
The nurse nodded, and left.
He leaned against a wall, and hit it with the back of his head.
She would damn him for his choice. Hate him for it.
So be it. She'd be alive to do those things, at least.

27 minutes passed. Vince rose swiftly as the nurse returned.
"I..." she began, struggling to find the words. "I'm so sorry, Mr. Barrett."
"No..." he whispered, burying his face in his hands.
"We did everything we could, but... Laura didn't make it."
The words echoed in his brain.
Years went by in a single breath.
"Can I... can I see her, please?" he finally asked.
"Of course." the nurse nodded, ushering him into her room.
He sat by her side, and squeezed her hand. She was pale and still.
Vince had always assumed that people would look peaceful in death. But she didn't. Her face was still slightly contorted from the pain. Her skin was pale, cold, and damp to the touch - a disturbing contrast to the searing red hours before.
Small pools of thick fluid remained dotted around her skin, as though she'd sweated them out. A pinkish foam remained near her lips.
Vince wept. He howled in pain and sorrow.
He rested his hand on her abdomen, and...
He felt movement.
Kicking. Punching. Flailing, perhaps.
Dead. Supposed to be dead.
Alive. Alive?
Alive.
"NURSE!" he screamed.

13 minutes had passed, and the nurse arrived - carrying a newborn baby.
Vince took him into his arms. He looked into the little one's eyes. Icy, steel grey. Pale skin.
He had his mother's brow. A tiny, reddish-blonde fuzz sat atop his scalp.
"I know this has been a lot to take in..." the nurse began.
"I thought he'd've died..." Vince whispered, still in shock.
"He's very strong." the nurse smiled proudly. "Did you... did you have any idea what name you'd like to use?"
Vince paused for a moment.
"She said... if it were a boy, she'd call him Connor."
"Connor." the nurse smiled. "It suits him."
She wrote down, placed down a milk bottle, and nodded. "I'll give you both some time. If you need us, ring the bell."
"Thank you..." Vince nodded back, his voice hoarse.
He held Connor, and gently stroked his head.
"Hey, my lad..." he whispered softly. Connor gurgled slightly, half-asleep. Vince passed the milk bottle up to his mouth.
Connor turned his head, as though trying to avoid it.
"Hey, buddy... come on, you need it." he whispered again.
Connor still rejected it, his face scrunching.
"Hey... I'm sorry..." he sobbed softly. "I know, it should be her..."
Tears streamed down Vince's face, dropping onto Connor's.
"Sorry, mate..." he whispered, wiping Connor's face. Connor reached up, his eyes clenched shut, and grabbed Vince's beard, before yanking.
"Ow!" Vince exclaimed in surprise, before chuckling. "You're a feisty one, eh?"
Connor, being a baby, didn't reply - but Vince could've sworn he was grinning.
He pulled him closer, leaning back so Connor could rest on his chest.
"Shh, shh..." he whispered as he rocked him slowly. He offered the milk bottle once more, and this time, Connor took it.
"Good lad..." he smiled warmly.

Lord Oxford sat at his desk, holding a golden cube with apparent fascination. It glimmered in the light in an unnatural way. Standing to attention was the Commandant, frozen in place - like he *wanted* to move, but couldn't. Oxford's irises were pulsating, ethereal violet.
The Judge entered, kneeling down in reverence.
"Master. The youngblood heathen fought well, but is dead."
Oxford grinned. "See that, George? In hours, I have resolved a problem you have spent scores of men and months of time failing to fix."
The Commandant's icy grey eyes shot a foul look back. The old man waved his hand, his eyes fading back to their yellowish-brown.
The Commandant breathed at last.
"Lord Oxford." he nodded disdainfully.
"I hope this punishment has taught you the values of efficiency and flexibility, George." Oxford grinned. "You can go, now."
The Commandant turned, his steel-grey eyes meeting the Judge's red orbs.
"Do not look at me, abomination." the Judge snarled. "You have been ordered by the Lord to leave."
The Commandant's lip curled in anger, and he stormed away.
"Such arrogance..." the Judge hissed.
"I suppose it *is* a pity." Oxford shrugged. "I thought he'd be stronger, too."

It was cold evening now. Chloe had grown bored after a day of working on the data, and so took Sparky out for a run.
She slowed down, and panted for breath, before checking her blood sugar, and then her wristwatch.
"38 minutes... new record..." she said between breaths.
Sparky trotted over, and licked her.
"Well done, Chloe! You're doing really well!" he cheered.
"Heh... thanks buddy. Right, let's go home. Hey, let's surprise Connor - we'll make him some nice food to come back to." she smiled.
"Yes, he'd love that!" Sparky barked happily.
"Hah... besides, he'll probably need it. I bet he's kicking that monster's arse..." she chuckled.

As she said this, Ivan spotted Connor's lifeless, impaled corpse.
"VANGUARD!" he roared, rushing over to him.
"FUCK!" Naismith shouted.
Ivan gripped the spear and, using all his superhuman strength, pulled it from the ground. The Judge thrusted it into the ground *hard.*
Connor's limp body flopped onto the ground. Naismith turned him over.
"Take his arms, I'll take his legs." Ivan commanded.
"Mr. Sokolov... he's... he's dead." Naismith replied sombrely.
"Nyet. Not dead." Ivan snapped defiantly. "His arms. NOW!"
Naismith stared deep into Ivan's eyes. "He is *fucking* dead. LOOK AT HIM!"
"I AM LOOKING!" Ivan barked, before looking back down at Connor's body.
Naismith looked down too, looking into the hole where Connor's hearts should've been.
"Holy... holy shit..." he stuttered.
"See, Mr. Naismith? Not dead." Ivan sighed.
Within Connor's chest cavity, there was movement - it was very, very slight, but it *was* there.
"He's trying to heal. We need to get him to a hospital." Naismith began.
"Nyet. No hospital. They'll cut him up." Ivan protested.
"And you have a better idea? Look, you're here on official state business, *da?*" Naismith snarked, trying and failing to pronounce it.
"Da." Ivan nodded, making a point of saying it properly.
"Then the Official Secrets Act applies. Come on, let's get him somewhere they at least have some equipment we can try and use."
With that, Nina - the token human - arrived.
"Holy shit..." she panted. "No..."
"He's not dead." Naismith cut in. "Come on, we have to get him to the hospital."
Ivan rose. "I'm going to get a friend - someone who knows this man's biology better than anyone on Earth. Get him safe. There's a surgeon who he has history with - Judith Rosenbaum. Have her handle the admin. If he doesn't make it out alive, we're all *fucked,* understood?"
"Come on, Nina. Mr. Sokolov, we'll meet you there." Naismith nodded.

Back at the safehouse, Chloe was making a meal in the staff kitchen - a cheesy pasta bake. She made a point of dropping some grated cheese on the floor every time she put some in the dish.
"I keep this up, there won't be much cheese for us!" she laughed.
Sparky lifted his head, having just devoured all the cheese she'd dropped.
"What's that, bud? You want more cheese?" she smiled.
"Yes, more cheese please." he begged, putting on his puppy-dog eyes.
"Aww... don't give me those eyes, you know they work too well on me... okay, here you go." she relented, giving him some cheese, which he greedily consumed.
Chloe squatted down and hugged him.
"You know, you're such a good boy. I'm really, really glad we can talk." she smiled warmly.
"I wanted to talk with you for ages." Sparky beamed. "You're part of my pack."
She chuckled, stroking his ears.

"DCI Naismith, TO-24. Get me Judith Rosenbaum." Naismith ordered, arriving at the reception. Behind him was Nina, pushing Connor's lifeless body on a gurney.
Judi arrived. "Henry, it's been a... oh my goodness, is that..."
"Yes it is. And he's still alive, Judi. He needs help."
"I... right, this way." she beckoned, as they brought him away.

Sparky greedily devoured his second--no, third--actually, fourth--bowl of pasta. Chloe chuckled as she watched from her workbench.
"Nice, eh buddy? Not bad for someone who never cooks." she smirked.
"It's nice." Shannon smiled, taking a break from her screen to eat at her bowl.
"Very nice!" Trey grinned, having eaten seconds. "Why don't you have some?"
"I've already had a bowl." she smiled. "Besides, this work isn't gonna finish itself."
"What are you looking at?" Shannon tilted her head.
"That Coagulator-thingy Connor and Amy use." she nodded. "The haemostatic foam cartridges are a nice touch - but I'm wondering if I could use Connor's own clotting factor to improve them a little - maybe even isolate his regenerative properties. It's tricky though - we don't wanna turn everyone we spray with it into a Super-Agent."
"Eh." Trey shrugged. "An army of Super-Agents who owe us a life debt... that sounds like aggressive recruitment."
"This place is crowded as it is. And I can't be arsed to come up with that many codenames." Shannon quipped.
Chloe chuckled. "How's things out there tonight?"
"Not too bad! Gwen and Amy have stopped a couple of muggings. I bet Connor's wiping monster brains off of his boots as we speak." Shannon nodded.
Chloe chuckled grimly. With that, the door opened.
Sparky bolted up.
"Is it safe?" she asked.
Sparky sniffed, then smiled and headed over to the door, opening it with his paws. He jumped up and licked the person coming in.
"Hello, Ms. Derrington." Ivan entered.
"Chloe! This is Ivan, he's a friend of ours." Sparky barked happily.
"Hah, that's right. You might know me as 'Verdict.'" Ivan nodded.
"Oh, nice to meet you!" Chloe smiled. "Connor mentioned on the radio you'd be stopping by. How's it going out there?"
Ivan cleared his throat awkwardly.
"Is everything okay?" Shannon asked, worried now.
"Connor... he was hurt badly in his last fight. He's alive, but he needs help."
"What happened?" Chloe asked, her face pale.
"He was impaled on a spear, and his hearts and lungs were destroyed. We thought he was dead, but... his flesh is trying to heal, even still."
Shannon gasped. Trey sat down in shock.
"Oh my god..." Chloe's ears started to ring, and her heart jumped into her throat.
"We need to hurry. You, Chloe, are the only person on this planet who knows so much about his biology, yet isn't part of the cabal. We *need* your help, now." Ivan said, shaking Chloe out of her stupor.
She put on her bravest face. "Let's go. Now. If he's trying to heal, there's hope. Without lungs, he'll have no oxygen... we need to get there right away."
Sparky whimpered. "I need to help him! We need to help him!"
"Sorry, tovarishch - they won't allow dogs in hospitals. And PS Chowdhury has seen you before - it could blow Connor's cover." Ivan shook his head.
"Fuck..." Trey finally whispered. "Please tell me he'll be okay."
"I'll... I have to go..." Chloe shuddered, her eyes wet.
"Tell him I love him, Chloe, please..." Sparky begged.
"You can tell him yourself. I promise." Chloe cried, stroking Sparky, before heading into Ivan's car.
"Keep me posted on comms." Shannon asked, her face pale.
"Will do. Don't tell the others - not yet. Not while they're in the field." Ivan nodded.
Sparky headed to the window, and watched them leave. He let out a mournful howl, as Trey rocked in his seat, and Shannon scratched the backs of her hands.

Chloe and Ivan sat in the car together, driving to the hospital. Chloe was frantically going through her research notes on her laptop while Ivan drove.
"So..." Ivan began, breaking the ice. "You known Connor long?"
"Since September." Chloe replied absently.
"And... you've been staying with him since the incident at Vitalis?"
"Yeah." she said, again focused on her laptop. A tear ran down her cheek, and pooled in her scar. She wiped her eyes, and continued working.
"Anything useful on that?" he asked, pointing at her screen.
"I don't know. I'm looking at the analysis we did on the cells from his severed hand. They were weird - like they were in hibernation. I'm comparing that to the data we found on the clones in the facility. It looks like, when Connor's cells are deprived of oxygen, they enter a dormant state - and also, they can use anaerobic respiration to keep working where needed, but it's slower, and the lactic acid build-up can eventually kill them. According to this, when one clone's lungs became unviable from the cancers, it still continued to be active for 5 days until it finally died." she explained.
"Hah... in Russian, please?" Ivan jested.
"[Connor's cells can go into a low-energy mode when they don't have enough oxygen. It's like putting them into sleep - they can still function, but not at full speed. They use a backup system to keep things running, but it's inefficient. The downside is that it leads to a build-up of toxins that can eventually kill the cells.]" she said nonchalantly.
Ivan looked at her, surprised. "[You speak Russian?]" he asked.
"Da."

In the hospital, Judi looked at Connor's body, confused. Naismith was with her.
"So... he's healing?" she asked.
Naismith nodded. "See the movement? He's at least alive."
"Okay... I don't know how to speed up his healing, but we can at least keep an eye on his vitals." she said, hooking up EEG stickers to Connor's head. She pulled up the tattered remains of his mask, and looked closer.
"He looks... familiar." Naismith pondered. "...nah, can't place him."
"He's pretty badly mangled." Judi shrugged. "I've never seen under his mask before now, either."
"I've spent months now, hoping to get a peek... and now I have, and I don't want it." Naismith nodded sombrely.
"I have to ask, Henry..." Judi began. "What is that on your eyes?"
"I, um..." he sighed. "I was dying. He saved me."
Judi swallowed, and took a deep breath. "Well, let's return the favour." she nodded.
Naismith nodded back, and left the room, returning to his squad.
"Sir." Nina whispered, still in shock.
"Nina... you can go home now. There's nothing we can do." Naismith said sadly.
"No way, sir." she said firmly. "I'm not learning anything in a phone call. I... I need to see this through."
He smiled softly. "Alright, PS Chowdhury. Let's lock this hospital down. Get CT on the phone, covering the weak spots. If this freak comes here... we can at least slow him down."
"Sir." she nodded, her brows creased with determination.

"[Okay, remember, you're not here as Connor's girlfriend. You're here as a scientist who works with the Werewolf. Naismith will be there, but don't worry, he's even more connected to us than you are. We just need to make sure no one knows it's Connor under that mask.]" Ivan explained.
"Got it." Chloe replied in English.
With that, they arrived. Naismith stood.
"Chloe? What are you doing here?"
"I, um... I didn't tell you this before, detective, but I've been working for the Werewolf for a little while now. I've told no-one, but Mr. Sokolov here wanted my help trying to patch him up." Chloe said, as confidently as she could muster.
"Well, great... it's good to have some specialist expertise. Judi just left, she's been adding EEG machines to his room. Go take a look - we'll wait out here. Let us know whatever kind of equipment you think you'll need."
"Thanks..." Chloe said, mentally preparing herself to see Connor's state.
Nothing could have prepared her.

She entered the room, saw his body, and she froze.
"Connor... no..." she whispered, before steeling herself. No time for panic. He needed her.
She looked at his EEG stats - they were the equivalent of a coma patient.
"Thank god..." she sighed in relief. Even with no oxygen, his brain was still running *at all.* He might just survive.
She looked into his chest cavity. By some miracle, his secondary, right-side heart was still beating. Not enough, but there was a spark of life inside it. It was smaller than his primary heart - which allowed it to be missed by the stab.
She went outside, and saw Ward talking to Ivan and Naismith.
"Hello?" Ward asked, her eyebrow raised.
"This is a specialist of ours." Ivan cut in. "Chto vam nuzhno, Chloe?"
Chloe paused. "Mne nuzhno oborudovaniye dlya dializa, i apparaty, kotoryye oni ispol'zuyut dlya podderzhaniya kisloroda v krovi do perelivaniya." she answered.
"Ms. Rosenbaum?" Ivan asked, turning.
"Yes, sir?"
"She needs dialysis equipment, and blood oxygenators."
"And..." Ward's voice turned to a conspiratorial whisper, "This is all covered by the Official Secrets Act. Do not divulge to anyone who is staying here."
"Don't worry, ma'am." Judi nodded. "Strictly confidential. I'll get those devices now."

An hour passed. Chloe tinkered away, trying her hardest to jury-rig the machines.
Each time it failed, she felt herself get closer to the solution. And yet, each time, her own heart sank.
She overlooked his stats, and quickly analysed them in her head.
His circulatory system was doing good. His backup heart must've been working extra hard... but if she couldn't get the oxygen transfer working, he wouldn't heal in time.
"Think, Chloe..." she muttered to herself.
She worked, managing to get the device to pass oxygen into the blood as it went through the dialysis machine.
"That should give you the boost you need. Your metabolism's high, so the lactic acid build-up from anaerobic respiration will make things worse if I don't keep the blood oxygenated." she said, trying to reassure herself. She took a look at the hole in his chest.
She could see his cells trying to repair the tissue. But if his metabolic system couldn't keep up with the damage, he was going to crash.
She poked her head out the door. "I need an IV drip, and a load of food paste." she said. Ivan nodded, and before long, the required material was delivered.
Her eyes darted over an EEG monitor.
His brain was running, but anaerobic respiration was far from perfect. Only the amygdala was actually running.
It was like his body didn't know how to give up.
"Heh... you're just as stubborn as ever." she chuckled grimly. "Brainwaves are minimal, but consistent. If I can get enough oxygen in your system for your brain to wake up a little more, it'll be able to start directing your healing again, and we'll have a virtuous cycle there... *If* I can handle the oxygen."
She shook her head. "No. I can do this. I have to."
"Need a hand?" Ivan asked, popping his head in.
"I..." Chloe thought for a moment, and looked down at Connor's body. "There's rib-bone in his chest cavity, where his hearts need to be. My hands are too shaky - can you remove the bone fragments? I'll keep working on these."
Ivan nodded, and grabbed a pair of tongs.
Chloe moved back over to the dialysis machines.
"Lactic acid build-up. Thankfully, your kidneys are still intact." she mused. She tinkered further, rigging the oxygenators to provide oxygen straight into his bloodstream.
Ivan looked over at Chloe, noticing the tension in her posture. "You're doing everything you can." he said, his voice steady but gentle. "He's lucky to have you."
Chloe nodded without looking up, her fingers still working.
A beeping noise came from the machine.
"Shit!" she gasped, rushing over to it. "Damn it... his secondary heart is failing! His blood's getting oxygen, but it's not moving around fast enough...."
She trembled. "Think, Chloe... *think.*" she whispered to herself. She turned to Ivan.
"We need to move him into surgical theatre." she began. "They'll have machines there that they use during heart transplants to keep the blood flowing. We can use those to sub in for his primary heart. Together with his spare heart, his blood should flow again."
Ivan clenched his jaw. "We can't move him. It's too dangerous - people could see him."
"And if we don't do anything, he'll die." Chloe retorted, a tear forming in her eye. "Ivan, please..."
Before he could answer, the door swung open.
"I heard the alarm from the corridor." Judi said briskly, setting a tray down. "What's happening?"
"His secondary heart is failing." Chloe nodded. "I can oxygenate his blood, but I can't get it moving fast enough. I need a bypass machine, something to sub in for his primary heart, but Ivan says we can't move him--"
"Then we don't." Judi cut in, pulling on gloves. "I've run cardiac ICU for six years. I can set up a portable bypass circuit at the bedside - it won't be pretty, but it'll keep the blood moving."
She looked at Chloe. "You keep doing what you're doing, I'll handle the circulation."
Chloe stared at her for a second, then nodded. "Okay. Okay - the oxygenators are rigged into the dialysis line. If you can tap into the femoral artery, we can run the circuit in parallel."
"Now you're speaking my language." Judi smiled, and got to work.

Ten minutes passed, with the two of them working in tandem.
Chloe tried to flush out the lactic acid build-up as much as possible, while Judi shunted mechanical implements into his chest cavity.
"I need to do this..." Chloe muttered to herself. "Come on... work with me."
She grabbed a needle and thread.
"I'll do left, you do right?" Judi asked.
"Sounds good."
"Okay... steady hands." Judi whispered, and they starting sewing his lungs shut again. They struggled against the superhumanly tough tissue, but managed to close up the wounds. Thankfully, the lower pair were in workable condition - the upper two, however, were like shredded balloons. Another set of stitches covered his windpipes.
Once the final suture was made, Chloe leaned back. Connor took a breath - ragged, bloody, and barely anything - but it was something. The upper pair wheezed ineffectively, but the lower set managed to inflate a little.
Behind her, Judi gave a quiet grunt of satisfaction. "Bypass is running. His pressure's coming up."
Chloe looked over at his stats, and his blood oxygen levels were rising. She breathed a sigh of relief.
She turned to Judi, and for a moment, the professional mask slipped. "Thank you. I--I don't think I could have managed that on my own."
Judi stripped off her gloves, and shook her head. "Don't mention it. He and his team have done more for this hospital than I can count - exposing the organ traffickers, catching the drug thieves, all of it. We owe him one. More than one. So, whatever he needs, whatever *you* need... you call me. Alright?"
Chloe nodded, blinking back tears. "Thanks."
She looked deeper into his wound - the gaping hole in his chest. The movement within had gotten faster, more meaningful.
"Okay..." she sighed. "Your blood should be flowing a little better now. And... yes, your leg muscles have gotten smaller. You're repurposing flesh to rebuild."
Ivan, who had been standing vigil by the door, nodded respectfully. "I'll give you a moment with him. I'd better let the others know."
"Yeah, me too." Judi nodded, before leaning in. "He's... he's someone to you, isn't he?"
"Y-yeah..." she admitted.
Judi smiled. "Well, when he wakes up - and he will - he's got some grovelling to do. Be prepared to win any argument in future with 'Well, I brought you back from the dead.' Because you did. Well done, Chloe."
"Hah... thanks, Ms. Rosenbaum."
"My friends call me Judi." she nodded.
Chloe smiled softly, as they both left.

Ivan's car pulled up outside the safehouse. He took a deep breath, and opened the door.
"Hey!" Amy waved, still in her powersuit. She took off the helmet. "We just got back from patrol. Sparky and Trey popped out for some air, if you're looking for them."
"How's Connor getting on?" asked Gwen, peeling off her domino mask.
"You haven't told them?" Ivan tilted his head.
"I thought you should." Shannon shrugged.
"Told us what?" Amy cocked an eyebrow.
"He's hurt." Ivan sighed. "Badly. Chloe's with him in the hospital now. We can't go visit, in case we blow his cover - or ours."
"Fuck..." Amy gasped, pale as a ghost. "We... we gotta do something. Where's the freak that did this?!"
"That's another thing..." Ivan rubbed his eyes. "No one patrols until Connor recovers. There's a monster on the loose, and it's strong enough to nearly kill our best operative."
"We can't just sit here doing nothing." Gwen protested.
"I'm sorry." Ivan shook his head. "We can't take that risk. Once Connor's okay, we can assess the situation, plan an counterattack... until then, we're benched. All of us."

Chloe sat on the chair next to him, and rested her head on his arm. Finally, she started to cry. Not just cry, but sob.
"Connor... you have to survive this. You can't do this to me. You can't make me fall in love with you and then go and die. You can't leave me like this... I need you. So, you're going to get up, aren't you?"
She leaned back, realising what she'd said - she hadn't admitted it to herself yet.
"I love you, Connor. Please wake up soon." she pleaded, before resting her head on him again, and falling asleep.

The morning sun slowly peeked over the horizon, and brilliant orange shined in through the windows of Riverside.
As the squad hauled in crates of lab equipment Ivan had requisitioned, Connor's motel room had been repurposed into a hospital bay.
Chloe was looking through a microscope at yet another blood sample. Her hair was frazzled, and her eyes bagged - she hadn't slept a wink.
Connor was laid on the bed still, the hole in his chest having healed somewhat. A vestigial heart had started to grow back, and his lungs were no longer visible - he was breathing unaided now. The machines continued to provide him with oxygen and nutrition.
Suddenly, the EEG reader made a beeping noise. Chloe rushed over.
It showed an increase in brainwaves - Connor had moved from deep coma to dreaming. She smiled sadly, and stroked his hair.
"Sweet dreams..." she whispered. Connor's face was still badly mangled, but he was recognisably Connor again. The hole in his throat had closed up, and his tongue had grown back.
Ivan entered the room.
"Privet, Chloe." he began. "How is he?"
"Privet." Chloe nodded. "He's okay, considering. His lower lungs are healed enough for him to breathe on his own, and his second heart is fully online again - he's rebuilding himself. He's just started dreaming." she explained.
"Excellent." Ivan smiled. "Knew it would take more than that to keep him down."
