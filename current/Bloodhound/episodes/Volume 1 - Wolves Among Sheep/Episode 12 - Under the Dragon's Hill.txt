VOLUME - Wolves Among Sheep
EPISODE - Under the Dragon's Hill
============================================================

It was the 5th of November, 2015, and the evening sun began to sink beneath the sea.
Like most evenings lately, though - this one was the beginning of a monster's prowl.
Connor overlooked a group of Super-Agents - each one with the tell-tale black-veined eye sockets of genetically enhanced super-soldiers. Scanning the warehousing unit with glowing red eyes, he watched their visible heartbeats become big red bullseyes in his field of view.
His wristblades made an audible *SHIK!* as they formed. His eyes flared, as he stepped off of the ledge.
He descended, moving faster than any human could follow, dropping down into the middle of the group with a sickening crack of bones. The Agent he landed on was now a heap of gore.
The Rage demanded blood. Connor grinned - for once, he could agree with it.
The fight was brutal and fast. Connor tore through them, the air groaning with every sweep of his blades. He didn't hold back - they weren't people, not anymore. They were weapons, and they needed to be neutralised.
His vision blurred as a pistol round slammed into his temple. His legs felt heavy, the pain in his hamstring felt sharp - he was strong, but not invulnerable.
One Agent lunged at him, a blade flashing in the moonlight. Connor caught his wrist, twisted, and snapped the man's arm in a single motion. A stab to the throat ended him.
Another Agent charged from the side, but he anticipated the attack, sidestepping and landing a crushing blow to the back of the man's skull. Fragments of bone tore through his brain, and he squirmed, then went still.
Connor's moment of triumph was interrupted by a hail of machine gun fire. He darted into cover, and thickened his costume back into chitinous armour, and his crossbows reformed.
The trick that worked so well in Algeria wouldn't cut it here - the gunner was covered in heavy-duty kevlar that would soften the bolt's blows. Instead, Connor half-focused his eyes, and shot at the gun, instead.
The bolts flew through the air, and hit their mark - the barrel. The first punched through; the second jammed in behind it.
The Agent screamed as the weapon bucked violently in his hands. The barrel burst with a flash of white-hot gas, tearing open along its length. Superheated fragments of metal and splintered casings ripped outward, punching into his torso as the gun detonated in his grip.
Before the Agent could recover, his armoured opponent was upon him. A calcified fist slammed into his head, shattering his helm. Then, Connor's armour softened, and he gripped the Agent's throat, and jerked it as hard as he could.
*SNAP!*
The Agent twitched out violently, before falling to the ground. Connor's chest heaved as he spat blood and bile.

No rest for the wicked, it seemed - police arrived on the scene. Bullets tore through the air, grazing Connor's chest.
He sprinted away, but Naismith aimed his pistol. "ARMED POLICE!" he shouted, and his shot hit its mark - Connor's hamstring.
The bullet ripped through Connor's flesh.
The pain was sharp, but Connor kept running. His legs ached, but still they carried him forward.
Naismith watched in disbelief. He muttered under his breath, his eyes wide in stunned confusion. "How the hell...?"
He steeled himself, sprinting on.
"All units, this is Alpha Charlie 4-4!" he barked into his radio. "Suspect heading north, towards Victoria Park!"
Connor's eyes darted rapidly from side to side, as the detective gained on him. He crashed through the park gates, and snapped his head around - his wounded leg slowed him enough for a human to keep chase.
He looked ahead. Then, a grin cracked under his mask.
"Remember, remember." he chuckled, as he spotted the festivities.
Belson rarely gave him anything good - but tonight, it was feeling generous, it seemed.
Swarms of tipsy spectators had clustered in the park - in preparation for the evening's bonfires and fireworks. An effigy of Guy Fawkes stood at the centre of the unlit pyre, as if watching the crowd.
Connor's wristblades deformed, shifting back into his bloodstream as he shoved his way through a group of drunken teenagers.
"ARMED POLICE! MAKE WAY!" Naismith roared, still in pursuit. He holstered his gun, and put all his effort into the charge.
Connor half-closed his eyes. He stopped looking. Started feeling. He listened to the park - felt its form.
The first distraction came fast: a group of children swung sparklers overhead. Naismith grunted in frustration as the light pained his eyes. Once his vision refocused, he could barely see his quarry.
Connor felt his leg muscles knitting themselves together, as the bullet was forced out by the reformed flesh. He grinned, and seized the opening, vaulting over a low market stall piled with firework crates. The wooden legs cracked under his weight, sending a cascade of items clattering across the path.
Then, he weaved his way through the crowd almost seamlessly, as his costume rippled and shifted. Every time he passed a group, he morphed another part.
Naismith hopped over the debris of the stall, determined to catch the freak.
Connor smirked devilishly, as he grabbed a burly man by his side, and threw him backwards.
The man stumbled into Naismith, tripping him up. He hit his head on the grass, and heaved the drunkard off of him.
"POLICE! GET BACK!" he barked, scowling. He shoved partygoers out of his way, his eyes darting to try and find his target.
Then, he spotted it - a black hood, walking away.
"Got you, you little--" he snarled, grabbing the man by the shoulders. Then, he sighed deeply, looking at the unmasked man in his black hoodie, cargo trousers, and decidedly non-kevlar shirt.
"Oh, uh... sorry, sir." he nodded quickly, before carrying on his chase.
Connor grinned cheekily, pulling his freshly-morphed clothes tighter, watching as his hunter fumbled desperately through the crowd.
"No worries, officer." he chuckled. The hoodie slowly shimmered into red, as he melted into the night.

As Connor lazily shuffled into his flat, he saw Sparky curled up on his bed.
He was asleep - but not resting. His legs twitched, and he whimpered.
"Sparky?" Connor asked, concerned.
His packmate didn't respond. He just twitched harder.
"Sparky, wake up!" he said louder.
More twitching. More crying.
"Buddy, it's okay! You're just dreaming! Come on, wake up--"
As Connor shook him, he bolted up. His eyes were aflame, glowing blood red.
The Rage.
"NO! NONONO! YOU WON'T HURT ME! YOU WON'T DO THAT AGAIN!" Sparky barked, gnashing his teeth.
"Sparky, it's me! It's Connor!"
"NO! I WON'T LET YOU!" Sparky howled, and lunged for Connor. He bit Connor's wrist and clamped down.
Connor shouted in pain. As the teeth sank in, his pupils constricted down to pinpoints. Visions flashed in his mind - buzzsaws, shock collars, injections, blowtorches...
Sparky realised what he'd done, jumped down, and whimpered again. The visions left Connor's mind.
"Connor, I'm sorry, I'm--"
Connor raised his fist, and Sparky flinched.
"Fuck... fuck, sorry." he relented. "Reflex. Same as you."
"I'm sorry. I--I didn't mean to bite you, I just--"
"It's fine." he said quickly. "I'm not mad. Just... ouch."
Sparky gingerly approached, and licked the bloody wrist.
"Haha! That tickles." Connor grinned, sitting down and gently brushing his packmate's fur.
"I'm sorry." Sparky whispered again.
"You didn't mean it."
"That doesn't matter. What if Amy had woken me up instead? I'd've taken off her hand! I'm--I'm a monster, aren't I?"
"No, you're not." Connor said firmly. "Besides, Amy's smart enough to not shake a wolf having a nightmare. I'll warn her, just in case, but... I'm not mad at you. You're a good boy, okay?"
Sparky smiled slightly, nuzzling into Connor's chest.
"How was your fight with those Agents you mentioned?" he asked, changing the subject.
"Rough." Connor shrugged. "How did your scouting go?"
"Good!" he replied, tail wagging. "Found a place where a bunch of Disciples were gathering. Heard one of them say they had to meet in 'Calston' for 'the toys.' I don't know what that means, but it sounded important!"
"Calston... there's a warehouse there. Most likely, they're moving some guns. The city's getting more unstable." Connor sighed. "We need to think tactically. There's too many fires to put out..."
"There's another fire?" Sparky tilted his head. "What are we doing here, then?"
"Sorry, figure of speech." Connor chuckled. "Do you think you could take the Calston job by yourself?"
"Easily."
"Then I'll cover patrols."
He looked outside, lost in thought.
"The cops were on my tail faster than usual - they almost caught me this time. I think they're getting lairy."
"Is that bad?" asked Sparky.
"If they find out who we really are, very." Connor said matter-of-factly. "I'm seeing Ivan next week - I'll see if he can pull some strings."
Connor's vision blurred for a second, the pain flooding his mind.
"Oh, and... remember, no killing."
"You don't have to keep reminding me." Sparky replied.
"I know..." Connor sighed.
The Rage chewed on his consciousness again. The corpses of his foes shot into his mind, almost taunting him.

A whizzing, then banging noise shook him from his stupor.
"Ahh! What was that?!" Sparky barked fearfully. "Gunshots? Grenades?"
Connor smiled. "Come to the roof."
The pair of them ascended to the top of the complex.
Fireworks.
Connor chuckled. "It's Bonfire Night."
"What's that?"
"An old human celebration." he began. "Hundreds of years ago, on this very night, a man called Guy Fawkes tried to blow up Parliament - that's the building where we make our laws."
"Why would he do that?"
"He didn't agree with the laws they were passing." Connor shrugged. "The plan was they blow up Parliament, killing the king and his government, and then organising an uprising, replacing the king with a Catholic."
"What's that?"
Connor chuckled. "You remember when I told you what 'religion' was?"
"Those stories humans tell about where the world came from?"
"Yeah, well remembered!" Connor smiled warmly. "The fight was between Catholics and Protestants - two different kinds of Christians. Where I grew up, in Derry, there's something similar - though it's not really about religion, that's just where the lines get drawn. Anyway, that's a story for another time."
"So why do we have these 'fireworks,' then?" Sparky tilted his head.
"Oh yeah - that's where we started, before I went off infodumping." Connor grinned. "So, the plot was foiled. Parliament never got blown up, the king survived, and Fawkes was killed. After that, every year, we start a big fire, and burn a little doll of him, before sending fireworks to blow up in the sky."
Sparky paused, deep in thought.
"Human celebrations are weird. First that 'halloween' thingy, now this..."
"Hey, don't knock it. The crowds in Belson helped me evade the cops." Connor shrugged. "Besides, you'll like Christmas. We have a big meal."
"How soon is Christmas?!" Sparky asked, wide-eyed and wet-mouthed.
Connor chuckled, and laid back, watching the fireworks. One detonated uncomfortably close to an airship, which blared its horn in reaction. Sparky smiled, enjoying the pretty colours from their viewpoint.
"Hmm..." he mused. "Maybe it's not *so* bad..."

"It doesn't make any sense."
Judith Rosenbaum - Judi to her friends - had uttered those words at least a hundred times that week.
The city's most talented trauma surgeon sat in the cold air, staring a hundred miles away. She still wore her scrubs, with only a cotton hoodie to keep warm.
"Hey, Judi." a young nurse began, taking a seat next to her.
She nodded softly, brushing a greying lock away from her face.
"There was nothing more you could have done." the nurse said softly.
"Exactly. I gave my all, and he still died." Judi shivered.
The nurse smiled sadly, placing a hand on her shoulder.
"When you're ready to talk, we're here for you."
Judi's eyes welled up as she cast her gaze back into the distance.
"It doesn't make any sense..." she whispered.

"Mornin'." Connor smiled. "You okay?"
"I'm fine."
"Amy..."
She sighed. "I'm fine, honest. Still a bit sore from the other night is all. Things could've been so much worse if it weren't for... for *him.*" she shuddered.
"You're afraid of him." he noted softly.
"I saw him get shot in the chest six times, and he... he didn't even flinch." she sighed. "Don't get me wrong, I'm grateful he was there, but... the thought of something like that being out there is pretty spooky."
He looked away, hiding his shame.
"You knew he had superpowers already, didn't you?" she cocked an eyebrow.
Connor hesitated. Amy could always tell when he was lying - he had to be strategic.
"He, um... yeah, kind of. It's... I'm not supposed to say, but--"
"Come on." she grinned, leaning in. "If you've got some tea about a superhero, I wanna know."
