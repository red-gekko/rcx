SCENE - Barrett Family Christmas

	The sky seemed almost determined to make this morning as miserable as possible. Slushy, cold sleet pelted down into the valley. Despite this, it failed to ruin the Barrett's morning.
	After all, it was Christmas.
	Connor, Amy, and Sparky stood outside the prison. A buzzer went off, and Vince came out.
	Amy ran over to embrace him.
	"Vince! Oh, I've missed you..." she said.
	"Oh, I have missed you so much, sweetheart." Vince replied, returning the embrace. He released her, and pulled Connor in for a bear hug.
	"Good to see you again, lad." he said.
	Connor returned the embrace, and replied "Missed you." while smiling.
	"And who's this?!" Vince said, facing Sparky, his face full of glee. "Good boy! Very well behaved, waiting to say hi! Come on!"
	With that, Sparky jumped up and began to lick Vince's face.
	"Hello! Hi! I'm Sparky! You're part of Connor's pack, which means we're family! Hello!"
	"Hah! Well, hello to you too! What a chatty boy!" Vince said, hearing only barks and yaps.
	With that, the group headed back to Riverside for Christmas dinner.
	"So... welcome to our new place." Connor gestured. "Work on the motel finished just ahead of schedule - so we finally have bedrooms again."
	Vince grinned. "This is incredible. You've both come so far."
	"Yeah. We've gone from living it large in a nice flat to squatting in a fuckin' garage." Amy snarked. The family laughed heartily, before sitting down for food.
	Vince ate well, enjoying the break from prison slop.
	"This is amazing! Who made this?" he asked between mouthfuls.
	"Uh, well..." Amy began shyly. "The shop made it. Pre-stuffed turkey. I just put it in the oven."
	"Hah! Wonders of modern society, eh? Good lass." Vince said, beaming with pride. Amy smiled happily.
	"The garden's lovely, too." Vince smiled. "Bet the wee man loves it. For a wolfdog, he's so well behaved - I'm surprised he hasn't torn this place to shreds."
	"I get plenty of exercise!" Sparky yapped happily, having greedily devoured his own bowl. He was now shamelessly begging for more food.
	"Amy..." he began, targeting the weakest link. "I'm so hungry... Connor never feeds me..."
	His pupils had almost engulfed his brilliant yellow irises.
	Amy chuckled. Connor gave her a subtle nod.
	"Go on, then, buddy. Only one, okay?" she said, giving him a sausage that was practically inhaled. She hesitated, giggled, and gave him another.
	It was never really 'only one' with him.
	The eating was done, and they began to nurse their full bellies. Connor brought a large, rectangular box to Amy.
	"Open it." he smiled.
	She hesitated, but chuckled, and did so.
	"Oh, my... oh my god." she gasped, holding it aloft.
	A guitar. A brand new one - better than her last one, too.
	"Connor, I--I can't---you shouldn't---"
	"Just say thanks and take it." Connor quipped.
	She smiled softly. "Thank you. Really."
	She hesitated, but began to play a song. An acoustic rendition of the Black Velvet Band.
	"Wow." Vince said at its end, almost crying. "I remember when you first played guitar, when you were wee."
	"Yeah, it was terrible." Connor grinned. "And look at you now."
	"Fuck you." she grinned. She may well have been the only person on Earth who could say that with such warmth.
	"So?" Vince piped up. "Any other news with yous?"
	"Oh, well, I've got a new job at a dog daycare." Amy began.
	"That's amazing." Vince beamed.
	"If she needs any tips on how we think, just let me know." Sparky grinned while nursing his full belly. Connor chuckled.

	Connor stood outside with Sparky, as he did his business on the grass.
	"Hey, bud." he began.
	"Hello!" Sparky replied, smiling. "That food was really nice! Can we have Christmas every year?"
	"Haha! Yeah, don't worry - Christmas is a yearly thing." Connor replied.
	"Yay! I can't wait until next year!" Sparky yapped, excited.
	"Haha, good... hey, there's something I want to do, but I won't do it if you don't want to."
	"What's that?" Sparky asked, tilting his head.
	"...I'm going to tell Vince about us. I mean, about what we really are."
	"Okay!" Sparky said, as nonchalantly as a talking wolf could.
	"That's it? You're not... scared?" Connor asked, surprised.
	"He's part of our pack, isn't he? He'll be happy to learn more about us, then."
	"Hah, I guess you're right..." Connor replied, deep in thought. He headed back inside.

	"Um... Vince?" he began, nervously.
	"Everything okay, lad?" Vince asked.
	"I... I need to show you something."
	"What is it?" he tilted his head.
	"So... I don't suppose you've heard about this mutant vigilante - they call him the Werewolf?" Connor asked timidly.
	"Where are you going with this?"
	Amy gave Connor a knowing nod of tacit approval.
	Connor didn't answer. Instead, he activated his morphing powers. His clothes turned black and melted, covering his body completely. The biomass hardened, forming itself into his suit - the costume now famously associated with the Werewolf. Vince turned pale. Connor un-morphed the mask, so his head became exposed. He sighed.
	"I'm sorry, I wanted to tell you sooner..."
	"Connor... Connor, my sweet lad... who did this to you?" Vince asked, tears forming in his eyes.
	"I don't know yet." Connor replied, choking back tears. "But I'm going to find out."
	"Did you know?" he asked Amy.
	"Yeah." she admitted. "I found out last month."
	"How?"
	"I, uh... accidentally shapeshifted in front of her." Connor explained nonchalantly. "Um... long story."
	"Who else knows?" Vince stammered.
	"Tommy. Trey found out a couple of nights ago. And I have a contact in the KGB who knows my real name, too."
	Vince leant against a wall, deep in consideration.
	"So..." he began. "You're the Werewolf? The one that killed Hughes?"
	"I..." Connor looked down, ashamed. "Yes."
	Vince paused for a moment, deep in thought. Then, he looked Connor in the eye.
	"Connor, my boy..." he said, tears now openly streaming down his face - but tears of pride. "You did good. You did very, *very* good."
	"I killed a man! Not just that, but I've been lying to everyone I care about! How can you be okay with this?!" Connor asked, crying now, practically begging to be condemned by his loved ones - begging for them to feel about him the way he felt about himself.
	But Vince denied this with a simple embrace.
	"You didn't kill a man." he whispered. "You saved hundreds of young kids who were being trafficked by a monster. There are children who haven't been snatched from their mothers because of what you did. How could I ever judge you for that?"
	Connor sighed deeply.
	"Connor..." Vince smiled warmly. "I won't tell a soul. Your secret is safe, lad. Just... be safe. And find the bastards that did this to you. Don't let them do it to anyone else."
	"I won't. Thank you..." Connor replied, smiling.
	Sparky barked. "See! I told you he'd understand!"
	"Oh yeah, Sparky can talk." Amy added.
	"WHAT?!" Vince gasped.
	The kids laughed giddily, and sat back down.

	Another hour passed, and Vince hugged his children, before going back to prison. Once he left, Amy and Connor sat down in the kitchen, missing him already. Sparky nuzzled between them.
	"That went well." Amy smiled.
	"Yeah." Connor nodded. "I just... I'm sick of lying."
	"I know..." she sighed. "But you can't tell people. Telling Vince was okay, but this needs to stay secret."
	"Pretty much everyone knows. It's just Chloe and Bea." he shrugged.
	"Yeah, but... not yet, okay?"
	"I know, I know..." Connor leant back.
	"Anyway... how are things going?" she asked.
	"We found out the name of the cabal we're fighting." he shrugged.
	"Who are they?"
	"They're called the Optimates." he began. "A conspiracy of the ruling class. Corporate fascists."
	"Holy shit." Amy turned pale. "And they're the ones making superhumans?"
	"Yeah." Connor nodded. "Some kind of ubermensch bullshit, only it somehow worked."
	"Doesn't make any sense." Amy shook her head. "Fascists never read to me as very clever or logical. There was a documentary I saw the other night about Mengele - he wasn't a mad scientist, he was just mad."
	"They probably stole whatever it is they know. Or they have ties to a laboratory that *is* staffed with clever people."
	"You say that like you know something..." Amy tilted her head.
	"Yeah... it gets worse."
	"How can it get worse than fascist super-soldiers?" she groaned.
	His face turned serious. "Chloe."
	"She knows?"
	"No. But she's working at that big genetics company in Belson, right? And they've got her working on the Werewolf case, studying the various superhuman DNA they've found. And the company she works for... they're connected to the Optimates." Connor said, his expression grim.
	Amy's face turned pale. "Oh my god... but why? I mean, she's only a uni placement student - why would they have her working such an important case?"
	"That's what I wondered when I found out. I mean, she's brilliant in biotechnology, but still... we think they put her there as a human shield." Connor replied, his voice cold.
	"What do you mean?" Amy asked.
	"Well, I have a tendency to smash the places connected to them. If she's there, there's no way I'm starting a fight there. Whatever it is they're doing at Vitalis, they need me to stay out of it. And I can't look into it without putting her at risk. I can't even tell her without putting her at risk."
	"That, or they're using her as bait." Amy added, her face betraying fear. "If she's involved, you're going to be focused on Vitalis. Maybe something's going to happen there, and they can hold her ransom through that."
	Connor slumped into his chair, pale as a ghost. "Whatever the reason is, it cannot be a good one. And it's all because of me..."
	"No." Amy interrupted. "It's because of who-the-fuck these people are. Do *not* cut them any slack here. I know you have feelings for Chloe, deny it all you like. But she's my friend too. And I will not let these bastards hurt her. Which means..." Amy paused for breath. "It means you need to find out what's going on. They've put her in harms way to stop you from learning something, right? So, learn it. The most dangerous place for Chloe right now is at arm's reach. It is not safe to let them continue. So, don't let them." she said authoritatively.
	Connor sighed, a mix of relief and fear. "I won't." he vowed.
	"But what I said before still stands. Do not tell her, or anyone else." she said firmly. "This day and age, the walls don't just have eyes - they have ears and noses too. All it takes is for one person you've told to slip up - not even betraying you, just making one mistake at the wrong moment - for this whole thing to collapse. As long as these bastards are out there, this has to remain under wraps."
	"I won't tell anyone else." Connor nodded, accepting the situation as it was.

	He hesitated, but cleared his throat.
	"I, uh... I wanna show you both something."
	Amy tilted her head as he stood.
	He logged into the computer. "I, uh... I did some digging. About your mum. I found this."
	He brought up a photograph of two people - a young woman, and an older man.
	"Who are they?" she asked.
	"Your mum, and her dad." he said softly.
	She sat down, looking closer. Without thinking, her hand reached out, gently brushing her mother's face.
	"I... I've never seen a photo of her before."
	"Doctor Zhao Mei. She graduated medical school a few years before you were born." Connor explained. "I couldn't find any information on who your father was, but..."
	"But?"
	Connor hesitated. "There's, um... there's something you need to know about your grandad."
	He pressed a few buttons, pulling up a profile.
	"Doctor Zhao Liang. Biochemist from the PRC. He and your mum moved here after his wife - your nan - passed away. And the weird thing is, he went off-grid completely after you were born. But it gets worse - not only do I kind of recognise him, but the name - Dr. Zhao - I heard it before. When I was being tortured."
	"Are you kidding me?!" Amy gasped. "You knew someone in my family was involved?"
	"Amy, 2% of everyone in China has the surname Zhao. You're not connected to all of them."
	She sighed. "Sorry, just... this is a lot to process."
	"If it helps, I don't think he was involved willingly." Connor smiled softly. "But he vanished right after your birth. I don't know anything yet... but I have a hunch he was involved in whatever happened when we were born. And worse, that implies the Optimates might've been involved too."
	Amy looked down, her skin paler than before. She tabbed back over to the photo.
	"They're both so... they look happy, at least."
	"Whoever Zhao is... we can assume he was a good father." Connor nodded. "I'll find him. And you can ask him everything you wanna know."
	She paused. Then she turned, and embraced her brother.
	"Thank you... really. This means a lot."
	"Don't mention it." Connor smiled warmly.
	She stretched out, yawning. "I'd better get to bed, I've got work tomorrow."
	"One more thing - I found a few more photos of her. I'll send them over to you - let you go over them in private."
	She squeezed him tight. It took a while for her to let go.
	"See you later, sis." he nodded.
	She smiled, slightly teary-eyed. "Yeah. See you later."

SCENE - Morgan Family Christmas

	That same day, Trey was having dinner at his grandparents' house. Trey's uncle and auntie, as well as his two cousins, were present too, though they kept their distance from him. The house was adorned with crucifixes, Union Jacks, and Jamaican knick-knacks - a typical Windrush generation house. While eating, Trey spoke up.
	"Nana, this is amazing - the turkey is so succulent!" he said cheerfully.
	His grandmother smiled. "Glad you like it, my boy." she replied in a heavy Jamaican accent.
	His grandfather chuckled. "The turkey is good, but the pigs-in-blankets? Oh my! You can thank our Patricia for that." he cheered, looking lovingly at his wife.
	"You just lucky they all here - if it were me and you, Jamal, I'd serve ya pasta and rice!" she said, eliciting laughter across the table.
	Trey's uncle piped up. "So, Trey. How's life been?"
	"Good, thank you." Trey replied tensely. "Work has been hectic, but at least the pay's gone up."
	"And socially?" his uncle continued. "Any new girlfriends?"
	"No. I went on a date with a boy the other week though." Trey replied, stressing the word "Boy."
	His auntie dropped her fork and gasped. "Not at the dinner table."
	"Indeed." his uncle scowled. "We don't want to hear about your--"
	"Andrew." Patricia said sternly. "You asked our Trey about girlfriends. If he said he'd been on a date with a girl, you wouldn't be moanin' and whinin'. All you should care about is that your nephew is happy."
	"I--" Andrew began, but stopped himself. He continued his food.
	Jamal chimed in. "Wicked boy. Don't be tellin' people we raised ya like that! Should be you with the pasta and the rice." With that, Trey laughed, causing his grandparents to chuckle. Phil beamed with pride at his son, and then turned to Trey's older cousin.
	"So, Jamie! How about you? Any new boyfriends?" he asked provocatively.
	Jamie caught the hint and smirked. "Oh yeah, loads. Total battyboy, me."
	Trey erupted into laughter. "I never should have taught you that word!"
	With that, his grandparents laughed too.
	"Oh, see! You tellin' everybody I told Jamie to say battyboy. The truth is comin' out in spades! Pennies dropping left, right, and centre!" Jamal grinned at Patricia, who chuckled delightfully.
	"Just because you're not guilty here, doesn't mean you're not guilty there, you wicked rogue." she retorted.
	Andrew was openly scowling at Trey and Phil, but chose not to cause a scene. The family finished dinner and sat down.

	"...and so she says to me, 'Don't look at me with that tone of voice! It smells a funny colour!'" Jamal finished an anecdote with, causing an eruption of laughter across the room.
	"Right, we'd best be off soon." Phil said, checking his phone.
	"So soon?" Patricia asked, saddened.
	"Sorry, mama - we've got to go see Kerry's folks." Phil explained, his voice breaking ever-so-slightly at the mention of his late wife.
	Patricia's expression changed to one of understanding. "Of course, my boy. We'll see you soon."
	She stood up and hugged Trey. "Bye, my little angel."
	"See you soon, nana. Love you." Trey replied, hugging back.
	"Love you too, my boy. God bless." she replied, smiling.
	Jamal stood up.
	"Be safe, my boys. Merry Christmas." Jamal said to both, hugging Trey, and then hugging Phil, who responded in kind.
	"See you." Andrew said to both, not standing up. Jamal and Patricia scoffed. Jamie and Trey's younger cousin stood up and hugged both goodbye. Trey's aunt stood and hugged both. She whispered an apology for her earlier behaviour in Trey's ear, which he gratefully accepted.

	The two arrived at Kerry's parents' house. Trey and Phil sat in the car for a moment, before taking an intake of breath. Phil had gripped the steering wheel hard enough to show the whites of his knuckles. Trey had accidentally cut his hand from scratching it in nervousness. They inhaled, then exhaled, in unison. They got out the car and exchanged supportive glances at each other. They knocked on the door.
	Kerry's father, Howard, answered the door. Upon seeing the two, he became visibly disappointed. "Fine. Come in." he said through gritted teeth. The two entered.
	The house was almost hypnotically minimalist. Unlike Phil's parents, Kerry's were born in the UK, and so spoke with English accents. Kerry's mother, Gloria, came in and hugged both.
	"Merry Christmas! How has your day been?" she asked. Howard rolled his eyes.
	"It's been good, nan. How has yours been?" Trey replied, smiling sadly. She was visibly drunk.
	"Good! Neither of us wanted to cook, so we had a Chinese instead." she replied, slurring slightly.
	Howard came in, as Phil revealed the presents they brought. Gloria excitedly opened hers - first, she opened Phil's.
	"Ah! I've been looking everywhere for this!" she said, holding the gift - a device which converted tapes into digital files - with glee.
	"Well, they don't sell them in shops anymore - I got that one online." Phil smiled.
	"Ah, I never got on with online shopping." Gloria said, cheerfully. She opened Trey's gift next.
	"Oh wow! Thank you so much sweetheart!" she said, holding the gift - a blanket hoodie with hand-and-foot warmers - high in the air.
	"Glad you like it, nan! I know you get cold at night sometimes, what with your circulation... I thought it might help you." Trey said shyly.
	"Such a sweet boy. I think I've got your presents here somewhere... ah!" she handed both their presents.
	"Oh... a £20 gift voucher to ASDA. That's... thank you so much!" Trey said, trying his hardest to be appreciative.
	"Ah... socks! Can never have enough of those!" Phil put on a smile. Trey looked over at Howard, who had not sat down yet - despite his bad leg.
	He refused to show any hospitality. He also had not acknowledged the presents brought for him.
	"Uh, grandad..." Trey began. He scowled at the title. "These presents are for you... merry Christmas. I hope you like them."
	Howard scoffed.
	"Is it your mother, returned to life?" he asked. Trey, Phil, and Gloria all looked down. "No? Didn't think so. Then I don't want it." he snarled.
	"Howard, please... Trey is all that we have left of Kerry." Gloria began, but she buttoned it once Howard continued.
	"Oh? And why is that? Oh that's right, because she *DIED* giving birth to him! And who's fault is that?" Howard growled, almost ferally.
	Gloria looked down in fear, shaking slightly at Howard's outburst.
	"Why are you defending him? Eh? You'd rather stick up for your daughter's killer than you would your daughter?!" he asked, looming over her. She cowered away from him.
	"Stop blaming my dad for what happened!" Trey snapped.
	"Oh, don't worry - I blame you plenty." Howard snarled back.
	"Enough!" Phil snapped. "We came here hoping to bridge the gap. I would never dream of keeping your only grandson away from you. But I can tell when we're not wanted. So, thank you for the gifts, Gloria, merry Christmas to you. Good night." Phil said. Both Trey and Phil left.
	Howard shouted out as they got in the car.
	"A couple of poncey gifts won't bring her back! You killed my daughter, Morgan! Both of you!" he screamed, throwing his gifts at their car. Trey cried silently while his dad drove them home. They entered their house, and started to openly cry with each other.

SCENE - Jefferson Family Christmas

	The aftermath of Christmas dinner at the Jefferson household was peaceful, if a little sluggish. The room practically oozed warmth - the scent of cinnamon and roast goose still clung to the air. Bea lay sprawled across a reclining sofa, her stomach full, her eyelids heavy. Her year-younger sister, Tea, was fast asleep across the room, wrapped in a throw blanket. Their dad had retired upstairs for a nap, and their mum had disappeared into the bath nearly an hour ago, likely dozing there too.

	Her phone buzzed softly on the side table. She blinked slowly, and reached for it.
	"Merry Christmas, gorgeous." Charlie had written. "I hope your day's been magical. I've been thinking about you all day. I got you a little something... was wondering if I could see you? Just for a bit. If not, that's okay. I get it."
	She smiled, cheeks warming. She tapped out a reply.
	"Merry Christmas! It's been lovely – my family are all in food comas lol. Hope yours has been good too?"
	He replied almost instantly.
	"It's been fine. Quiet. Just wish I had someone around who really gets me, you know? Everyone's so wrapped up in their own little worlds. It would be lovely if you wanted to meet up?"
	Something about the phrasing made Bea pause. She bit her lip, then typed:
	"Where would you want to meet?"
	"The town square? Or... you could come to mine? It's warm. I've got mulled wine and an actual fireplace lol." he sent.
	Her fingers hovered, hesitant.
	"I can meet at the park for a bit! I can't stay too long though – everyone's asleep but I'll need to be back soon."
	"Sure." he replied abruptly.
	She stood up quietly and grabbed her coat.
	"Where are you off to?" Tea murmured from beneath the blanket, revealing herself. She was almost identical to Bea in terms of facial structure. However, where Bea's skin was a constellation of vitiligo, Tea's was a solid sheet of light-brown. Where Bea's eyes contrasted through heterochromia, Tea's were both a shimmering hazel.
	In most dynamics, the 'normal' sister would be the suave, confident one; while the 'abnormal' would be shy and socially deprived. The key word there was 'most.'
	Tea's clothes were almost impressively plain. A grey hoodie over light blue jeans - in this case, unbuttoned at the top to accommodate Christmas dinner. Her hair was as curly as Bea's, but rather than the bright and loud red-dyed afro bob that Bea had opted for, her thick curls had been bunched into a low ponytail, with a few strands dangling in front of her face. All in all, neither sister could be put into a label - fittingly, neither would desire to be.
	"Just a walk." Bea whispered. "I'll be back soon."
	The streets were cold and slick. The sky was darkening, the stars blurry behind thin clouds. Bea pulled her coat tighter as she reached the park.
	Charlie stood waiting, hands in his coat pockets, eyes on the ground.
	He looked up and smiled as she approached. "Hey."
	"Hey!" she replied, breath puffing in the cold.
	"I didn't think you'd come." he began. His voice was low. "I thought maybe you'd changed your mind."
	"No, I just didn't want to stay long. Everyone's still up, mostly."
	He pulled a small wrapped box from his coat and handed it to her.
	Bea opened it carefully. Inside was a delicate silver bracelet.
	She stared at it. "Charlie... this is beautiful."
	He smiled.
	She looked down at the bracelet again. "Thank you. Really."
	He reached forward, brushing her hair behind her ear. "You don't have to say anything. Just being here... it means more than you know."
	She nodded, pushing her face into his hand.
	"I wanted to give you something that reminded you how different you are. How real." he said. "Not like... the people around you."
	She shifted. "Charlie..."
	"I mean, it's nice they're there." he said quickly. "But they don't see you. Not like I do. You said yourself you feel like the extra in your own friend group sometimes."
	"I didn't mean it like that."
	He studied her for a moment, then reached for her hands. "Come back to mine?"
	She blinked. "What?"
	"Just for a bit." he said quickly. "I've got this bottle of wine I've been saving, and I made mince pies. I thought... maybe we could just have a moment. Something that's ours."
	"I can't, sorry. I should probably head back soon."
	His expression shifted. The smile disappeared. "Right."
	She frowned. "I'm sorry, I just - "
	"It's fine." he said sharply, pulling his hands away. "It's totally fine. I just thought maybe you'd want to spend time with me. Guess I misread things."
	"Charlie, I--"
	"No, it's okay. Seriously. Don't worry about it." His tone was brittle. "Enjoy your perfect little family night."
	"That's not fair." Bea said, a tremble in her voice.
	He turned away, jaw tight. "You know what? Just forget I asked. I shouldn't have. This was stupid."
	There was a beat of silence, and then, as suddenly as he had snapped, he softened.
	"I'm sorry." he said, running a hand through his hair. "God, I'm such an idiot. You didn't deserve that. I just... I missed you so much today. I guess I let it get to me."
	Bea stood frozen.
	"I'm sorry, Bea. I just thought... I thought we had something. And I thought maybe today, of all days, I'd matter enough to you to get a little of your time. But it's okay. Really."
	He offered a weak smile, eyes sad. "Go home. They'll miss you."
	Bea gulped, a lump in her throat. "Of course you matter to me. It's just... it's Christmas, you know? We see each other every day at uni."
	"You don't have to explain." he said, backing away.
	She didn't reply. She couldn't. Her feet felt stuck to the pavement.
	"Merry Christmas, Bea." he whispered.
	And then he was gone, his footsteps echoing off the wet stones, leaving her standing alone with the bracelet still in her hands.
	She sighed deeply.

SCENE - Derrington Family Christmas
