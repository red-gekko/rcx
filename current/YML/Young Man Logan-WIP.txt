	EPISODE - Lost Cause
		______________________________
		SCENE
			"GET DOWN!"
			The cannonball tore through Billy's line, and he hit the ground.
			Another soldier - a pimply young conscript, he imagined - stumbled headlessly, before collapsing.
			Across the killing field, the famed Marse Robert uttered his most famous quote.
			"It is well that war is so terrible, otherwise we should grow too fond of it."
			Wrong. Dead wrong.
			As Billy looked down at his blue uniform, now stained red with offal, he cursed his lot in life.
			It is damned that war is so terrible, for we have no choice in our fondness for it.
			"Billy! Get up!"
			Billy rolled his eyes at the familiar voice, as another solider raced over and dragged him to cover.
			"Logan..." he groaned. "Lucky damned Logan."
			"Takes more than a cannonball to put me down, bub." he grinned. "Come on, let's get you outta here."
			He reached for his friend, but gasped as he realised.
			"Yeah..." Billy spat. "Not all of us are as lucky as you."
			"It's bad. You need a doctor." Logan nodded. "Come on, I'll cover you."
			"I ain't gonna make it back." Billy shook his head. "Logan..."
			"Billy, come on! There's... there's gotta be *something!*"
			"Logan... can you tell my mom I'm sorry?" Billy asked.
			"Tell her yourself! COME ON!"
			"Please... Please, Logan..."
			He looked away.
			Thirty years on this Earth, though you wouldn't know. He still had a spry, almost teenage look about him.
			He had many, many more years to come. Too many.
			And no matter what, this would always be the bit he hated.
			Outliving.

			Fredericksburg, Virginia. December 13th, 1862.
			The deadliest day of the battle. One of the deadliest days in American history, up until the wrath of Thanos.
			And for young Logan, a chance.
			Maybe, just maybe... a chance to die. To satisfy that morbid curiosity.
			To find out if *anything* on this spinning ball of water and rock could kill him.
			Alas, alas.
			Logan walked through the battlefield, numb and shellshocked.
			His uniform was torn and bloodsoaked; but underneath, he was the picture of health.
			He always was - and he hated that, too.
			"Hey! HEY! YANKEE!" a grey-coated Reb jeered. "WELCOME TO VIRGINIA! HAHAHA!"
			Logan stopped in his tracks, and turned.
			"Ooh, lookee here boys! Yankee boy's angry!"
			"Don't move, bluecoat." another growled, training his repeater on Logan's head. "You're under arrest for trespassing on Confederate property."
			"You bastards killed Billy." he said matter-of-factly.
			"Huh? Who's Billy?"
			"Seriously? A literal Billy Yank?" another laughed.
			"You killed Billy." he snarled ferally.
			"Alright, that's enough." their officer barked. "You're coming with--"
			*SNIKT!*
			The officer wanted to scream. Most in his position would.
			However, side effects of having a screaming berserker's claws bury into your throat include, but are not limited to, a certain difficulty vocalising.
			"SH-SHIT! GET HIM!"
			Too late. Logan pounced, with three bony knives poking out from each fist. Then, he swept, like a... bobcat.
			The first slash blinded the jeering greycoat. The second liberated his bowels from his abdomen.
			Then he turned, and drove them into th lungs of the third. He raised him in the air, and threw him to the ground.
			"*RRRARAAAGGH!*" he screamed, as his blades swept into the lifeless body again. And again. And again.
			The Berserker Rage. His gift - his curse.
			"DIE! DIE! *DIE!*" he roared, as he continued to rip at the gory husk.
			Finally, he fell to his knees, and his breath came out in ragged gasps.
			Gore reached up to his elbows, and he shivered as he stood.
			"Hellfire..." he cursed, as the blades sheathed back into his hands. The torn holes where they used to be quickly closed up.
			"Logan..." a voice echoed.
			He looked up at the darkening sky, and howled.
			"Logan..."
			"Logan!"
			He shot up out of bed, and raised his fists.
			"Mister Logan!" the boy gasped. "I--I--"
			"Damn... sorry, kid." Logan sighed, dropping his guard. "Nightmare."
			"I get those." the boy nodded. "Mama says nightmares are the Devil's dreams."
			"...I can see that." he shrugged. "What's the matter, anyway?"
			"Oh! We're getting ready to head on. Mister Monroe says the weather's clearing up, so we can start moving again."
			Logan sniffed the air like a dog, and grimaced. "No way. There's a snowstorm coming. Crossing the Rockies now is suicide."
			"...what's that mean, Mister Logan?"
			"N-nothin', kid." he quickly added. "Don't worry - when we get snowed in and run out of food, I'll eat you last."
			The boy laughed, as did Logan. Then, he left the tent.
			Logan laid back down, and sighed.
			Bunch of idiots. He'd be better off leaving them all to freeze, just like the Donners he'd read about.
			But... no. As much as he wanted to, he couldn't be the kind of man who let kids freeze on a damn fool's account.

	EPISODE - Joy to the Just
		______________________________
		SCENE
			To most, the hot Californian sun was an oppressive god. Peeled skin and cooked flesh; dried crops and tired men; chapped lips and hot rashes - that was its worship, its liberty.
			Logan rolled his shoulders as the orange tyrant crawled behind the Sierra Nevadas; he enjoyed it. The peel. The cook.
			Feeling. Something he held onto, as hard as he could.
			Not the healthiest coping mechanism; but it was his, and his alone.
			Bucey nickered at the approaching settlement, as Logan reached forward and stroked his neck.
			"Good boy..." he smiled softly.
			"Evenin', mister." he nodded as he passed a townsman, half-sleeping at the entrance.
			"Hm? Oh, hey mister." the man nodded. "Welcome to Hope, San Joaquin."
			"Hope?" Logan grinned. "We could all use some of that, bub."
			"Indeed. Um... who's your friend, there?"
			The gagged man slung over Bucey's rear wriggled in protest.
			"He's a bad fella." Logan chuckled. "Where's the Sheriff's office?"
			"Down by the saloon, right next door. Can't miss it." the man pointed. "Sheriff McGillis insisted he be stationed there to keep an eye on the drunkards."
			"Smart man." Logan nodded. "See you around."

			The door swung open, and McGillis looked up from his newspaper.
			"Well, I'll be." he cocked an eyebrow. "Is that Ronnie Smalls?"
			"In the flesh." Logan grinned. "Gift wrapped and everythin'."
			McGillis motioned for his deputy to take Smalls away, and then extended his hand.
			"Sheriff Nolan McGillis. And you are?"
			"Jim Hawkins." Logan smiled, taking it and shaking.
			"Pleasure to meet you, Mr. Hawkins. So, I'm guessing you a bounty hunter?"
			"What gave it away? The tied-up outlaw, or the fact I sauntered into this dump and came straight here?"
			"The dry wit and attitude, mostly." McGillis grinned. "You lookin' for work, Mr. Hawkins?"
			"Matter of fact, I am." he nodded. "I'm pretty good at what I do."
			"But what you do ain't very nice." McGillis snarked. "Alright. Wesley Donovan - real piece of work, him. His gang have been robbing stagecoaches like it's 1659."
			"Sounds to me like I need a stagecoach, then." Logan stroked his chin.
			"You wanna use yourself as bait?"
			"Poisonous bait. Best kind." Logan chuckled. "You got one I can borrow?"
			"Yeah, matter of fact, I do." McGillis nodded. "Johnny Freeman, down by the workshop. He's a jumpy little fella on account of his history, but he'll fix you up a wagon no trouble."
			"Thanks." Logan bowed his head. "See you around, sheriff."
			"I sure hope you will, Mr. Hawkins." McGillis smiled. "Donovan's gang are savages - don't underestimate them."
			"Hey, nothin' wrong with a little savagery." Logan grinned, his teeth showing.

			"Johnny Freeman?" Logan called out, as he entered the cluttered barn.
			His ears pricked up, and heard Johnny's heart race.
			"Hey, easy. I ain't here for trouble."
			"H-h-hello?" Johnny asked, trembling. "A-are you with Smitty?"
			"Who the hell's Smitty?"
			At that, Johnny relaxed immediately, and rushed over.
			"S-sorry about that, mister. Johnny Freeman, at your service."
			"Jim Hawkins." Logan smiled, shaking his hand. "Sheriff McGillis told me you'd be able to fix me up a coach? I'm tryin' to draw out this Donovan character."
			"Donovan?! Mister, are you crazy?"
			"A little." Logan grinned. "Can you?"
			"Y-yeah, 'course." Johnny nodded hurriedly. "I'll get her ready before the evening."
			"Thanks." Logan smiled. "Hey... who's Smitty?"
			"He--he's a bad man, mister Hawkins." Johnny shivered. "He's the son of my old master."
			"Master? California was a free state." Logan tilted his head.
			"That didn't stop Master Calhoun from bringing us along with him from Texas during the Gold Rush." Johnny nodded awkwardly. "Smitty was a bushwhacker during the War, and he never forgave me for havin' the audacity to be free. I been sharecropping for him ever since."
			"That ain't right." Logan growled. "Where's Smitty now?"
			"Probably the Saloon. He's always in there, when he ain't bullyin' or beatin' me."
			"I'll be back for my wagon in the mornin'." Logan nodded, heading off.

			Smitty and his boys cheered, as their fifth whiskeys of the night were clinked and downed.
			The saloon went silent, as the doors swung open. The floorboards creaked on Logan's entrance.
			"Evenin', mister." he nodded at the barman. "I'll have a whiskey. Actually, make it two."
			"Comin' right up."
			"Hey, stranger." Smitty called over, joining him. "You new here?"
			"It would seem so." Logan nodded curtly. "You are?"
			"Smitty. Smitty Calhoun." he said, extending his hand.
			"I've heard of you." Logan scowled. "Now, you wanna listen up. You're gonna leave Johnny Freeman alone, you hear?"
			Smitty scoffed. "Oh, you're one of them bleeding-hearts, ain't ya? Where you from, boy? New York?"
			"Canada." Logan grinned.
			Smitty and his boys laughed - a whiny, rattling sound. He put his hand on Logan's shoulder.
			"Listen here, Canuck. I don't know how things work in Limey Country, but over here? You don't talk that way."
			Logan growled. "Take your dirty hand off me, kid, or I'll make you eat that bar."
			"Hah! Kid? Boy, I'm older than you! Go on - threaten me again." he grinned, putting his free hand on his revolver.
			Logan flashed his teeth, and then--*SLAM!*
			He wrapped his arm around Smitty's shoulder, and slammed his head mouth-first into the bar. Then he raised him up, and slammed again.
			Smitty collapsed to his knees, his teeth hitting the ground one by one.
			"Shit, hellfire!" the bartender gasped, dropping down in fear.
			The first of his boys came with a knife. Logan gripped it blade-first, and drove his elbow into the man's throat. Then, he shot his boot up another's chin, and finally, a haymaker sent the third man sprawling.
			"HEEEEY!" McGillis roared, storming in. "WHAT IN TARNATION IS GOIN' ON DOWN HERE?!"
			"Sheriff." Logan grinned. "These boys jumped me outta nowhere. Threatened me. Ain't that right?"
			Silence.
			"I *said,* ain't that right?"
			Murmurs of agreement came from the bruised, bloodied men.
			"Is that so?" McGillis scowled, walking over. "Mr. Hawkins... let me be clear. You can stay here tonight because I need Donovan swinging. But you start anything like this in my town again... it'll be you up on my gallows."
			"Oh, me? Sir, I'm a real church boy." Logan chuckled. "You won't hear any more trouble from me."
			"I'd better not." McGillis nodded. "Alright, everyone, show's over. Bed time."
			"Yes, sheriff." the patrons whispered, walking off to their homes.
			Logan rolled his shoulders, and smiled devilishly. He hopped onto Bucey's back, and they trotted downstream. There, he pitched his tent, and curled into a ball.
			
			They were itchy. Itchier than normal.
			They *hated* that they weren't being used.
			Logan sighed, and closed his eyes - then, his fists.
			*SNIKT!*
			He grimaced, as the bone claws cut from his hands. He reopened his eyes, and looked down at the bloody blades. Then he shifted them back in, and watched intently as the cut flesh knitted itself back together.

	EPISODE - Cain and Cain
		______________________________
		SCENE
			Logan gasped and spat blood, as he shot up from the cold marble table.
			He looked around frantically - ah. He recognised the place quicker than he wished.
			NYPD's morgue.
			"Heh... couldn't even do the decency of killin' me properly, could you Creed?" he scowled, rising.
			His clothes were still just as bloodied as before - oh well. He prowled out into the streets.
			"Extra! Extra! Read all about it!" a newsboy announced. "Famous socialite and philanthropist James Logan murdered by the Slasher!"
			Logan sank, and scurried into an alleyway.
			Well, this stint had lasted longer than most.
			No... Creed was still out there, for sure. Somewhere.
			So be it - the hunt would have to continue.
			He finished changing into freshly stolen clothes, and walked slowly along the railroad.
			He was James Howlett. Logan. The Wolverine. Brother and, hopefully, killer of Victor Creed.
			A man. Not a beast.
			Not.
			Not.

			"Who are you?" the voice pressed for what felt like the thousandth time.
			Logan couldn't see  - no, the great metal helm that encased him made sure of that. Electrodes struck into his brain as he tried to answer.
			"J-James Logan Howlett."
			"Who are you?"
			"Wolverine."
			"Who are you?!"
			"...Weapon X."
			The scientist grinned, as the lab door opened. Beside him was a young woman, with light brown skin and a long labcoat. She looked down at her notepad - trying not to look up.
			"Ah, colonel Stryker." the doctor nodded. "Weapon X's reconditioning is going well. Its old memories are beginning to fade."
			"Good. And the Adamantium?"
			"Fully bonded, sir."
			"Excellent. Let's go - I want to discuss our strategy."
			The younger woman crept over subtly, and flicked a few switches at the console. Then, some more.
			"Coming, doctor Crow?" Stryker pressed.
			"Yes, colonel." she nodded, heading out.
			Logan's body tensed. The machine on his head stopped buzzing, and started dimming.
			He stirred. His new, alien skeleton struggled to move.
			Then...
			*SNIKT!*
