**CASE FILE: VOLKOV SYNDROME**

  **ICD-XX: VLK-8752
  **AKA: Complex Systemic Dysregulation Disorder
  **Disorder Type: Rare congenital multi-system disorder
  **Prevalence: ~1 in 50,000 individuals
  **First Identified: 1985, Ukrainian SSR, by Dr. Stepan Volkov

______________________________

CLINICAL OVERVIEW

  Volkov Syndrome (VS) is a rare hereditary condition characterized by multi-system involvement, present from birth in its congenital form. It arises from a combination of a genetic predisposition and in-utero environmental stressors (e.g., maternal illness, drug use, or other gestational abnormalities). It causes immunological and neurological dysregulations, as well as endocrinological, haematological, psychiatric, respiratory, sensory, and orthopaedic complications.
  Despite the severity of symptoms if left untreated, VS is considered manageable with early diagnosis and consistent treatment.
  VS is controversial in some circles, due to the wide variance in symptoms and the extreme diversity of mutation present. However, mainstream scientific literature considers the disorder valid, due to the near-universal concurrence of mutant expressions and common trigger for activation (prenatal stressors)

  A rarer 'late-onset' form exists, typically emerging in early-to-mid adulthood with milder symptoms, often misdiagnosed as lupus or Addison's disease.

ETIOLOGY, EPIDEMIOLOGY, & PATHOPHYSIOLOGY

  Genetic Markers: mutations in the following genes have been identified:
  
    TYK2 - involved in cytokine signaling and immune regulation. Known associations with autoimmune conditions and hematologic effects.
        Mutation appears to trigger a number of autoimmune disorders and immunocompromisation.
    PAX6 - Transcription factor involved in development, especially of eyes and CNS
        Implicated in neurological and ocular symptoms
    FOXP1 - Essential to healthy brain development 
        Implicated in neuropsychiatric, epileptic, and neurological symptoms
    FOXP2 - Primarily involved in neurodevelopment and speech
        Implicated in speech disorders and neuropsychiatric symptoms
    IRF5 - Immune regulator, key in the production of pro-inflammatory cytokines
        Implicated in immune dysregulation
    COL1A1 - Primarily involved in the development of collagen
        Implicated in orthopaedic symptoms
    CTLA4 - Primarily involved in immune checkpoint regulation and self-tolerance
        Implicated in hypersensitivity and autoimmunity
    VWF - Gene relating to production of Von Willebrand Factor, a glycoprotein used in coagulation
        Implicated in haematological symptoms
    PDCD1 - Primarily involved in triggering apoptosis in cytophagic T cells and preventing apoptosis in regulatory T cells
        Implicated in immune dysregulation

  It is assumed there are more genes implicated. Further testing is underway.
  These genes are located on a variety of chromosomes. The concurrence of such a diverse range of genes is caused by meiotic drive present in all Volkov-type alleles.
      To elaborate: VS-type mutations all possess an unusual ability to favour their own selection over non-mutant alleles during meiosis. Thus, whereas most alleles have a 50% chance to be inherited, VS-type alleles have a ~93% chance to be inherited. It is rare, but not unheard of, for only a few or one allele to be inherited anyway, leading to Partial VS.
  Differential diagnosis required to avoid conflation with similar mutations such as Lupus.
  Triggering Factors: In utero exposure to:
   * Maternal autoimmune diseases
   * Drug or alcohol abuse
   * Severe maternal stress or malnutrition
   * Environmental toxins

  Abnormal fetal development in genetically predisposed individuals triggers "Active" state, leading to chronic, progressive inflammation across multiple systems.

  Later-life exposure to triggering factors (typically severe stress or drug misuse) can result in development of Late Onset Volkov Syndrome (LOVS).
  A subset of LOVS previously called Senile Volkov Syndrome (SVS), now properly known as Elder-Onset Volkov Syndrome (EOVS) occurs in 40% of carriers aged 70 and older. EOVS is often confused with complications associated with old age such as Dementia.

  "Active" cases of VS (AVS) are currently prevalent in ~1 in 50,000 individuals, making the disease more common than Huntington's and IPEX but rarer than Lupus or Osteogenesis imperfecta, with which it is commonly confused.
  The genetic markers exhibit autosomal dominant inheritance. One parent with a mutation can pass it on, but development of AVS depends on additional prenatal environmental triggers.
  It is still currently unknown what exact conditions in utero will cause AVS.
  Individuals with homozygous expression (that is, both versions of VS alleles) appear to be guaranteed to develop AVS.
  Homozygous VS patients also are significantly more likely to develop a VS variant (see below.)

  In addition to the above, epigenetic markers, particularly DNA methylation of FOXP2 and acetylation patterns near the IRF5 promoter, are being investigated as potential severity predictors.

  VS appears to be more common among individuals of Eastern European, particularly East Slavic heritage. However, it is unclear if this is due to early detection bias resulting from original discovery and excessive testing in the USSR in the late 80's.
  It was once erroneously assumed that excessive consumption of vodka resulted in higher incidence of AVS among Eastern Europeans, but this has been debunked as anti-Soviet propaganda.

  AVS is negligibly more common in female individuals, with a ratio of 52:48. This appears to be because male fetuses with AVS are slightly more likely to be stillborn. It is unclear why this is, especially as male sufferers of AVS do not appear to have more severe symptoms than female sufferers.

  Due to misdiagnosis, lack of screening capacity, and social stigma, it is difficult to accurately estimate the number of inactive Carriers worldwide. Global averages suggest 1 in 7,000.
  "Partial Carriers" (individuals with 1 or more VS-associated mutation, but not the full 5) are much more rare, estimated at ~1 in 29,000.

  Since the 1980's, reported cases of AVS have risen 204%. It is unclear if cases are actually rising or if accurate diagnosis is becoming more commonplace.

  VS is found in all known ethnic groups, with minimal variance in prevalence, aside from above statistical anomalies in Eastern Europe.
  It is believed VS originiated among early Homo sapiens who had not left Africa, explaining its global existance.
  Prior to the advent of modern medicine, most children born with AVS would have been described simply as "sickly", and would not have made it to adulthood.
  Due to the Meiotic Drive effect, the condition has survived despite being detrimental to survival.
  Circumstantial evidence suggests VS provides some innate resistance to retroviruses, including HIV.
  Individuals with Congenital VS have on average higher lateral thinking skills, even those with cognitive impairments.
  AVS patients, despite the aggregated biological stressors they face from the condition, have similar lifespans to the general populace when treated with medication. Circumstantial evidence suggests that Congenital AVS patients experience senescence at a slower rate than average. Studies are underway to identify the cause of this. VS patients on average have longer telomeres than the general populace. However, the increased cancer risk limits their life expectency.

SIGNS & SYMPTOMS

  Note: Due to variant types of VS, not all patients will exhibit all symptoms.

  Typical symptoms (seen in both treated and untreated cases):
   * Diabetes Mellitus (type 1)
      This symptom is universal among all VS patients, and is caused by autoimmune responses causing damage to the pancreas.
      Treatment: Insulin
      Frequency: 100%
   * Epilepsy
      This symptom is caused by neurodysregulation resulting in irregular electric signalling
      Treatment: Anticonvulsant medication
      Frequency: 58%
   * Asthma
      This symptom is caused by autoimmune responses resulting in chronic inflammation in the lungs
      Treatment: Usage of inhaler
      Frequency: 73%
   * Hyperreactivity to allergens
      This symptom is caused by CTLA4 mutations triggering hypersensitivity due to immune dysregulation
      Treatment: Provision of antihistamines and epinephrine
      Frequency: 62%
   * Joint pain
      This symptom is caused by chronic inflammation affecting the joints
      Treatment: Provision of analgesics and anti-inflammatory drugs/Disease-Modifying Anti-Rheumatic Drugs (DMARDs)
      Frequency: 51%
   * Chronic stutter/slurring/Aphasia
      This symptom is caused by FOXP2 mutations resulting in language difficulties, as well as hearing loss affecting speech development
      Treatment: Speech and language therapies
      Frequency: 38%
   * Chronic migraines
      This symptom is caused by various inflammatory responses resulting in pressure-headaches
      Treatment: Analgesics/pain relief
      Frequency: 61%
   * Fatigue
      This symptom is most likely caused by constant autoimmmune responses draining the patient
      Treatment: Healthy living and regular rest periods
      Frequency: 73%
   * Psoriasis/Dermatitis
      This symptom is caused by TYK2 mutations
      Treatment: Anti-inflammatory drugs and application of topical creams where needed
      Frequency: 46%
   * Mild hearing loss/Tinnitus
      This symptom can be caused both by COL1A1 mutations deforming inner earbones, neurosensory disruption, and inflammation in the inner ear
      Treatment: Provision of hearing aids and management of osteoporosis and inflammation
      Frequency: 53%
   * Strabismus (typically esotropic, rarely exotropic)
      This symptom is caused by PAX6 mutations
      Treatment: Vision-correcting aids such as glasses
      Frequency: 62%


  Advanced symptoms (seen in untreated or treatment-resistant cases, or during flare-ups)
   * Neurological disease (Encephalitis, meningitis)
      This symptom is caused by autoimmune responses targeting the brain
      Treatment: Steroids to combat inflammation
      Frequency: 15%
   * Neuropsychiatric manifestations (e.g., hallucinations, severe mood changes) not explained by previous mental health history
      The mechanisms of this symptom are unclear, though it is believed that the FOXP1 mutation makes patients more prone to neuropsychiatric symptoms
      Treatment: Psychiatric care
      Frequency: 31%
   * Chronic bronchitis, Haemoptysis
      This symptom is caused by asthma symptoms becoming severe
      Treatment: Healthy management of asthma
      Frequency: 24%
   * Anemia
      This symptom is caused by VWF mutations resulting in chronic blood loss
      Treatment: Iron supplementation during flare-ups
      Frequency: 25%
   * Acquired haemophilia
      This symptom is caused by the VWF mutations resulting in hypocoagulation
      Treatment: Desmopressin to be taken during flare-ups
      Frequency: 12%
   * Arthritis
      This symptom is caused by untreated joint inflammation
      Treatment: Provision of DMARDs and physiotherapy during flare-ups
      Frequency: 35%
   * Osteoporosis/Bone deformities
      This symptom is caused by COL1A1 mutations
      Treatment: Provision of Bisphosphonates, Teriparatide, and/or Alendronate. Physiotherapies and surgery to correct deformities
      Frequency: 44%
   * Anaphylaxis, unprompted allergic reactions (that is, anaphylactic shock with no evident trigger)
      This symptom is caused by immune dysregulation becoming more severe
      Treatment: Epinephrine injections
      Frequency: 9%
   * Cataracts
      This symptom is caused by PAX6 mutations
      Treatment: Surgery to remove cataracts
      Frequency: 27%
   * Melanin changes
      This symptom is caused by autoimmune responses damaging the adrenal glands and causing addison's-like symptoms. Patients may experience hyperpigmentation, cafe-au-lait patches, abnormal freckling, vitiligo, or a mixture.
      Treatment: Provision of hydrocortisone
      Frequency: 3%
   * Hepatitis
      This symptom is caused by autoimmune responses targeting the liver
      Treatment: Provision of corticosteroids to reduce autoimmune response
      Frequency: 19%
   * Cytokine storm in response to mild infection
      This symptom is caused by extreme immune dysregulation
      Treatment: Immunosuppression and intensive care
      Frequency: 4%
   * Cancer development
      This symptom is caused by immune dysregulation
      Treatment: standard oncological treatments, regular screening, and/or palliative care
      Frequency: 53%

  Currently, it is unknown if any symptoms are expressed among carriers. Circumstantial evidence implies that carriers have a slightly elevated risk of cancer compared to non-carriers.
  Mild-to-moderate cognitive impairments are present in ~18% of AVN patients. These may manifest as attention deficits, slowed processing speed, or impaired working memory. Executive functioning can be reduced, especially in VS-N, VS-D, and mixed subtypes. Cognitive outcomes improve with early intervention and stable medical management.
  
  Prognosis:
   * Congenital Form: Manageable with modern medicine; high quality of life possible with early intervention.
   * Adult-Onset Form: Milder, often misdiagnosed, but highly treatable.
   * Untreated Severe Cases: Risk of organ failure, encephalitis, and death.

DIAGNOSIS

  Confirmed via:

    * Genetic testing
    * Elevated inflammatory markers (CRP, ESR)
    * CSF analysis (if neurological symptoms are present)
    * "Halston Test"
        Extract blood sample from patient. Isolate cytotoxic T lymphocytes.
        Expose lymphocytes to two (2) cell lines: one, a HeLa sample, and two, a sample from the patient. The patient's sample must not have any cancer cells contained within.
        In individuals without VS, lymphocytes will immediately target the HeLa sample, recognising it as foreign and malignant, while ignoring the patient's tissue sample.
        In individuals with VS, lymphocytes will exhibit breakdown in cell signalling, targeting both malignant-foreign and healthy-native cells with little discrimination.
        The disorganized response is theorized to stem from abnormal expression of immune checkpoint proteins such as CTLA-4 and PDCD-1 in T lymphocytes, leading to impaired self/non-self recognition.

VARIANTS
  
  Due to the complexity of VS and the multiple genetic factors present, it is unsurprising a number of variants have been identified. These are described below.
    * NEUROLOGICAL (VS-N)
      - In this variant, patients primarily express neurological symptoms (migraines, sensory impairment, epilepsy, strabismus, encephalitis, etc.)
      - These patients are more likely than others to experience neuropsychiatric symptoms. VS-N patients are on average 5x as likely to express symptoms consistent with severe psychiatric disorders (schizophrenia, bipolar, etc) than the general populace.
      - Patients with VS-N are twice as likely to be diagnosed with Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) than the general populace.
      - Patients with VS-N typically suffer language defects, such as stuttering, slurring, aphasia, or selective mutism.
        - Further to the above, VS-N is more common in homozygous expressions of FOXP2 mutations, explaining language defects.
      - VS-N patients on average have milder symptoms outside of the neurological category, though symptoms are usually still present.
      - VS-N patients have a reduced risk of cancer compared to other AVS patients, though still elevated compared to non-AVS individuals.
    * HAEMATOLOGICAL (VS-H)
      - In this variant, patients primarly express haemophilia, even when treated for VS. They also express anemia at near-100% rates, compared to 25% in baseline VS patients.
      - VS-H is more common in homozygous expressions of VWF mutations
    * ENDOCRINE (VS-E)
      - In this variant, patients primarily express endocrinological symptoms (hyperallergy, diabetes mellitus, etc).
      - VS-E patients often suffer from melanin changes, experiencing vitiligo and hyperpigmentation.
      - VS-E patients often experience borderline-immunocompromisation. Seasonal colds present as severe flus. VS-E patients experience 50% greater mortality rates in cases of infectious disease.
      - VS-E patients have significantly elevated risk of developing malignant tumours. VS-E patients are recommended to attend cancer screenings every 3 months, regardless of age. In particular, 1-in-8 VS-E patients will develop Leukemia or non-Hodgkin's Lymphoma at some point in their lives.
      - VS-E is more common in homozygous expressions of CTLA4 mutations.
    * DEVELOPMENTAL (VS-D)
      - In this variant, patients primarily express ocular and neurodevelopmental symptoms.
      - These symptoms are typically absent in other VS variants, as well as standard VS. They include, but are not limited to:
        - Aniridia (absence of the iris)
        - Cataract development
        - Severe visual impairment/blindness
        - Intellectual disability/learning difficulties
        - Motor coordination issues
        - Potential neuropsychiatric symptoms, though less prevalent than in VS-N
      - VS-D is more common in homozygous expressions of PAX6 mutations
    * ORTHOPAEDIC (VS-O)
      - In this variant, patients primarily express orthopaedic symptoms like osteoporosis.
      - VS-O patients are often misdiagnosed with Brittle Bone Disease due to similarity
      VS-O is more common in homozygous expressions of COL1A1 mutations
    * INFLAMMATORY (VS-I)
      - In this variant, patients primarily express inflammatory symptoms.
      - This variant is the most misdiagnosed, often being confused with Lupus.
      - VS-I patients typically develop arthritis early in life due to persistant inflammation.
      - VS-I patients typically suffer from chronic joint pain and fatigue.
      - VS-I patients who are untreated will suffer from skin lesions and photosensitivity.
      - VS-I is more common in homozygous expressions of IRF5 mutations.
    * COMPLEX ACTIVE VOLKOV SYNDROME (VS-X)
      - VS-X is typically found when a patient has more than one homozygous expression of a VS-associated mutation.
      - Their condition will combine multiple VS variants.
      - VS-X is typically the most severe variant. Life expectency without treatment is very poor.
      - Couples where both parents are VS carriers, and especially where the mother is an AVS patient, are advised to undergo preimplantation genetic diagnosis (PGD) to predict risk of VS-X
    * PARTIAL VOLKOV SYNDROME (VS-P)
      - VS-P is the result of a patient having active VS expressions in 1 or more associated genes, but possessing healthy copies of 1 or more.
      - VS-P is even less understood than regular VS. This is because it is typically much less severe than VS, resulting in misdiagnosis.
      - Due to milder pathophysiology, VS-P patients can typically function without regular VS treatment.
      - On average, 1 in 34,000 people have Active VS-P, making it more common than standard AVS. However, it is frequently misdiagnosed.
      - Individuals with homozygous VS-P variants will often express the full symptoms of the associated variant, e.g. an individual with VS-associated FOXP2 mutations but no other VS-associated mutation will have the symptoms associated with VS-N, but not possess the other typical symptoms associated with AVS.

PREVENTION, TREATMENT, & MANAGEMENT

  * Prevention largely revolves around prenatal care of at-risk families. Healthy pregnancies in Inactive VS carriers typically results in asymptomatic, Carrier-type expression. Pregnant patients with AVS are significantly more likely to give birth to children with AVS, with approximately 5-in-7 live births of such individuals presenting AVS.  
  * Immunosuppressant drugs like hydrocortisone should be taken regularly as prescribed. Usage will reduce or eliminate severe symptoms. In fortunate cases, development of disease can be reversed over time.
    *Note:* Immunosuppressants reduce risk of major systemic failure but increase vulnerability to infections. Regular vaccination (non-live) and close infection monitoring are essential.
  * Secondary health conditions (Diabetes mellitus, epilepsy, sensory impairment, etc) should be treated in accordance with typical healthcare guidelines.
    - Diabetes will require insulin treatment, as prescribed by doctor.
        In very rare cases, early intervention and treatment of VS can result in patient only requiring minor insulin treatment combined with Metformin, though this treatment method has not been clinically proven.
        Metformin may be considered only in controlled clinical trials under endocrinologist supervision, and is not recommended for routine treatment of VS-associated diabetes.
    - Epilepsy can typically be treated by anticonvulsant medication. Severe epilepsy should be dealt with separately to VS by experienced neurologists.
    - Anemia presents in roughly 1-in-4 cases, and can be treated with iron supplements.
    - Asthma is usually treatable with Salbutamol inhalation.
    - Sensory impairment can typically be treated with QoL improvements and sensory aids, like eyewear and hearing aids. Early intervention and treatment of newborns with VS will typically prevent severe or total sensory loss. Untreated patients commonly experience more severe sensory impairment, and will require more bespoke treatment.
    - Joint pain and chronic migraines can be treated with NSAIDs or analgesics like Paracetamol, as well as QoL improvements and healthy living.
    - Fatigue is common, and patients will require regular rest and healthy sleep.
    - Allergic reactions are rare in well-treated patients, however patients are advised to carry an Epinephrine autoinjector for emergencies.
    - Even when treated, patients will require regular cancer screenings due to elevated risks. In patients aged 0-25, screenings are recommended every 6 months. Patients aged 25-45 will require annual screenings. Patients aged 45 and up will again be advised to receive screenings every 6 months.

    For those with advanced symptoms, immediate application of hydrocortisone doses are recommended. Treatment of comorbid disorders should be applied normally. Currently, there are no known interferences caused by VS on these treatments.

  Carriers are typically unaware.
  For congenital onset, both parents must be carriers and fetal development must involve significant in-womb stressors.
  Preimplantation genetic diagnosis (PGD) is available for at-risk families.
