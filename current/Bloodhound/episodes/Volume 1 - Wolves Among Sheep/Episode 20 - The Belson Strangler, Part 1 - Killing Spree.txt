SCENE - Old Belson Strangler

	Her eyes were still open. That was the worst part.
	It was the middle of December, 2005. The sun was setting after a short day.
	Police Constable Henry Naismith - young, fresh-faced, and inquisitive - looked at the scene and grimaced.
	A woman's body, with a chalk outline. Her neck had been snapped. Bile had pooled at the back of her throat.
	The flash of an FI's camera shook him from his stupor. He pulled out his notebook, and began writing.
	"Naismith." his superior, DCS Dorsey, nodded. "Alright, give it to me."
	"Cause of death is the broken neck." he began unsurely.
	"Yeah, no shit." Dorsey grinned. "Come on. You want to make Detective, you've gotta start spotting things deeper than that."
	"Right, sir." Naismith nodded. "Um... trauma to the fingers - the tips have been removed. Judging from the lack of blood spatter, I'd say post mortem."
	"And what does that tell us?"
	"That... she managed to scratch the killer in the struggle, and they removed the fingertips to avoid leaving any DNA behind?"
	"Good." Dorsey nodded. "Anything else?"
	Naismith looked down, deep in thought. "I... can't see anything else, sir."
	"Look at the pock marks." Dorsey grinned. "She was a heroin addict."
	"I... I don't see how that's relevant."
	"Don't you?" Dorsey scoffed. "Clearly, she was selling herself to pay for her habit. Tragic, I know."
	"I'm... I'm not sure that's relevant either, sir." Naismith said, trying to hide his scowl.
	"Right... well then, that's why I'm DCS, and you're PC." Dorsey chuckled. "We'll leave it with Vice."
	Naismith looked away, enraged. He looked down at the dead girl, and faltered.
	In that moment, he felt like he could be sick.
	He wanted justice.

	Naismith sighed as he approached the desk sergeant in the Vice office.
	"Afternoon, mate." he nodded. "I need to speak with a superintendent Chapman?"
	"Ah." the sergeant winced. "He's on personal leave. I'm surprised you haven't heard."
	"Why's that?" Naismith tilted his head.
	"You're on Murder Squad, right? His missus got stabbed to death by some junkie last week."
	"Jesus..." Naismith shuddered. "Well... who's your acting super?"
	"Come this way." the sergeant nodded.

	That night, he absent-mindedly twirled his fork in his food.
	"If my cooking is that bad, all you've gotta do is say, babe." a woman grinned.
	"Hm? Oh, sorry love..." he sighed.
	"Hey..." she began softly. "Is it work?"
	He sighed again, looking off into the distance.
	"You know... maybe I should've been a firefighter instead." he mused.
	"What do you mean?" she asked, sitting next to him.
	"I dunno... no one ever looks at a burning house and goes 'it was asking for it,' I guess."
	The woman smiled softly, and pulled his head to her shoulder.
	He took a deep breath, and nuzzled in.

SCENE - First Body Found/Flight of the Werewolves

	"Ugh... dead tonight, eh?" Lydia chuckled.
	Maryam grinned in response, pulling the hem of her faux-fur coat and letting out a chilled breath.
	"Oh, here's one." she pointed. A car pulled up beside the ladies, and the window rolled down.
	"Evening, ladies." the man in the driver's seat began. "Working tonight?"
	"Depends who's asking." Lydia shrugged.
	The man pulled two £50 notes out of his wallet, and showed them.
	Lydia smiled, and walked around to the front seat.
	"Nowhere public, alright?" the man said.
	"Of course. There's a free car park just down the road. No one will be there this time of night."
	"Good." the man said lowly, almost like a growl.

	The car pulled into its space, and the engine switched off.
	"So..." she began, almost exasperated. "How do you want to start?"
	The man chuckled mirthlessly. "You're a bad girl, aren't you?"
	"I can be a bad girl, if you want." she shrugged halfheartedly.
	The man's lip curled, and he reached out, putting his hands on the sides of her neck.
	"Whoa... let's take it slow, baby?" she said, grabbing his hands.
	"Shut up. SHUT UP!" he snapped, as if a switch had been flipped. He gripped onto her neck, and squeezed.
	Lydia struggled against his iron grip, but it wouldn't budge. She frantically clawed out with her hands, cutting him across the face. He roared, and intensified his grip.
	She choked and spat, as things went blurry. She just barely managed to make it to her bag, and - oh thank god. There it was.
	She pulled out the bottle, and sprayed. Right into his eyes.
	The man screamed as the pepper burned at his face, and Lydia used the moment to break free. She fell out of the car, choking and spluttering.
	"HELP!" she screamed hoarsely. "THERE'S A PSYCHO! HE'S GONNA--"
	Before she could finish, she felt something hard knock onto the back of her head. It didn't hurt - not at first. Then she felt it again, and again, and again.
	Before the agony could even register, his hands were back upon her throat, and the world faded to black.

	It was a cold evening in Drackhill. If you were DI Naismith, it was, perhaps, a little colder.
	He looked on at the sight - a young woman laid sprawled on the ground, her eyes bloodshot and bulging. Her neck sat at a sickening angle, and was badly bruised.
	"She was found an hour ago by a late-night jogger." Nina began. "We're working on finding an ID, but... well, the mayor's push to crack down on sex work means she probably wasn't carrying one."
	"Anything on CCTV?"
	"No, sir." she shook her head. "We've got some images - enough to identify the suspect as male, of average height and build. But he knew to avoid showing his face at all times."
	"Damn..." he muttered. "Poor girl."
	He walked a little closer, getting a better look at the body.
	"Fuck... not again." he whispered.
	"Sir?"
	"Her fingertips." he pointed.
	"She must've got a handful of him." Nina sighed, looking at the carved stumps.
	"That implies our killer knows a thing or two about forensics." Naismith mused.
	"You said 'not again?'" she asked.
	"Ten years ago. Very similar case." Naismith sighed. "String of stranglings all over. Never found the killer."
	"You think it's the same guy?"
	He rubbed his temples.
	"I don't believe in coincidences. Not anymore."
	Nina walked over, and sighed.
	"It's... horrible. She must've been so scared." she lamented.
	"There aren't many worse ways to go..." he shook his head in disgust. "We'll get her to the pathologists, see if..."
	"If?"
	"If we can find any semen." he shuddered. "Oh, God... I hate this fucking job."
	"We'll catch the bastard, sir." Nina nodded firmly. "And we'll bring him to justice."
	"Yeah... that we will." Naismith sighed. "Just... how is it I can look at people with their heads caved in by that monster and stay detached, but when it's this..."
	"It's the mundanity, I suppose." Nina shrugged. "When two different breeds of mutant are tearing chunks out of each other, it's easier to feel like a mouse looking up at two cats fighting. This is... more human, I guess."
	Naismith gave her a sad look. "I suppose, as your first normal murder case, it must be hitting you pretty hard too, eh?"
	Nina looked away. "I... I hope I feel this way every time, sir. Just like you."
	Naismith smiled softly. "You're a good cop, Nina. Not a lot of those."
	"I've got a pretty good mentor." she chuckled.
	"Sir!" an FI called over.
	Naismith walked to him.
	"We found this." the FI nodded, passing over a note.
	"WEREWOLF." it began. "LOOK AT WHAT I DID. DO YOU LIKE IT?"
	Naismith shook his head. "Jesus..."

	Atop Rydon Tower, the two Werewolves perched, overlooking their city.
	"Alright, I'll take Belson." Connor began. "You alright on Caldermouth? I've heard there's been some muggings lately, so it'd be good if we could cover both."
	"Sounds fun!" Sparky barked happily. "I like doing this."
	"Feels like a ritual." Connor chuckled. "Two Werewolves climb atop the tower, and plan their nightly hunt."
	"Well, maybe it should be a ritual?" Sparky tilted his head. "If we were normal wolves, we'd bring back our kills that night too. Obviously what we do is a little different - but maybe we should meet back here and report?"
	"That's... that's a really good idea, bud." Connor smiled. "It would make the night feel more... organised, I guess."
	Sparky nodded. "Right, well... I guess it's time to swing down."
	"Hang on..." Connor motioned, leaning forwards a little. He closed his eyes, then opened them, red - his super-senses honing in.
	"What are you doing?" Sparky whispered.
	"Trying to watch the wind..." Connor shrugged. "When I was fighting Whiplash last week. I noticed the way she moved - she used her powers to almost walk on the air."
	"But you don't have her powers?" Sparky tilted his head. "It'd be cool if you did, but..."
	"No... but maybe I don't need them." Connor pondered. "Yesterday, before I rescued Clara, I made up this wingsuit membrane to help me turn in the air. There might be a way to improve on that concept..."
	His suit rippled and morphed, and long struts formed out from his wrists - almost as long as his arms. The material around them hardened, giving them the rigidity of bone.
	Thick gel flowed from his shoulders and spread along his arms, stretching into a dark membrane. It widened behind him, reaching down along his sides and across his back before tapering towards his hips. The membrane pulled taut as Connor raised his arms, forming two broad, angular wings. Beneath, his legs moved freely, and he bent his knees in anticipation.
	"What's that?" Sparky asked, amazed.
	"Hopefully, a glider." Connor mused, rising. "Well... there's only one way to test it."
	Sparky smiled in anticipation, as Connor leaned forward. He stiffened the membrane, letting it catch the updrafts coming from the coast into the city. He spread his arms, and flexed them, watching as the wings moved with him.
	Finally, he let himself fall.
	At first, it felt like he was about to splat into the pavement below. Mentally, he prepared to use his grappling hooks just in case - but instead, the descent stabilised, and he soared forwards. His legs dangled, before he straightened them, and spread them slightly.
	"Holy... HOLY SHIT!" he screamed in excitement. "WOOOO!"
	He turned his head, and saw Sparky sailing in the air behind him. He grinned wildly, and caught another upwards wind, flying through the night sky.
	"THIS IS SO COOL!" Sparky howled, his fur being buffeted by the wind.
	Connor laughed giddily, looking down at the city below. His senses were distracted somewhat by the winds, but he could still use them. In mere moments, he'd covered several street blocks.
	He felt his tactile senses tingle, and he looked back up.
	"Oh, shit..." he gasped, bracing for impact.

	The evening was going exactly as planned.
	The glittering of chandeliers lit up the massive, high-rise ballroom. A quartet played near the balcony doors, while a swarm of tuxedos and gowns mingled. In darker corners, high-stakes games of politics and commerce were being played; while an old man in his finest suit tapped his spoon on his champagne glass.
	"If I could have your attention, ladies and gentlemen?" he began. "I'd just like to thank you all for coming this evening. With your continued support, the Maxwell Society has continued to promote the development of open markets, lower taxes, family values, and economic freedom."
	"And plenty of subsidised champagne!" one other man cheered.
	The attendees roared with laughter, and raised their glasses.
	"Well, then! To Maxwell! To free markets! And to--OH MY GOD!"
	People craned their necks, and then ducked out of the way, as a hulking black mass smashed through the expensive glass wall. Shards exploded across the marble floor as Connor skidded across the tiles and rolled to a stop, smashing into a table. His membranous glider morphed back into him, as he rose, arching his back and feeling it click.
	Some screamed. Some fainted. Others simply stood, like deer in headlights.
	"Ugh... practice makes perfect, I suppose." he shrugged. "Ooh, food."
	His mask rippled slightly, pulling back to expose his mouth. He grabbed a shrimp canape from a stunned server's tray, and shoved it in.
	It crunched. He froze. Then, he spat it out, and retched.
	"That is fucking grim. How do you people eat that?" he asked, horrified. "Ew..."
	He grabbed a champagne flute and took a hurried sip.
	A second later, he spat that out too.
	"Okay... at least now I know I was never missing much."
	At that, Sparky glided in, landing gracefully.
	"That's how you're supposed to land." he grinned cheekily.
	"Shut up." Connor snarked back. "Rich people are fucking weird."
	Sparky trotted over to a nearby table, and began eating pastries.
	"These are nice!" he smiled between mouthfuls.
	"Aha!" Connor grinned. "More of those next time, please?"
	"SECURITY?!" the old man screamed at last.
	"Ah, our cue." Connor chuckled. He rushed to the balcony, and leapt off from it. Stretching out, he rode the winds away.
	"Sorry about the mess!" Sparky barked happily, before likewise gliding away.
	The great and the good looked on in horror.
	"What the hell was that?"
	"What's going on?"
	"Are we being attacked?"
	Their thoughts were interrupted by a weak clapping.
	A very old man in a wheelchair was laughing hysterically.
	"Oh, the entertainment gets better every year!" he cheered.

SCENE - Daycare/Hookman Homage

	The morning sun tried its best to poke through the sky. Instead, it merely turned some of the grey clouds from dark to light. Amy, however, was undeterred. She stood outside a mid-sized unit labelled 'Skye Road Pawz' - the local rescue and dog daycare centre. She took a deep breath, and entered.
	"Hiya!" she began. "I'm here about the trial shift? My name's Amy Zhao."
	"Excellent timing." the tired older woman at the front desk nodded, putting on a jacket. "My name's Alison. We've just had a call about an abandoned dog by the creek. I know it's a little manic, but fancy tagging along?"
	"I--Absolutely!" Amy smiled, surprised.

	The poor thing was terrified.
	She barked savagely at any passers by. Thankfully for them, but frighteningly for her, she was secured to a post by a metal chain and an inward-spiked collar that pushed into her neck. Her body was covered in scars, and one ear had been ripped off, along with an eye gouged out. The eyelid was crudely stitched shut.
	Amy and Alison arrived in their van. Alison hopped out with a catchpole, while Amy looked at the dog in sorrow.
	"Poor thing..." she said.
	"I know. Oh god, look at those scars." Alison agreed. "She's been badly treated, no question."
	"There's no way she'll let us approach with the catchpole. It probably reminds her of god-knows-what." Amy noted sombrely.
	"Good point. I'll get the tranq gun." Alison began, but Amy raised her hand.
	"Hang on... can I try and calm her down?"
	"Be careful. You're not insured yet." Alison warned, while nodding.
	Amy approached gingerly. The dog roared and screamed almost rabidly, but she was undeterred.
	The dog carried on its terrified roars. Amy responded by going prone, then rolling onto her back. She gently edged up to the poor thing, backing away at each bark intentionally. Slowly, the dog's barks became more subdued. Finally, she cautiously held her hand out, just close enough for a sniff. The old girl sniffed, then backed away towards the pole. Amy crawled over, and unfastened the collar. The dog shook her head, letting her one ear slap across her cheek, before stretching. Amy stroked her back gently.
	Alison approached similarly slowly, letting the girl sniff her. She revealed some treats, which the dog greedily devoured. The pair coaxed her into the van, and Alison hopped into the front. Amy stayed in the back, comforting the dog.
	"You're hired." Alison nodded. Amy tried and failed to subdue her glee.

	Chelsea giggled as the car pulled in. Harry didn't wait before his lips were upon hers.
	"You sure no one will see us?" she asked nervously.
	"Babe, I'm sure." he chuckled, moving down to her neck.
	Her vocal chords flexed as if she was going to make a token protest, but instead, she clutched his head and pulled it closer. He grinned.
	She reached down, and gripped. "Oh, my god... it's so big--"
	"Uh..." he blushed. "That's the handbrake."
	Both giggled gleefully.
	"What do you say we take this to the back seats?" he winked.
	"Oh, and they say chivalry is dead." she laughed, crawling through with him. They'd barely got through the gap before they were upon each other again. The radio continued playing some pop music - specifically, Harry had chosen her favourite.
	He leaned back as she laid down, and pulled off his shirt. She ran her hands over his chest, and before long, it began.
	Chelsea gasped for air, her body shaking. She opened her eyes, and shrieked.
	"Ah! Who's that?!" she pointed, pale as a sheet.
	Harry turned his head, to see a blinding flashlight shining in.
	"Fuck off, you perv!" he snapped.
	"Shit, is it my dad?" Chelsea asked, her breathing quickening.
	Harry quickly pulled up his trousers, and opened the door. The light made the figure holding it look like a shadow.
	"Oi! Who the fuck are you?!" he barked.
	The figure responded by smashing the flashlight around the side of Harry's head.
	As the metal made contact with his temple, he stumbled down to the ground. Pain radiated through his skull, and he fought as hard as he could to remain conscious.
	Chelsea screamed, jumping out of the car and rushing to him.
	"Please, don't hurt us! Just take what you want and go!" she begged.
	"Oh..." the man growled. "I'm afraid those two things are the same."
	He struck her aside the head as hard as he could. She fell down, her eyes rolling backwards.
	At that, Harry rose in fury. He tackled their assailant, and tried to force him to the ground.
	Before things could go any further, he felt something cold sink into his gut.
	"HOW DARE YOU TOUCH ME! HOW *DARE* YOU!" the man screamed. He stabbed him again. And again. And again.
	Then, a slash ripped Harry's throat open. He fell to the ground, fighting, begging for life.
	As the world slipped away, all he could do was watch, as his murderer wrapped his hands around Chelsea's neck.
	He gurgled in protest, and then... nothing.
