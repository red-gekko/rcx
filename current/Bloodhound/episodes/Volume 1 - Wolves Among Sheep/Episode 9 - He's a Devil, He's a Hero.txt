SCENE - Meditating

	As the morning sun rose over the valley, it gave only a sliver of light to the people below - for dark, rainy clouds had gathered, as if in jest.
	Despite the damp, Connor sat atop his block of flats. Legs crossed, eyes closed - almost meditating. Black veins raised over his eyelids and down to the top of his cheeks.
	He took a deep breath, and felt his vocal chords move and twist in ways unnatural to a human. Experimenting, he let out a noise.
	It sounded... strange. Not unpleasant, but he could feel the noise wrap around his body and float across the rooftop. The sound went through his ears, but it was almost like he could see it. He 'looked' down into the flats beneath, seeing the people below going about their morning routines.
	Echolocation. He chuckled, and turned it off.
	"That'll come in handy." he muttered to himself. He then twitched his eyes, and opened them, before looking around. The inky blackness on his veins drained rapidly, and sheathed beneath his skin.
	"You okay?" Sparky asked, plodding over. "That was a funny noise."
	"Yeah. Just experimenting with our super-senses. So far, I've found echolocation, thermovision, and if the fact that I can spot people's phones and computers is any indication, we can also detect electromagnetic fields."
	Sparky tilted his head, relying on their telepathic link to figure out what he meant. "So, we can see with our ears?"
	"Kinda! Try it. Use your throat." Connor nodded encouragingly.
	Sparky shuffled, then threw his head back.
	"Awoooo!" he cheered, letting out a howl that echoed through the city. Connor giggled, and stroked his packmate affectionately.
	"Did I do good?!" Sparky yapped excitedly.
	"Hah, yeah, not bad at all." Connor grinned, before demonstrating the effect again. This time, Sparky replicated it flawlessly.
	"Nice one, bud!" Connor smiled warmly. "Now, put your super-vision on, and look downwards."
	Sparky did so, and they both could see humming auras throughout the complex.
	"Phones. TVs. Computers." Connor pointed them out. "Electromagnetic vision. I can also see us, too - our natural bioelectric fields."
	"That's what those things are? Cool!" Sparky yipped, licking his packmate.
	"That also explains the pigeon-sense." Connor chuckled.
	"What, we can detect pigeons?" Sparky tilted his head.
	Connor laughed. "That too. But have you ever noticed, when we're out, we know exactly how to get home? Pigeons can do the same thing by detecting the Earth's magnetic fields. That's why flying over to Algeria and back felt so weird."
	Sparky nodded, enjoying the lecture.
	Connor looked out at the rising sun, and saw a strange violet halo appear around it. He squinted, and realised it wasn't quite violet at all - but there were no words beyond "strange violet."
	"Ultraviolet vision, too." he whispered, awestruck.
	"Hm?" Sparky tilted his head.
	"I can see colours I couldn't before. I can't see much ultraviolet, or infrared for that matter - but there's gotta be *some* benefit. Oh, that reminds me!" he gasped, before getting a colour chart on his phone. "How many colours can you see?"
	Sparky looked at it, and shrugged. "16?"
	"Yeah, same. Just... dogs can't see as many colours as humans. I thought, if I can see infrared and ultraviolet, even if only a little, then surely you can see the same colours as humans."
	Sparky nodded. "Oh, you mean the weird violet and red thingies when I focus my vision!"
	"Yeah, that's the one." Connor chuckled. He pulled the 200-dinar souvenir from his pocket, held it in the air, and dropped it out of his hand.
	Immediately, his eyes flashed red again, as the black veins returned. Time slowed down to a crawl, as he watched the coin drop down at no more than a twentieth of the speed.
	"Coooooooonnnnnnoooooooorrrrr." Sparky said, comically extended by the power. Connor tried to chuckle, but breathing in felt like a strain, as two lungs crawled open, and another two squeezed shut. His heartbeats were seemingly minutes apart. Finally, he let time resume.
	"Jesus..." he muttered. "Well, that'll be nice in a firefight, for sure."
	"What is?"
	"Super-reflexes." he sighed nonchalantly.
	He leaned back, and let the sun begin to shine over him. He yawned.
	"You're tired again?"
	"Nah." he shrugged. "Just... using the super-senses. It wears me out."
	"I get that too." Sparky nodded. "It makes the headaches worse, as well."
	"Same with the armour I used in Algeria. I guess actively triggering these powers is a little draining."
	"You say that as if running and punching isn't draining." Sparky noted.
	"That's... a very good point." Connor nodded. "Is it really a superpower if it's part of our biology? Hell, what even *is* our biology anymore?"
	Sparky looked off into the distance. "There's still so many questions out there. Why can we talk? Why can't other mutants hear me? What made us different? What's in those syringes?"
	"And who the fuck are these people?" Connor added. "And why did they kidnap me? Not to mention, why do other Super-Agents have those weird black veins on their eyes all the time, but we only get those when we use our powers? Why don't their eyes turn red?"
	Sparky nodded, and stretched himself. "Anyway... that can wait. I'm bored. Can we do something?"
	"Well, we have no plans until later. Ivan texted me a place to hit this evening, and I need you to do some scouting while I'm out. And the first payment from Hattab just came in, so to pass the time... wanna go shopping?"
	"Shopping? What's that like?"
	"We'll go to a pet store. If you see anything you like, we'll buy it. It's high time you got spoiled." Connor grinned.
	Sparky yapped excitedly, and the two descended to the ground.

	Across town, in an expensive house in Caldermouth, an older man sat at a desk. He had thin, wiry hair that greyed at the temples and receded at the corners. He took a deep breath, and made a phone call.
	"Good evening, Commandant." he began.
	"Report, Dorsey." the Commandant said gruffly.
	"The Council's put pressure on me to find a certain vigilante killer - the traitor." Dorsey said, a hint of nerves in his voice.
	"How much do they know?"
	"We managed to keep the worst out of the press - they don't know the worst of it. Our... 'associate,' Dr. Vacher, has assigned himself as the fire crew's therapist. He's going to gaslight them - make them think they didn't see anything."
	"Good. How about the other coverups?"
	"We've explained the car incident as a regular crash - and kept the fact that the men were armed quiet. As for the other deaths, we're applying the Terrorism Act to keep the media quiet."
	"How much do the police know?" the Commandant pressed. "Do they know anything about our organisation?"
	"No, sir. But... the detective on the case, DI Naismith, he... he's investigating the paranormal elements. The incident with Dobel; the death of Tomlinson; the episode in the church..."
	"Has Dobel talked?"
	"No, sir. We believe he was mutilated by Vincent Barrett - a former gang leader - for trying to kill his wards. That, combined with his knowledge of what happens to traitors... I doubt Dobel will talk."
	The Commandant chuckled. "Good. One less person who we need to kill, at least. Now... how much does your detective know about the recent xenohuman activity?"
	"More than I'd be comfortable with." Dorsey began awkwardly. "He saw Pierce, as well as the dead Super-Agent operatives; not to mention the messes that the traitor has left; he knows that no man could do that."
	"Having Naismith eliminated would bring increased scrutiny." the Commandant sighed. "How extensive are your protocols?"
	"Very, sir. I think I can keep the inquiry under wraps."
	"How?"
	"Well, we've been seen to assign officers from Serious Crime onto the case. I've assigned a has-been from the department - DI Henry Naismith - as well as a rookie, PC Chowdhury. I'll reduce all resources available to them. As long as I keep pestering them for a result, the politicians can tick their boxes and this whole thing will be ignored."
	"And if the 'rookie and the has-been' end up finding something?"
	"Then I'll take action."
	"You'd better." the Commandant growled, before hanging up.

	The shop bell rang as the twin Werewolves sauntered in. Sparky looked around in awe.
	"Oh wow! This place is amazing! So many smells, and food, and plenty of other animals..." he beamed.
	"Haha... let's go have a look, bud!" Connor smiled warmly. Their first point of call was the reptile aisles.
	"Wow..." Sparky gasped, making direct eye contact with a ball python for sale. "Weird!"
	Connor smirked, and joined in the looking. The python moved its head rapidly, trying to get a good look at the pair.
	"Ah... pythons see heat, like we can. And our temperatures are pretty high, so maybe we're freaking him out." Connor whispered softly.
	Sparky shrugged, before activating his own thermovision.
	"He's freezing cold in there!" he gasped. "We need to help him warm up!"
	"No, it's fine!" Connor said quickly, before catching the look of a confused fellow shopper. He awkwardly herded Sparky away, before kneeling down next to him.
	Sparky's glowing red eyes faded to their normal yellow. "Doesn't he need our help?"
	"Snakes and other reptiles are cold-blooded." Connor whispered. "So their body temperature should be the same as the room they're in. Also... it's for the best if you don't use super-vision in front of other people. Is that alright?"
	"Oh, okay!" Sparky barked affirmatively, before the pair walked onwards to the rodents and rabbits.
	"Not food." Connor whispered sharply.
	"I know." Sparky grinned. He sat down, and looked at the rabbit pens, smiling giddily. As they hopped around, he laughed like a child. Connor couldn't help but crack a big smile too.

SCENE - Hog and Hounds - Shotgun Scene

	A white van pulled up in a pub car park. The rain spattered down onto the city, providing an ambient noise as the van door slid open. Out hopped a group of men, with shaved heads and tell-tale leathers identifying them as Stoker's former goons. They sauntered into the pub - the Hog and Hounds.
	"Pints, please." their leader - a large, burly man with a protruding gut - barked at the barman. He hurriedly poured out their drinks. The men leant against the bar, their eyes darting across the room.
	"Oi." one snarled. He was a young, skinny man, whose attitude implied he thought he was a hardened thug. He pointed at a young man - one who had committed the apparently unforgivable sin of "being black in public."
	"What's that doing in here?" the leader snapped. "The Skinners'll be here soon. You trying to piss us off?"
	"N-no." the barman stammered. "I... I didn't see him."
	"I--I'll go." the young man said, rising.
	"Too right you will." the skinny one grinned as he left. "Boss, you want me to teach him a lesson first?"
	"Leave him, Colin." the leader shrugged. "Rest of you lot - out."
	The other patrons left - some muttering discontent, others going pale in fear. Either way, the pub cleared out. The barman headed into the basement, ostensibly to "check on the barrels."

	As the people filtered out, their heartbeats were heard by something - two somethings, in fact - perched on a roof overlooking the pub.
	Connor and Sparky's eyes glowed an eerie blood-red, scanning the site. Their augmented vision showed the world as an utterly alien picture of sound, smell, sight, thermals, electricity, and more.
	"So... 15 of them, total - is that right?"
	"Yes, well done! You're getting better - it's all about trusting your first instinct." Sparky replied, smiling. "Your senses usually get it right the first time - don't overthink it."
	"And... the older, fat one - he's been drinking, hasn't he?"
	"Yes! He smells of it. You're getting better at sniffing things out!" Sparky said proudly.
	"Well, super-senses have to have *some* advantages."
	"Wait 'til you sniff Chloe's butt. You'll never be happier." Sparky innocently smiled.
	"I... I appreciate your flirting tips, but I think I'll stick to human interactions." Connor replied, smirking.
	"I'll let you know when she's in heat if you like!"
	"Yeah... I don't think humans go into heat."
	"Oh. You guys are *weird!*"
	Connor chuckled. "Anyway, you'd better go."
	"Where are we meeting tonight?"
	"Back at the flat. Amy's working in the tattoo place again tonight, so we don't have to sneak around as much." Connor nodded.
	"Okay. See you!" Sparky yipped. "Oh, and... don't try the smoke bomb trick again."
	"Hah, hah." Connor rolled his eyes. "The problem wasn't the *idea,* just the execution. I didn't use enough biomass last time."
	"Don't say I didn't warn you." Sparky grinned, before he leapt from rooftop to rooftop to begin his patrol.
	Connor rose, and rolled back his shoulders. He felt his red hoodie shudder and shift into the black kevlar suit.
	He dropped from the second floor, landing onto the concrete below with a thud. His legs bent slightly with the impact, but he didn't flinch. He was getting better at ignoring what would kill a human.
	He sauntered in casually through the doors.

	"Oi. Private party, cunt." one barked.
	Another grinned. "You know Halloween is next week, right? Still... nice ninja costume."
	Connor chuckled. "Cheers. I think it could use some more red, though."
	A hulking man walked towards him, drawing a switchblade. "I can help with that." he growled.
	Connor's fist shot out like a bullet, smearing the man's nose across his face. He threw him to the ground, and looked at his knuckles.
	"Thanks! It looks better already." he quipped.
	"GET HIM!" the thugs roared.

	The first man swung a knife toward him. Connor twisted backwards to dodge, then stepping into the strike and driving his fist into the man's gut, winding him. The man stumbled back, gasping for air. Connor didn't wait for him to recover; he swung a brutal right hook to his chin, sending him crashing to the ground.
	A kick exploded from him, sending one man flying back into his comrades. He dashed in, lifting one by the chest and slamming him into a tables.
	Without breaking his rhythm, Connor grabbed the third man by the leg, lifting him into the air like a ragdoll and launching him at the fourth. The collision knocked both men to the ground, and Connor moved forward, faster than they could react.
	"DIE, CUNT!" another screamed, smashing a barstool over the back of Connor's head. He fell forwards, catching himself on his hands, before spinning around like a breakdancer. His legs swept his assailant's, throwing him aside. As the hapless thug was mid-air, a hammer strike from Connor landed into his abdomen, sending him diving downwards onto the ground. He made a pathetic groaning noise as the wind shot out of his lungs.
	A familiar grin cracked under his mask.
	"Now you see me..." he quipped - an iota less lamely than before - and threw down a globule of gel.
	It burst... splattering black goo across the room.
	"Damn it..." Connor thought. "Too much biomass."
	"Ew! What is that shit?!" one grimaced, before charging.
	Connor sighed, and grabbed the man's baseball bat, ripping it out of his hands with ease. With a single, fluid motion, he spun around. The tip of the baseball bat struck right into the man's temple, and his eyes rolled back as he collapsed. Then he snapped it in half on his knee, and dropped it.
	"Uh... sorry I gooped on you." he said awkwardly, before sending a punch to another's gut.
	Before he could carry on concussing, he heard the unmistakable sound of a shotgun being cocked.
	*CLICK-CLACK! BANG!*
	The pellets tore into Connor's abdomen. He staggered back, pain flashing through him as his body slammed into the wall behind.
	He gritted his teeth, pushing through the pain as he slowly rose to his feet. But before he could fully recover, he heard another *BANG!*
	This time, the shotgun was aimed directly at his face. The blast ripped through his features, and Connor's seemingly lifeless body crumpled to the ground.
	The gunman smirked. He turned to his remaining men, barking orders.
	"Clean up this fucking mess! Last thing we need is looking like a bunch of pussies when the Skinners get here!"
	One of the goons, grinning, stared at Connor's body. "Would a bunch of pussies kill that little wanker?"
	Another voice chimed in, panicked. "Fuck that - we need to get out. The cops will have been called by now - you fucking shot him!"
	"It was self-defence." the gunman growled. "The little fucker tried to start shit, and we retaliated."
	He paused, glancing down at Connor's motionless form. "You know, this is the wanker that's been giving the Skinners trouble. But you know what, cunt? You don't look so tough."
	Just as he leant in to gloat, Connor's eyes snapped open, glowing with an unnatural red hue. Where the prior glow was measured and studious, this one was feral. Demonic. Like they were on fire.
	He'd gotten used to the urges, to an extent. The Rage. But now... it was like a hurricane of fury.
	Without warning, Connor's hand shot out, grabbing the gunman by the throat with terrifying strength. The man choked, his eyes bulging in panic as Connor effortlessly slammed him to the ground.
	The gun clattered away, but Connor grabbed it with one hand, holding it by the barrel, and began to beat the gunman's head into the floor with savage force. The sickening sound of bone cracking filled the room.
	The other men, now trembling in fear, screamed and scattered like rats, rushing out of the pub as fast as they could.
	*SMACK! SMACK! SMACK!* the barrel went, each hit more wet than the last.
	Connor's breath came in heavy, ragged gasps. Finally, he stopped.
	A choke.
	Connor's hand was slick with blood, and his hearts were still pounding with adrenaline. His eyes slowly faded from glowing red to their normal steel-grey colour as he regained some semblance of control.
	The man below clinged to life, almost admirably. His jaw was a thing of the past - obliterated. His tongue wriggled in the mess, trying and failing to let more breath in through the throat.
	Connor's body shook involuntarily, and he stumbled back, retching violently. The bile rose in his throat, and he vomited on the floor.
	He took a deep breath, steadying himself. Reaching up, he morphed his mask back into place, covering his face, and then stood, a little unsteady, before walking to the man.
	"Hold still." he ordered, morphing a tiny, pinpoint blade. His eyes shimmered as he made a careful incision.
	Turns out, tracheotomies are easy enough if you can see every vein in a man's neck.
	The next breath was better. Not good, but better. Connor rose, and shuffled over to the bar.
	He poured himself a pint, his hands still trembling slightly. He wiped the blood from his palms with a rag, his eyes briefly flickering toward the heaving body of the gunman.
	He shook his head, trying to banish the thoughts. But they stuck, clinging to him like a second skin.
	"Am I becoming one of them?"
	Connor shuddered, pulling up his mask to expose his mouth, and taking a long drink from his pint. The bitter taste did little to wash away the gnawing sense of guilt.
	Suddenly, the door crashed open. Police officers stormed into the pub, assault rifles raised.
	"For fuck's sake!" Connor growled under his breath, tossing the pint aside and diving out the window in one fluid motion. Glass shattered as he leapt, just as bullets tore through the air, filling the space he had just occupied.
	He landed in the alleyway below, but the officers were quick to follow. Connor sprinted, instinctively grabbing onto a nearby wall. His fingers latched onto it, and for a moment, he just stood there, eyes wide in realization.
	He looked down at his gloved hands. The costume had morphed a special adhesive, allowing him to scale the wall with ease. Without a second thought, he began to climb, moving up the building like a moth.
	As he reached the top, he took one last glance down, ensuring no one was close. He caught his breath, before sprinting across the rooftop, disappearing into the night.

	As Connor scanned the wreckage, his mind raced. The break looked violent - concrete torn open, steel rebar jutting out like exposed bone. Could it be linked to the conspiracy? He studied the fractured supports for a moment longer, searching for signs of an explosive force. He shook his head. Too many coincidences. And right now, speculation didn't matter. People were still trapped.
	"As you can see, Liz, a section of the Drackhill flyover has collapsed during the lunchtime rush. Emergency services are attempting to reach several motorists believed to be trapped beneath the fallen carriageway. At present, there's no official explanation for what caused the failur--oh my god!" the reporter gasped, as Connor vaulted over the cordon.
	"Hey, wait! You can't--" a fireman meekly protested, before going pale as Connor gripped the twisted frame of a crushed hatchback. With a sharp pull, metal screamed in protest. The door tore free in his hands.
	"Oh my God, thank you!" the man in the driver's seat exclaimed.
	"No worries." Connor nodded, smirking beneath his mask.
	"A masked man - the same one seen at the Mason Court fire, by the looks of it - is assisting rescue efforts..."
	As he spoke into the camera, Connor moved over to a second vehicle. The driver had managed to crawl free, but the passenger was stuck. A worrying trail of blood had run down from her hairline to her temple, but mercifully, her chest still heaved. Connor grabbed the door and ripped it off with his hand, before a morphed knife cut through the seatbelt, and he hoisted her free, handing her over to the paramedic team.
	"Mum! Please, someone help me!" a child's voice cried out. Connor pounced forward.
	There, a group of firemen struggled with a slab of fallen concrete pinning down a woman, who sobbed in pain. Her son pawed at the rubble, trying desperately to help as rock cut into his palms.
	"Move! Let me!" Connor barked, before crouching down, and wedging his fingers beneath the cracked edge. Muscles tightening, he lifted. The slab rose just enough for them to drag her clear, and a duo of paramedics arrived with a stretcher.
	"Hey... thanks." one of the firemen nodded, catching his breath.
	"No need." Connor shook his head. His hearts skipped their beats as he recognised the face, and his consciousness flashed backwards to his horrific reanimation.
	For a second, or an eon - trauma will do that to you - he was back in the Blackmarsh rubble, after the monsters tried to bury him away. And he was lifting this fireman up by the neck, consumed by pain.
	"Uh... you okay?" the fireman asked, nervously rubbing his helmet.
	Connor cleared his throat.

	"Yes, it appears he's actively helping emergency services extract those trapped in the wreckage. This is... astounding." the reporter continued, his voice wavering between professionalism and disbelief.
	A deep groan echoed through the air, as Connor looked up. His eyes flashed again, as his super-senses turned the world to greyscale.
	The upper section of the flyover shifted, the fractured support beam above beginning to bend further. Dust rained down, and the fireman by his side gasped in horror.
	"You... you're..."
	Like a blur, Connor moved, shoving the fireman and the child out of the way, before leaping a few feet in the air - just in time to catch the falling segment of road.
	As he landed down, he felt the joints in his arms and legs buckle and crack. And if his spine could talk, it would call him a prick. His feet smashed down into the tarmac below, making new, bootprint potholes.
	His muscles burned as he forced his arms upward, halting the collapse inch by inch.
	The remaining crew looked up in shock and awe.
	"Don't just stand there! Move!" Connor roared.
	"You heard the man! Go!" the fireman barked. His team quickly rushed past, their adrenaline giving them the strength to pull open locked car doors and drag casualties to safety.
	"Holy shit... tell me we're getting this!" the reporter gasped.
	"Yeah, we did, we..." the cameraman replied, stunned. His lens zoomed in, focusing on the impossible sight.
	A man - or something looking like one, at least - holding up a section of carriageway with his bare hands.
	"Liz... Liz..." he stammered, recovering, "I... I don't quite know how to describe what we're seeing. The masked individual appears to be physically supporting the collapsed structure while emergency services clear the area."

	Connor's muscles rippled as his bones ached. Pain radiated through him, washing over like a wave.
	"Fuck... fuck... GGGRAAAAGH!" he howled, his eyes flashing a brilliant red. He pushed, the segment hoisting further into the air.
	"CLEAR!" the fireman barked out. It was music to Connor's ears.
	He planted his feet, and threw the chunk of road upwards, before darting out of the way. It slammed into the ground with enough force to shake him.
	Paramedics rushed over.
	"Uh... sir, are you..."
	Connor breathed out, and chuckled.
	"No, I'm really not. I broke a nail."
	The paramedic tilted his head, before walking off.
	"...well, *I* thought it was funny." he shrugged. At that, the reporter hurried towards him.
	"Hi - hi there... Zack Powell, Channel One News - do you have a moment?"
	Connor froze.
	"I just want to ask - how did you do that? You must have some kind of... well, super-strength."
	Connor remained silent, staring at him through the mask.
	Noise crackled over Zack's earpiece.
	"Oh, shit... for fuck's sake!" he shouted, yanking the earpiece out. He tore off his mic and threw it to the ground.
	"What?" the cameraman asked.
	"They're cutting the whole segment. No reason why."
	He ran a hand through his hair in frustration. "You wouldn't happen to know--"
	He turned back toward Connor - who had vanished.
	"Fucking superheroes..." he chuckled, before picking up the mic.

	Connor stumbled into the flat, aching all over. He laid down on the sofa, and closed his eyes.
	"Hey Connor! How'd it go?" Sparky asked, jumping up and curling into a ball on his lap.
	"You wanna know something funny?" Connor began, stroking his packmate's ears. "I got back to my car - it had a fuckin' ticket. I almost wish it got written off at the flyover."
	Sparky chuckled, not quite knowing what that meant - but knowing his best friend was joking.
	"How about you? How was recon?"
	"I found a spot where they were keeping more serum." he began. "Out in the moors. Seemed heavily guarded though."
	"I'll call Ivan - we'll attack tomorrow night." Connor nodded, before sniffing.
	"Hey... how come your mouth smells so... bloody?"
	"Oh, I got hungry on patrol." Sparky shrugged. "Caught a few animals. Did you want some?"
	"I---No, no thanks." Connor shuddered.
	"You sure? I grabbed a deer, had a good meal. There's still some left on the roof, if you're hungry."
	Connor paused. "You... left a deer carcass on the roof?"
	"Yeah!" Sparky smiled innocently. "I thought I should bring it back to share."
	Connor cringed a little. "I... thanks, buddy, that's really thoughtful of you..."
	"Did I mess up again?"
	"No, no..." Connor shook his head. "It's just..."
	He sighed, putting his arm around Sparky.
	"Before I met you, I was going for a run in the woods. And I don't know why, but I started chasing this deer, and before I knew it... I was eating it. Raw."
	Sparky nodded, intent on listening.
	"And that freaked me out." he continued. "Killing another creature..."
	"But we kill Super-Agents all the time?"
	"Yes, we do. But... it's different when it's something helpless. Killing Super-Agents is okay because they're coming after us. Killing humans is... trickier... but there are some circumstances where we have no choice. But killing a defenceless animal? Just because we're hungry?"
	"Where do you think the meat in the fridge comes from?" Sparky countered.
	"Yeah, you're not wrong. I'm not saying we can't eat meat, or that we have to go barefoot to avoid crushing bugs. But it's different when *we're* the ones doing the killing."
	He sighed again, and took a deep breath.
	"There's something inside us, Sparky. Something... monstrous. And we're both doing what we can to use it for good. But if we start killing things for food, or eating the freaks we kill... we're giving into it."
	"Yes, we are. We're giving into instincts, like we should."
	"No." Connor shook his head. "Just because you have an instinct doesn't mean you have to follow it."
	Sparky tilted his head, genuinely confused.
	Connor shrugged. "You can eat the deer, if you're hungry. But... please don't make a habit of hunting food. We've got plenty of food here."
	"Okay." Sparky nodded, still thinking. "I'll go get rid of the body."
	"Good idea. After that, let's get some rest." Connor smiled.
	As Sparky trotted upstairs, Connor remade the handguns from the night before.
	He held them aloft, and inspected them. They were pretty good imitations of airsoft pistols - except the mechanism was just as alien as the rest of him.
	"No..." he whispered, remembering his vow. "No."
	They skulked back beneath his skin.

	Later that evening, Zack was leaning against a wall in an alleyway, with a middle-aged man pacing before him.
	"Adam." he began. "We have to publish this."
	"For the last time, Zack, I'm ordering you to drop it." Adam replied, looking over his shoulder.
	"For Christ's sake! You saw the footage! This is big. A superhero. A literal superhero!" Zack protested.
	"Shut up!" Adam snapped. "Listen, you can't just say things like that out loud."
	"I'm a journalist, Adam. It's my *job* to say things like that out loud."
	Adam sighed, and rubbed his temples. "Zack, there's something big going on. I'm only saying all this to protect you."
	Zack scoffed. "And that's my point! There's something big going on. We're a public broadcaster! It's our duty to--"
	"Zack!" Adam snapped again. "We've been ordered to drop it. If you can't do that, you have to leave. Before you put us all in deep shit."
	"What's that supposed to mean?" Zack pushed.
	"Stop asking me that." Adam sighed.
	"No." Zack rose. "Fuck this. I'm going to report this, whether you want me to or not."
	At that, a large man in combat fatigues emerged from the darkness.
	"I was hoping you'd say that." he growled, grinning menacingly. His eyes were black-veined - a Super-Agent.
	"No! Please!" Adam protested. "He doesn't mean that!"
	"Adam?" Zack asked, shaking.
	"Shame. Can't take any chances." the man smirked, before pressing a rag against Zack's face. He struggled for a moment, before going limp.
	Adam squeezed a stress ball from his pocket, refusing to look at what was happening.
	The man hoisted Zack onto his shoulder. "You have your orders, I have mine." he said to Adam, grinning. He walked out, and bundled Zack into a car boot, before entering the driver seat, and speeding off.
