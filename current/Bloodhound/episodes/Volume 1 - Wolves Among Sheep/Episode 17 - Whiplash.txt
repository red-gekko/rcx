"I managed to vent your lung when you came in. Normally I'd say we put a tube in, but air build-up is minimal. If your breathing gets worse again, come right back in."
"Thanks, doc." Connor bowed his head. "I owe you one."
"Not even slightly." she smiled. "Although, that haemostatic foam gun - I'd love to get a few for the trauma department."
"Somehow, I doubt I'd get manufacturer's certification." Connor grinned. "Still, I'll cobble one for you to keep on the DL."
He motioned, and Whiplash rose. She limped as the pair left Judi's living room-turned-clinic.

"Why'd you save me?" she asked.
Connor paused, folding his arms.
"Three reasons. First, because I wanna know some things. Second... well, you're not with Paladin, so there's no inherent reason to kill you. Third... you were in a lot of pain. And I could help."
"Oh, don't tell me you've fallen for me." she grinned.
"I do that." he sighed. "Masking. I do it all the time. I quip during fights, I banter with my friends, I--"
"Spare me the psychoanalysis." she cut in harshly. "What's the real reason?"
"I've just given three."
"Three bullshit reasons." she scoffed. "You wouldn't save me unless there was something in it for you. Something more than a little chat."
"Is it that hard to believe I would help you?" he tilted his head.
"Yes." she nodded curtly. "I tried to kill you."
Connor sighed again. "You know, one guy shot at me with a sniper rifle. Then he unloaded a shotgun into my chest. He's now one of my closest allies."
"'Allies?' Are you asking me on a date?" she snarked.
"I'm guessing the guys who paid you won't be too happy." he shrugged. "And I'm guessing someone with your... 'talents...' would be a lot of trouble for them to go after. Why not work together?"
She looked away. "No. It's too dangerous."
"Whiplash." he said firmly. "I can see in slow motion, so I saw what you can do."
She sighed, leaning back. "It's called Trajectory Manipulation. A form of telekinesis."
"How do you do it?"
"How do you turn yourself into a smoke bomb and shit knives out of your hands?" she chuckled, before pausing. "I... I was only a little girl when they took me."
Connor nodded, wordlessly encouraging her. She hesitated, and folded her arms - as if putting up a shield.
"They put you in this airtight pod, filled with lukewarm water, and they whack an oxygen mask on your face." she continued. Her eyes squeezed shut, as if she was back in the pod once again. "You can't hear, smell, taste, feel, see... complete sensory deprivation."
"How long do you stay in?" he asked.
"As long as it takes." she shuddered. "They let you out once you manage to tell them the password. And the only way to get the password... is to read someone's mind."
"How do you do that?"
"That's what the deprivation is for." she nodded. "If your five senses are shut off, you're forced to rely on your sixth. That's why they lock up kids - apparently, children's myelin sheaths are less developed, which makes them more receptive to... to that kind of training."
"And once your sixth sense is activated... you're what, a psychic?"
"Esper. That was the term they used." she shrugged. "You still need to be trained, though. Most of our 'powers' are far too complicated to use right off the bat. For example, I can manipulate trajectories, but I never trained in general telekinesis. For my... line of work... you need a more subtle touch."
"Am I an 'Esper?'" he tilted his head. "I can do this... weird mind-merge thingy."
She stroked her chin. "Describe it to me."
"Well, I can understand what my dog is saying." he nonchalantly shrugged. "He's got the same powers as me. Also, if I'm touching someone, I can understand them, no matter what language they speak - and vice versa, too. I can also read people's emotions - and tell if they're lying. I've even... even managed to see into their memories - and them into mine. Like we become one person for a moment."
"Tactile telepathy." she nodded. "That's one of the first things they teach you. Makes sense - if you've never been trained, that's probably the only thing you'd be able to do off the bat."
Connor cleared his throat. "So, this... psychic laboratory, or whatever - who are they?"
"ODIN." she shrugged. "Operational Directorate for Investigative Neurokinetics. They were originally a CIA black project called MKULTRA; when they realised they were *actually* on to something, they went solo."
"ODIN... they really squeezed to make that acronym fit, eh?" he snarked.
She let out a soft laugh. "You're telling me. What with us, PaladinCorp, and Eidolon - you have a habit of getting people with ominous names to try and kill you."
"So, ODIN - they're the ones who want me dead?"
"No. They produce assassins. The man who *hired* me wants you dead." she chuckled grimly.
"And who's that?"
"I'm not sure." she sighed. "He's some rich man, by the looks of it. I don't get hired to ask questions."
"Did he mention the *why,* at least?"
"He gave me a dossier." she admitted. "You killing Hattab was highlighted. That, and your kill count on Super-Agents."
"What's yours?"
She hesitated. "103 Super-Agents and fellow Espers. And... 12 humans."
"And when you say 'rouge elements...'"
"Paladin mercs and ODIN operatives who deserted." she nodded. "Our creators don't want the general public knowing about superhumans. Your playing the hero is what made them hire me."
"Well, if your employer has a problem, I'd love for him to lodge a complaint in person..." Connor savagely grinned.
She chuckled, before pausing. "He'll be after me, now. And so will they..."
"Why would they come after you?"
"Because I failed." she admitted, choking up a little - not out of shame, but fear. "I know the punishment for failing and not dying. I've seen it delivered."
"Then stay here." he said softly. "If they come here, we can fight them off together."
She looked at him, her eyes trembling slightly. "I... I can't. I've got to go off the grid - at least for a while."
"I get it." Connor nodded gently. "But listen - if you do need help, come to the Singing Horse - it's a nightclub in Redbridge. Ask for me - the owner knows me."
"Why are you doing this?" she asked, her brow furrowing.
"Doing what? Being kind?"
"Yes! Why would you do that?!" she hissed.
"Because... I know what it's like to be tortured." he shrugged. "And to be hunted. And it's my fault you're in this situation. And... well, I'm something of a superhero. It's my *job* to be kind. Besides, it sounds like we're both in the business of killing Super-Agents."
She paused, looking at him up and down. The monster in the kevlar suit - her target, her prey... it didn't make sense.
She hesitantly reached out her hand. He paused, but took it, and their consciousnesses shot against each other.
She saw the Commandant's torment of him. His body twisting and reshaping against his will. He saw... nothing. The horror of nothingness. The pod.
It was too much. They pulled away, and rubbed their temples in unison.
She turned away, then turned her head back. She pulled off her domino mask, and gulped.
"Before they took me..." she began, choking on her words. "My name was Gwen. Gwen Blake."
Connor hesitated, but his mask rippled and melted away.
"I'm Connor. Connor Barrett."
Whiplash - Gwen - bowed her head, and walked away into the night.
Connor stood there, as she faded into the fog. The kevlar melted back down into his soft red hoodie, and he pulled the cloth closer for warmth. He took a deep breath, and smiled sadly.
