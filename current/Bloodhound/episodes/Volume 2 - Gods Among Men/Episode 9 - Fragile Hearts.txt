The sky rippled a brilliant orange as the sun headed toward the sea.
For most, this heralded the end of their shift.
For the heroes of Drackhill, it was the start.

"Privet, tovarishch!" Ivan began the call with.

According to a friend in the home office, this whole thing is causing quite a stir - so if the police learn of your presence, they'll bring armed backup this time." Ivan explained. Connor stretched and rolled his shoulders as he headed into Riverside Repairs. The garage was empty, and almost silent. He chuckled, and headed down into the basement.
"Heyooo!" Trey cheered, looking up from a circuit board he was tinkering on. Amy was unloading a crate of machine parts onto a shelf.
"Hey, guys. What are you doing here?"

"Alright... what's wrong?" Trey asked.
"Hm?"

"Har har." Connor snarked. "Uh... I was speaking with Chloe last night, and... she asked me about Laverty."

"I try to block them out most of the time." he shrugged. "Besides, I don't wanna invade people's privacy. If I wanted to do that, I'd mind-probe you all."

"So that's one kill explained, then. All I need to do is explain the other 116, and then we should be golden." Connor snarked.
"By the way... Chloe said we probably shouldn't tell Bea about Laverty just yet. Give her a week or so to get her head straight, and all." Amy added.

"It feels like fixing up a dam. Plug one leak, and five more spring up. I mean, it's great the cops are gone and Gambit's forced to hide, but I'm spending so much time focused on the Super-Agents, I don't really have the time to deal with normal crime."

"The same police force I single-handedly dismantled last week, right?" Connor tilted his head. "Besides, they weren't doing much on that front anyway. But the city's too big for just a few of us. I might have enough time to do the odd patrol, but... we need to prioritise."
Amy sighed, and put a hand on his shoulder. "It's the ugly maths of war, isn't it? Let one fire burn so you can put out a hundred others later..."
He gave a sad smile. "No use moping, I suppose."
"What about that psychic dominatrix? Couldn't she help?" Trey cocked an eyebrow.
"First of all, please don't let her hear you call her that." Connor chuckled. "Second of all, she's busy this week. But yeah, Whiplash did say she'd be willing to work with us more often. Until then..."

"Take it easy, Werewolf." Trey smiled.
Amy watched as he left. She tried not to, but couldn't help as her eyes glanced over the Crusader-pattern combat suit.

And, for the love of all life, do not under any circumstances let anyone inject it."

"10PM. Listen out for gunshots - you'll find us."

"Hey, Connor! Yeah, I'm good. You free? I'm between classes at the moment, and I am *so* bored. Wanna meet at the cafe?" she asked.

"Hey, bud." he called Sparky over. "We're gonna go meet up with Bea."

She chuckled. "How, um... how was it, seeing him while you were locked up?"
"It was weird..." he shrugged. "It was obviously a horrid situation, even worse for him - it was his worst nightmare. But still, spending that week seeing each other... I don't know."
Bea smiled sympathetically. "By the way, I... I don't know if Chloe told you, but... last week, I said some pretty horrible things about you and Trey being in the Hoods."
Connor tilted his head, and raised his eyebrows.
"I... Chloe was telling me she was worried about Charlie - I should have listened to her. Instead, I went on a rant about how you being in a gang meant you were no better. I was just deflecting..." she admitted.
Connor smiled softly. "It's okay. You... you weren't yourself. Besides, if me and Trey started finding things offensive, we'd be the biggest hypocrites going." he grinned.
Bea chuckled.

With that, a barista approached. "What can I get you?" she asked.
"I'll have a latte, please. One sugar." Bea replied.
"And can I have a mocha with two sugars, please?" Connor asked.
"And, can we get some dog treats for the little man?" Bea smiled, gesturing at Sparky - who had fallen fast asleep at their feet.
"Treats?" he mumbled.
"Of course!" the barista smiled, and handed over the card reader. Connor reached for his pocket, but Bea grabbed the machine.
"You sure? I can pay for it."
Bea smiled again. "Nah, it's cool. My treat." she replied, paying with her card.
The drinks were brought over. Connor sipped his mocha and smiled.
"Thanks, Bea. When they reopen the Horse, I'll get your round."
Bea chuckled. "So, anyway... we hang out a load, I feel like we haven't ever had a conversation where it was just us, right?"
"Hah, that's true actually." he nodded. "There's always at least one other person around."
She smiled, and sipped her drink. "Well, not to sound like a stupid icebreaker, but.... what do you do for work?"
"Well, I work with Tommy - he owns the Horse, and the repair shop me, Shannon, and Trey work at."
"What do you do there?"
"Mainly computer stuff." he shrugged. "A bit of a techie, me. I investigate the Skinners - find out stuff like planned drug deals, smuggling operations, the like."
"And you... you work with the Werewolf?"
"A little." he shrugged. "Sometimes the Werewolf brings us laptops he's seized. That's how the Chief Constable got exposed."
"Did you study IT in school?" she asked, sipping her latte.
"For a year in college, before I dropped out."

"By the way... I saw you and Chloe on the dance floor." she began. "Tell me honestly - how do you feel about her?"
Connor turned pale. "I, um... well, I... it's... um..." he stuttered.
Bea chuckled. "It's alright. I think I can guess."
Connor smiled shyly. "Yeah, I... yeah, you know."
"Chloe is... very important to me." she began. "I just... look, I trust you, Connor. But... are you going to ask Chloe out?"
"I... I mean, she... I..." Connor stuttered, even harder this time.
"Connor." she asked, more firmly.
"I'd like to, I suppose. But... well, I'm a wee bit shy is all. And... I've got a lot going on in my life right now. I don't know if it would be right to ask Chloe out, if I can't promise I can give her my undivided attention."
He cringed slightly. "Can I be trusted? Is it safe for her?"
This double life of his was tearing him apart.
Bea sighed softly - more out of sympathy than anything else. "Connor..." she began. "Chloe is my best friend. And I'm glad to hear you're taking your circumstances into account. But... please don't string her along."
"I would never want to do that. I swear."
Bea smiled. "I know. Just... make your mind up soon, yeah? Whether it's a yes or a no, just tell her so she knows. Can you do that?"
Connor paused for a moment. "This... this stays between us, yeah?" he asked.
Bea nodded.
"I... look, I *do* like her." he admitted.
"But?"
"No buts. I just... I don't know if it's fair on her to ask her out. I mean... I'm probably not the right guy for her."
"What do you mean?"
He took a deep breath. "I just... she deserves someone with an actual future, you know? Not some chav from the slums."
"Or some mutant murderer." he thought.
Bea smiled softly. "Connor... do you honestly think Chloe gives a crap about any of that?"
"No. Which is a big reason why I like her. I just..."
"Chloe's been through a lot. Has she mentioned..."
"Francis?"
"Yeah." Bea sighed, her brows furrowing at the mention of his name.
Connor nodded affirmatively.
"Well, then you know about all of that. So, let me tell you - Chloe would be happy to be with you. She doesn't care about where you came from. She likes you, Connor. Like, *likes* you. And I... I haven't seen her like this before. And you're so good to her. Just... think about it, okay?"
"I will. I promise."
"Thank you... anyway, I'd better head off. Bloody uni." she grinned.
Connor smiled. "It was good to catch up. And... if you need to talk about what happened with Charlie, I'm here for you."
"Thanks." she smiled back. "I'll see you around!"
Connor leaned back, and exhaled.
"Am I right for Chloe?" he thought to himself.
"Is it fair to put this double life on her?"
"Isn't it up to her?"
"But how can she make an informed decision?"
He sighed, and headed off.

That evening, Connor overlooked the derelict shopping centre-turned-laboratory in Halford. Sparky emerged from the shadows behind him, in costume and hunched on his feet.
"Hey, bud." he smiled, stroking Sparky's head. "You're getting better at walking bipedally."
"I've had practice." he smiled. "Anyway, I've finished scouting it out."
"What did you find?"
"There are 27 people in there. 15 Super-Agents, 3 normal humans in lab coats, administering syringes, and 9 other humans. I think they're waiting to be injected."
Connor's eyes narrowed, a flicker of impatience in his gaze. "No time like the present, then."
With that, he leapt off the edge of the building, Sparky following closely behind, landing in a fluid roll before springing into action.
The old urge whispered, eager for blood.
As they entered the facility, a heavy metallic door slammed open ahead of them. Super-Agent sentries, already on high alert, opened fire the moment they spotted the intruders. Bullets slammed into Connor's body, as he dived into cover.
"Yeah, keep shooting at me. It'll definitely work eventually, right?" Connor taunted.
Sparky's fur rippled as he darted forward, avoiding the barrage. He was covered by a hail of crossbow bolts.
These weren't the blunt, dull bolts he used against humans - these were sharp enough to punch through metal.
One unlucky Agent found two bolts bury into his eyes - breaking past his skull and into his brain.
An Agent stepped into Sparky's path, firing a rapid volley. Sparky crashed into his chest, and a flurry of claws sent viscera covering the ground.
Melee time, now. Connor dashed forwards, his crossbows de-morphing and being replaced with wrist-mounted blades.
In a single motion, he grabbed an Agent by the throat, lifting him off the ground as though he weighed nothing.
With a quick, fluid thrust, the blade pierced the Agent's armour, sliding through his body like butter. The Agent's eyes widened in shock as it punched through his spine and exited his back, extending out to impale the one behind him as well.
He grinned under his mask, as his suit bubbled. Then, it burst.
Sparky, now under the cover of smoke, dived in. With a savage snarl, he pounced on one of the Agents, his jaws snapping shut with brutal force around his head. There was a sickening crack as he tore the head clean off, the body collapsing lifelessly to the floor.
Without pause, Sparky turned to another, his claws slashing through the air. He mauled the second, raking through his chest with such force that he was thrown across the room. A third charged, but Sparky's teeth sank into his throat, ripping through flesh and muscle with ease.
As the pair progressed deeper into the facility, the remaining Agents began to fall back, realising that they were outmatched. They tried to regroup, but Connor and Sparky moved too fast.
One Agent made the mistake of trying to flank Connor. He turned just in time to grab his arm mid-swing, twisting it violently. The Agent's scream was cut short as Connor slammed him into the ground with bone-crushing force. A swift stomp to the head made a *pop* noise.
Sparky was still on the hunt, his heightened senses allowing him to track the last few Agents through the chaos. One tried to escape through a side door, but Sparky was faster. With a burst of speed, he leapt through the air, landing on the Agent's back and sinking his teeth into his neck. He struggled, but Sparky's jaws tightened, and within moments, the Agent's movement ceased.
The final Super-Agent made a desperate attempt to use a grenade as a suicide bomb, but Connor was there in an instant. He grabbed the Agent by the wrist, twisting it with enough force to snap the bone - while another, bladed hand punched through his gut. The grenade fell down, and Connor shoved it into the now-open abdomen, and dived down, taking cover.
*BOOM!*
Shrapnel and fragments of bone buried into the room's walls, while red paste coated them.
Once his ears stopped ringing, Connor rose. He looked down at his hands, and saw them shaking.
Sparky trotted over and inspected. "You okay?" he asked.
"I don't know." Connor whispered. "I still... I still get the shakes, sometimes. I don't regret killing these fuckers, but it's... it's not always as easy as I'd like."
With the last of the Super-Agents neutralised, Connor and Sparky made their way to the lab's exit. The sound of sirens grew louder in the distance.
"All Super-Agents dead. Just the humans left." Sparky barked.
"Time to go." Connor nodded. "The Detective can handle them."
Sparky nodded, and the pair left. The police arrived just as the duo disappeared into the night.

When injected into a living host, they take orders from the brain. They look at the host's DNA, and assume their body has suffered severe damage to its DNA and physiology - so they get to work, trying to 'fix the damage.' Other, 'natural' brains don't have the ability the Werewolf does to direct them properly, meaning they can only do their best - but that seems to be enough, if creatures like the Giant are any indication."

The silence hung in the air for a moment. Both siblings breathed heavily, as if they were trading physical blows.
"I need you, Amy. I need my sister safe. If you're doing this... I can't protect you."
She sniffled, and leaned back against a wall.
"You can't always protect me, Connor. And the truth is, I'm not doing this to impress you, or anything. I'm doing it because... because doing good things makes me feel good. Sometimes that means working in the dog shelter, sometimes that means beating the piss out of Skinners. Please. Please let me do this. Let me help. I... I know the risks. And I think it's worth them."
Connor paused for a long while.

This was his little sister.
A human.
A civilian - one of the citizens of Drackhill he swore to protect.
Mortal.

She was also one of the strongest people he knew.

"...You don't fight any superhumans." he finally spoke. "You stick to street thugs *only.* And you never, *ever* assault a location without backup from me or Sparky. And for the love of God, do some training lessons with me before you go out again. Your hand-to-hand is sloppy."
"You... you mean it?" she asked, her eyes damp.
"If I said no, you'd steal the suit anyway. At least this way I can keep an eye on you." he sighed, folding his arms.
He wanted to believe that was the reason - that he was only reluctantly accepting the things he could not change.
It was a lie. His steel-grey eyes flickered with pride.
She squeed in excitement, and wrapped him in an embrace.
"OWOWOWOW TURNITOFFTURNITOFF AAAAGH!" he convulsed.
"Shit, sorry!" she gasped, quickly flicking a switch on her gauntlet.
He panted, falling to his knees. Then, he burst into laughter, and she joined in.

Night became morning, as it often does.

Nina clicked her heels in respect. Ward nodded and smiled.
Naismith bowed his head respectfully. Ward likewise nodded.
"How has Acting Chief Constable Tulloch been faring?" she asked.
"He's good, ma'am. But, with so few numbers in the force after the... 'incident,' he's struggling, to be frank."

"Yes, that's correct, Ma'am." Naismith began. "We have been working this case for around 2 months now."
"Specifically, we were first assigned on the 9th of November." Nina added.
Ward smiled. "Impressive memory, Constable."
Nina nodded humbly.
"Anyway..." Ward continued. "It would seem that, well, to be blunt... the case isn't going well, is it?" she asked, studying their reactions.
"The progress has been... limited, ma'am." Nina said awkwardly.
"Ma'am, if I may?" Naismith began. Ward nodded.
"Well, Ma'am, I know Dorsey's rap sheet is already a mile long... but he was hindering our inquiry. It was his decision to bring in a private company, Vitalis, as opposed to escalating this to a government agency." Naismith explained, trying not to seem like he was making excuses.
"You have the floor, Inspector." Ward motioned for him to continue, intrigued to see where this would go.
"Well, we were forbidden from directly collaborating with Vitalis, ma'am. In addition, we were not permitted to investigate any leads regarding the targets of the Werewolf - our only task was to locate, identify, and either capture, or eliminate him." Naismith began. "Knowing that Dorsey was involved in illicit sexual activity, with a trafficking ring that appears to have ties to a criminal conspiracy the Werewolf has been targeting... I would like to formally request that the remit of our investigation be expanded, and that we be permitted full access to Vitalis's work, ma'am."
Ward leant back in her chair, deep in thought. Finally, she spoke.
"You are asking to be allowed to investigate his victims?" she asked.
"I am asking to be allowed to consider all avenues in the pursuit of justice, Ma'am." he replied.
"And you, Constable? What are your views?" Ward asked, turning to Nina - eager to hear her opinion.
"Ma'am." Nina replied, preparing to speak. "While our mission of course remains to identify the vigilante known as the Werewolf and bring him to justice, it is clear that there are several criminal elements at play that need to be investigated."
Ward smiled. She turned, now addressing both. "And why do you wish to have full access to Vitalis?"
"Ma'am..." Naismith began, choosing his words carefully. "I don't know if you've read the reports on the... on the capabilities of both the Werewolf, and his enemies?" he asked, realising he didn't use the word "victims."
"I have, Inspector. And I agree, they are troubling." Ward answered.
