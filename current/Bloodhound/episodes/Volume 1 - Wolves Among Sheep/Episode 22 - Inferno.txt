VOLUME - Wolves Among Sheep
EPISODE - Inferno
============================================================

Sunday. Mid-March, 2002. The Bogside, Derry.
Bloody Sunday. Always a Bloody Sunday.
Every day was Sunday, those days.

A young Connor sat in his room, in a house on the Bogside. Other sections of the district were bombed-out ruins - the dreadful Bogside War was beginning, unbeknownst to the little boy. He was drawing on a piece of paper.
His grandparents, Eamon and Geraldine, came in through the door. Eamon headed straight for his armchair, and began reading a book. Geraldine sighed, and went upstairs.
"Hello, sweetheart." she began.
"Granny!" Connor smiled.
Geraldine hugged him tightly, and sat with him. "Let's see what you've done..."
She looked at the papers, and saw doodles of cats and dogs playing in a field. She smiled warmly.
"You're very talented, Connor." she beamed. Connor nodded, and blushed.
"Was church good?" he asked.
She nodded, and pulled out a notepad, and a pocket Bible. "As promised." she smiled. "Let's begin."
Connor put his hands together, before remembering to cross himself.
"In the name of the father, the son, and the holy spirit. Amen." he said.
Geraldine smiled, and awaited his recital.
"Our father, who aren't in heaven.
Hello be thy name.
Thy kingdom come,
thy will be done,
On Earth as it is in Heaven.
Give us this daily bread,
and lead us not into temptation,
but deliver us from evil,
Amen."
Geraldine stifled a chuckle, and gave him an encouraging pat on the shoulder.
"Repeat after me." she smiled.
"Our father, who art in heaven,
Hallowed be thy name.
Thy kingdom come,
Thy will be done,
On Earth as it is in Heaven.
Give us this day our daily bread,
And forgive us for our trespasses,
As we forgive those who trespass against us,
And lead us not into temptation,
But deliver us from evil,
Amen."
Connor recited with her, and she stroked his hair gently.
"Well done, darling. You're doing really well."
She looked at the notepad, and saw "James 5." written. She opened her pocket bible, and recited it.
"Come now, ye rich men, weep and howl for your miseries that shall come upon you. Your riches are corrupted and your garments moth-eaten. Your gold and silver are cankered, and the rust of them shall be a witness against you and shall eat your flesh as it were fire. Ye have heaped treasure together for the last days. Behold, the hire of the labourers who have reaped your fields, which you kept back by fraud, crieth; and the cries of those who have reaped have entered into the ears of the Lord of Sabaoth. Ye have lived in pleasure on the earth and been wanton. Ye have nourished your hearts, as in a day of slaughter. Ye have condemned and killed the just, and he doth not resist you."
Connor looked up in awe. "What does that mean, granny?"
She smiled. "It means we must always be generous, dear. If we hoard all the money in the world, other people will suffer - and God doesn't want that, does he?"
Connor shook his head. "But God can do anything! Why doesn't he just give everyone everything?"
Geraldine sighed. "I'm not sure, sweetheart. But I have faith that he has a plan."
Connor nodded, deep in thought.
"Why can't I come to church with you and grandad? Why do you have to write the verses and bring them here?"
She rubbed his hair lovingly. "I'm sorry, dear. If it were up to me, you'd come to church with us. But as long as your grandfather says no, we'll always have this special time together, where I read to you."
Connor looked up, and smiled. "I love you, granny."
"I love you too." she replied, hugging him.
"Also... when can I see Vince again?" Connor asked. "It's always so nice when he comes to see me."
Geraldine sighed painfully. "Oh, dear... your uncle is a very busy man, you know."
"Last time he was here, him and grandad were shouting at each other." Connor said sadly. "Do they not like each other?"
Geraldine looked away. "Look... I'm sure we'll see him soon, okay? Once the Brits take away this awful blockade."
"What's that mean?"
"Nothing for you to worry about, dear. Now, let's work on your reading..."
She put down the pocket bible on a specific page. Connor looked down, and tried to read it.
"I-In the beginning, God cre... crate... cree-ah..."
"Created." Geraldine encouraged.
"Created the ha--no, Heaven and Erith."
"Earth."
Connor smiled. "And the Earth was... wit--without form and--and v-void, and d... dark--ness was oo--upon the face of the deep. And the Spirit of God moff... no, 'moved' upon the face of the waters."
Geraldine looked on, and smiled proudly.

It was early, now - the night sky slowly became paler, as the sun lazily prepared to rise above the mountain.
Over in Redbridge, a dingy, run-down pawn shop stood - Ee-Zee Kash, as it was imaginatively named.
And outside, a group of three. They had names - Freddie, Pablo, and Giles - but in the criminal underworld, they had a different name.
The Copperheads.
"Hey boss, how come they call us the Copperheads?" Freddie tilted his head. He was the biggest of the bunch, and cracked open the store entrance with a crowbar.
"...what?" Pablo asked, eyebrow cocked.
"Oi, I 'eard it's some kind of snake out in America, or somethin'." Giles nodded. His frame was thin, and his voice was nasal.
"Shut up, boys." Pablo sighed. "It's because of the Norringford job. Where we stripped everything, down to the copper wiring. I've told you this story, like, fifteen times!"
"Nah, I'm pretty sure they're reptiles, not snakes." Freddie stroked his chin.
"Reptiles *are* snakes, you moron!" Giles hissed.
Pablo groaned. "*Snakes* are *reptiles.*"
"That's what I said!"
"No, you... all snakes are reptiles, but not all... ugh, just shut the fuck up and start robbing!"
Unbeknownst to them, a creature perched outside, on a street light.
Not a reptile. Not a snake.
A Werewolf.
Connor's eyes flashed crimson, as he grinned - before descending. He crept in through the door, while the three goons filled their bags with valuables.
"Do you reckon the Werewolf's gonna show up?" Freddie trembled.
"That thing's just a myth. He's actually a vampire." Giles nodded confidently.
"Oh yeah? What makes you say that?"
"He only comes out at night. His eyes are bright red. And he's out regardless of the phase of the moon."
"Will you two just shut your traps?!" Pablo snapped.
"But boss, I'm worried. What if he comes here and drinks my blood? I need my blood!"
"He's not gonna drink your blood, Freddie!" he sighed. "The Werewolf don't kill, unless you're one of them super-pricks."
"He killed Alan Hughes." Giles noted.
"Hughes was a nonce. We're just good, honest robbers." Pablo grinned.
"*Ahem.*" Connor announced himself. "You know it's rude to talk about people like they're not in the room, right?"
The three screamed, dropping their bags and pouncing backwards.
Freddie steeled his nerves, and whacked Connor in the head with his crowbar.
His costume rippled, and he fell to the ground.
"No..." he weakly cried out. "It can't be..."
"Huh?!" Freddie gasped, gripping the bar tighter.
"That crowbar... is made of boron steel..." he groaned in pain, clutching his chest.
"...so?"
"Boron steel... is... my super-weakness." he rattled, reaching out.
"It is?!" Giles tilted his head. "I thought it'd be silver?"
"Common misconception... we find it offensive..." Connor's chest heaved, before gripping Freddie tight.
"Listen to me... listen closely..."
"Oh god, I'm so sorry! What do I do? What do you need?" Freddie said hurriedly.
"Not enough time... too late..." Connor gurgled. "If a mortal slays the Werewolf... he must *become* the Werewolf..."
"What?! No, I--"
"The city is yours... guard it well..." he finally breathed out, before going still.
Freddie dropped the crowbar, and looked down at his hands in horror.
"You... you killed him." Pablo said incredulously.
"I didn't think I could! Oh god, am I a supervillain now? I don't want to be a supervillain!"
"No, you heard him!" Giles nodded. "You're the new Werewolf!"
"I'm not ready for that kind of responsibility!" Freddie lamented.
Giles pulled him back to the group. "Go on. Try and use your powers!"
"I... I'm not sure how." Freddie tilted his head. "Um... moon! Wolf! Uh..."
Then, the two idiots felt something from behind - an arm on either side, and a head leaned in through the middle.
"You bozos didn't *actually* believe that, did you?" Connor grinned.
Pablo buried his face in his hands. "Are you gonna beat us up now?"
At that, Connor chuckled, dusting himself off. "How about you all put everything back where you found it, and get out of here?"
"R-really?" Freddie beamed.
"I'm warning you, though. If I catch you stealing again, I won't be so merciful."
The two smiled giddily as they replaced the valuables, while Pablo sighed deeply.
"You guys get home safe!" Connor waved as they left.
"Thanks, Mr. Werewolf!" Freddie cheered.
Connor nodded as they walked away, then finally burst into laughter.

Amanda ran her hands through her hair, her heart beating fast.
She grabbed her work bag, and took a deep breath.
"Mummy, where are you going?" Kyle - her little boy - asked.
"Oh, um..." she began guiltily. "Mummy's just been called into work, darling. Don't worry, daddy will be here in an hour to look after you."
"Can't you stay here?"
"Sorry, sweetie... we need the money, okay?"
"But I'm hungry!"
"I--" she stuttered, before her eyes caught the time on the clock. "You'll have to wait, darling. Daddy will be here soon."
She turned, and headed out.
Kyle sighed. "I'm hungry." he complained again, this time to himself.

"Look, can you just get here as soon as possible?" Amanda asked over the phone.
Noise came back.
"Yes, I know it's irresponsible, but in case you forgot, we're skint! Because *someone* hasn't been paying my child support!" she hissed.
More noise.
"Oh, shut up. I don't care what's happened with us, but Kyle deserves a better father. Now get your lazy, drunken arse over here, or you'll never see him again."
She scowled, and cut off the call.
She looked up, and saw a pair of youths walking in, along with their large "dog."
"Good evening, Ms. Irwin!" Connor smiled, waving.
"Evening." she nodded, half-smiling and walking past.
The trio ascended into their flat.

Kyle looked at the kitchen sadly, rubbing his belly.
Then, an idea sparked.
He walked over to the fridge, and pulled out a stool so he could reach.
It was mostly empty - but there were a few things, nonetheless.
He grabbed a metal tray, filled with scraps from the night before, and placed it into the microwave. Then, he randomly pressed buttons - just as he'd seen his mother do.
As the machine turned on, it made a crackling noise. He shrugged, and moved to the hob.
There, he dropped some eggs into a frying pan - shells and all - before twisting the dials. He cocked an eyebrow when no flame came out, so he twisted some more. The smell of gas tickled his nostrils.
He assumed that was how it worked, and so hopped off his stool and headed to his room, to go play with his toys until dinner would magically be ready.
A sharp crack rang out from the microwave, and a small plume of smoke curled into the air.

Connor yawned as he stretched, feeling his joints click.
"That was fun!" Sparky yapped, before flopping into his bed.
"Ugh... long day." Amy grinned.
"Do you want anything?" he asked, smiling.
She looked at him quizzically. "Right, enough of that. You've been a right weirdo ever since last night. What's wrong?"
"Can't a man be nice to his sister every so often?" he snarked.
"Not when the man is you, and the sister is me, no." she quipped back. "What is it?"
He sighed, placing down the stack.
"It scared me." he admitted. "I nearly got you killed."
"Connor..." she began, placing her hand on his shoulder. "It was a freak coincidence. That psycho wasn't with those mutant pricks, was he?"
He looked away, bracing his arms together. "You could've died. And I could've stopped the whole thing before it began. If it weren't for this... this stupid secret identity, this *lie,* I--"
"Hey." she cut in. "You don't have a choice. What do you think would happen if the cops found out who you really were? No... it's better for now that we keep going as we've been going."
He sighed again. "That psycho outright said I inspired him to start killing again. And I... broke my vow, even if he's still alive. What if... what if I'm doing more harm than good?"
"You're not." she cut in, firmly but caringly. "Ask anyone on the street what they think about the Werewolf - they'll call you our very own superhero."
Connor scoffed, but cracked a smile.
She beamed, before opening up the powersuit's display case.
"You like it?" Connor chuckled.
"It looks awesome." she nodded. "Can I try it on? Just for ten minutes?"
"No."
"Pleeeaaase?"
"...five minutes."

Amy stood tall, flexing the suit's carapace. Connor and Sparky morphed their own costumes up, and the three looked into the mirror.
"Damn... don't lie, we'd make an awesome team." Amy grinned.
"Don't get any ideas." he laughed. He paused, before re-morphing his air-powered handgun.
"What's that?" she asked.
"I'm experimenting." he nodded. "Trying to make it less... lethal."
"I thought you hated guns?"
"I hate being able to kill someone with a twitch of a finger." he shook his head. "If I can make some kind of slam round, though... that doesn't count."
He pushed it into his palm, and pulled the trigger.
*bang!* it went quietly. He pulled his hand back - barely wounded.
"Needs a little less velocity at short range, but... that shouldn't kill, if I'm careful where I aim."
"Damn." Amy grinned. "It'd be useful for intimidation, too."
"How do you mean?"
"Well, everyone knows you don't kill humans, or use guns at all. So, if you wanna scare someone, pull a gun on them! Then, they'll think they're *really* pissed you off if you're doing that."
"Huh, good idea." Connor chuckled. "Right, anyway, you'd better put that suit back--"
His ears pricked up, as a noise from four floors down hit them.
*FWOOM!*
His eyes flashed.
"Explosion. Downstairs. Smells like gas." he began. "Sparky, let's go."
"What about me?" Amy asked.
"Put my suit back." he said firmly. The Werewolves rushed out of the window, as she sighed.

Kyle looked at the blazing kitchen in terror.
"Help! Please, someone help!" he cried.
His door smashed open as Connor burst in.
"It's okay! You're safe." he barked, grabbing the boy. He turned back to the doorway, but it was already engulfed by the flames.
Shit. This was bad. *Really* bad.
He kicked open their window, and vaulted out, before placing Kyle on the ground. He rushed back in through the reception, and his hearts skipped their beats.
The fire was spreading fast. Way too fast.
Sparky crawled up by his side, wide-eyed.
"What do we do?" he asked, as the fires began to spread to the stairway.
Any moment now, it would rush up the cladding like a chimney.
The reception ceiling groaned as smoke thickened, black and choking. The people on his floor filtered out the door - but above, they were trapped.
He reluctantly pressed his fingers to his ear.
"Amy, can you hear this?" her helmet whispered.
"Yeah!" she said quickly.
Connor grabbed a fire extinguisher, and started fighting the stairway blaze. He kept it controlled, but couldn't put it out.
"Get down here, now."

The robotic exoskeleton enhanced her at least tenfold. She ripped down the stairs and crashed into the hallway.
"What can I do?" she asked.
Connor thought for a moment. "We can't fight the fire here long enough to get everyone out..." he panicked.
"If we get them to the roof, you and me can glide them down." Sparky barked.
"...that works." he nodded, before morphing a funnel-shaped object - a megaphone.
"Everyone! It's not safe down here! Get to the roof, and we can get you to safety!" he roared.
He looked up, his eyes flashing as he watched the heartbeats above. Thankfully, they began moving.
"Spartacus, you go to the roof and start getting them down. I'll check each floor, and herd them up. Amy..." he turned, hesitant. "That suit has an inbuilt rebreather. It's fireproof too - but you're not. Stay down here, and use the extinguisher to slow down the spread of the blaze. Do whatever you can to stop the building from collapsing. You won't be able to put the fire out - but if you keep it limited, that'll give us just enough time."
"Got it." she nodded, her eyes narrowing with determination under the helm's visor.
Sparky went outside and shot upwards, while Connor charged through the flames.

The first floor wasn't as bad as the reception.
Of course, that was like saying "The arsenic wasn't as bad as the cyanide."
Tongues of flame licked against the walls hungrily, and Connor frantically scanned the flats for heartbeats.
A few cowered behind a lit doorway, thumping quickly within the chests of people holding each other, choking on the smoke.
Connor smashed through, and they looked up at their red-eyed saviour.
"Come with me!" he barked, reaching out his hand. Under his mask, he smiled as they took it.
Another flat was filled with immobile residents - the smoke simply too thick.
Connor tore rags from his suit, and handed them out - they covered their faces, and made their way out.

On the roof, the first crowd of residents arrived. Sparky rolled out his shoulders, and stood up on his back legs - changing his posture from lupine to almost apelike.
"Um... um..." one adult stammered. Sparky grinned, and shot a grapple at one of the kids.
"Wheeee!" they cheered as they yanked towards him, then screamed happily as he glided them down. He shot his grapple back to the top of the building, and sailed upwards.
Amy sprayed out the last of the extinguisher's foam, before dropping it and grabbing another. She went to wipe a bead of sweat off of her forehead, but groaned as her gauntlet clanged against her helm.

The last of the humans on the floor had been gathered, and Connor herded them upstairs like a sheepdog. Then his hearts skipped a beat as he heard a cracking noise.
"MOVE!" he roared, pouncing forwards as a section of stairway fell through. He stuck to the walls like an insect, and shot his grapple down, catching a resident who had tripped into the hole.
Then, his eyes widened in terror.
"NO!" he gasped, as the stairway fell down, crashing onto Amy. He threw the civilian back into the crowd and dived down.
Amy had tensed as it hit her. She was dazed, and winded - but unharmed. He ripped the rubble away, and pulled her free.
"I'm okay!" she nodded. "I'm fine. Get them to safety!"
He hesitated, but nodded back, grappling up through the hole.
He felt the steps ahead, and they crumbled too. The stairs were no longer an option.
"Think... think..." he muttered to himself. He looked up, checking the rest of the tower.
The second floor was still packed with humans, now beginning to choke on the rising smoke. The third was likewise filled with cowering residents. The fourth had finished evacuating.
Finally, inspiration struck. He dashed down to the out-of-service lift shaft, and ripped it open.
"Bingo." he grinned. He ran vertically up the shaft, opening the second floor's doors. Then, the third. And the fourth. He raced out, making his way to the roof.
"Spartacus!"
"Oh, we're using our codenames." Sparky nodded, rushing over.
"The stairs won't work. I'm gonna evacuate the first floor by grappling them up the lift shaft. I need you to handle the second. Then we'll tackle the third."
"What about the people up here?"
Connor paused. "They're safe for now. We need to focus on rescue first, evac second."
"Wait! Please don't leave us up here!" one man pleaded.
Connor turned, and spoke firmly. "Right now, your neighbours are choking on smoke and at risk of getting caught in the fire. You're safe up here, away from the smoke and the flames. Do you want them to die, so that you could get down a few minutes quicker?"
The man looked up, and shook his head. Connor nodded, and made it to the shaft. He shot a grapple at the ceiling, and zipped down. The rest of the first floor had coalesced by the lift doors, so he grabbed as many as he could, and ferried them up - as did Sparky. Their motions resembled a pulley system as much as they did monstrous heroes.
Before long, they'd got all the remaining humans up to the roof.

"Conn--Uh, I mean, 'Vanguard.'" Amy began over the commline. "I'm almost out of foam. You don't have long before the fire reaches you."
"Or worse, the building collapses." Connor grimaced. "Any news on the firemen?"
"They're at least ten minutes away - fuckin' rush hour traffic." she sighed.
Connor thought hard, before his eyes flashed.
"Am--Robot." he said, quickly coming up with a codename. "Forget about the stairs and halls. Focus on keeping load-bearing structures safe. Buy us enough time to keep the building standing."
"Got it." she audibly nodded. Connor turned back.
"Spartacus - we need to get everyone out, now."
"How?"
"Shoot your grapple at mine."
Sparky tilted his head, but did so. Their grappling wires caught into each other.
"Now, get to ground level. Put as much biomass as you can into the chord."
At that, he realised his packmate's plan, and descended.
The rope went taut, and Sparky moved so it sat at a 45-degree angle.
Connor turned his head.
"Okay, here's how this works. I'll create a pair of cuffs, and you each zipline down. Once you're at the bottom, we'll release the cuffs, and go again. Don't worry - we thickened the rope towards the bottom, so you'll slow down at the end. Got it?"
The people nodded, and Connor smiled softly.
"Children first!"
He knelt down slightly, relaxing his posture.
"Now, who is the bravest kid here?"
"I am!" a little girl firmly proclaimed. Connor grinned, and sent her down.
She closed her eyes in fear as she fell, but opened them as she landed, and smiled.
Sparky gave her a lick, and dissolved the cuffs, sending the biomass back up through the rope - and Connor repeated the motion again. And again. And again.

Finally, the fire engines arrived, just as the flames reached the fourth floor. They showered the block with water, plugging right into the nearest hydrants to keep the flow running.
Connor let the water wash over him, and took a deep breath as the final people escaped. He unformed his rope, and glided down.
He smiled proudly, as Amy helped carry anyone with smoke sickness over to the ambulances for respiration.
"CONNOR?! AMY?! SPARKY?! WHERE ARE YOU GUYS?!" Trey cried, rushing through the crowd.
Amy made herself scarce, while Connor approached.
"It's okay." he said, being sure to mask his voice. "No one died, and there's no one still in there. If your friends aren't here, then they're safe."
"I..." Trey stammered, looking up at the Werewolf. "Thank you."
Connor bowed his head, and returned to his pack.
"Robot. Spartacus." he began.
"We need much better codenames." she grinned.
"You're not getting a codename." Connor sighed. "Look... get to Riverside, and get out of that suit. I'm gonna go back to the flat and see what stuff I can salvage. We'll crash at Riverside until we can find a more permanent solution."
"Got it." she nodded. "I'll see you there."
Connor scaled back upwards, and entered through his window.

The flat was dark and smokey - its position on the fourth floor kept it safer than most, but the floor was unstable - as though a stiff breeze would knock down the whole tower. Water dripped in through the windowsill, splashing on the ground.
He sighed deeply. His home.
Then, he shook his head.
His 'home' was his friends. His family. The people of the city.
His pack.
And they were safe.
He went through, and let out a sigh of relief as he found their most important things had been left undamaged. His computer - the one he used for intel - still worked fine.
More importantly - a box. A box he kept under his bed, and barely ever moved.
He pulled it out, and gingerly opened it. One look was all he gave, before closing it again. He smiled softly, grabbed some toiletries, as well as a bag of Amy's clothes, and headed back out.

Amy stood in the shower, her muscles aching as she let the water run over her.
Thankfully, the shower water wasn't too cold - that is, until the hot water ran out.
She sighed, and turned it off, buffeting herself with a towel just as Connor walked in.
"Hey." she smiled. "What you got there?"
"My laptop." he began. "It's got all my intel on it. I also got this..."
He revealed the box, and opened it.
"Thank god it wasn't damaged..." Amy gasped in relief, pulling out some of the objects inside.
A photo of her, Connor, and Vince. She held it aloft, and beamed.
"I also got you some spare clothes." he nodded, handing the bag over.
"What about you?"
"I can morph new clothes if I need them." he shrugged.
"Yeah, but... if we're living here, you'd better keep some clothes here too, for appearance's sake."
"I'll go shopping tomorrow."
He hesitated. "Your guitar was ruined. I'm sorry."
"Ah, I hadn't used it in ages." she shrugged.
Connor looked at her softly.
"Don't do that."
"Do what?"
"Pretend to not be upset."
She sighed. "Okay, fine. What about you?"
He looked away, and sighed. "I... we did our best, today. No one died. That's a win."
She smiled slightly, and brushed his arm. "Anyway... I've just ordered pizza."
"Legend."

'Ordered pizza' was an understatement - Amy had got them a banquet.
Connor and Sparky greedily devoured their extra-large meat feasts, while she made her way through her BBQ chicken.
Their gorging was interrupted by a door swinging open.
"WHY THE *FUCK* HAVE YOU GUYS NOT BEEN ANSWERING YOUR PHONES?!"
"...oh, hey Trey." Connor said, swallowing a mouthful.
"Um... we've been a bit busy." Amy quickly added.
"Hi Trey!" Sparky yapped. "Do you want some pizza?"
Trey took a deep breath, and embraced them all. Then he sat down, and shamelessly robbed one of Connor's slices.
"Dick." Connor grinned cheekily.

"So... how come you guys are here?" Trey began.
"Our house burned down, dipshit." Amy snarked.
Trey rolled his eyes, as Connor grinned.
"Hotels don't let you bring dogs." he began. "And it's not like Tommy's gonna evict us. I grabbed some mattresses from next door, so we have something to sleep in until we can get something more permanent."
"Next door? You mean the old motel?" Trey cocked an eyebrow.
"It's not the first time I've stolen shit." Connor grinned. "I would say we just squat there, but there's no power or plumbing."
"Yeah, I'll take sleeping on a floor mattress over shitting in a bucket any day of the week." Amy shuddered.
"I guess it works as a good stopgap." Trey smiled. "But if you guys wanna crash round mine..."
Connor shrugged. "I don't mind it. This basement's way bigger than my room anyway. Besides, I've got no excuse not to take some shifts here, now."
Trey beamed. "Some honest work'll do you good."
He paused. "How come you guys weren't home?"
"We were out for a walk with Sparky." Amy covered. "By the time we got back, we saw the fires being put out. Connor snuck in, managed to grab the essentials, and we crashed here."
"You went in? Connor, the building could've been unstable!"
"I know..." he looked away. "But we needed our stuff."
Trey shrugged, before yawning and stretching.
"Don't worry about us." Connor smiled. "You can get yourself home."
"I've just messaged Bea and Chloe." Amy added. "We'll go out next week - once we're all settled in here."
"Sounds good." Trey smiled. "Listen, if you guys need anything..."
"I've ordered a bunch of furniture - it comes tomorrow." Connor nodded. "You could help me unpack it, if you like?"
"It's a date."
Connor grinned as Trey rose, and then hugged him before he left.
Trey took a deep breath, and smelled something... odd.
Something smokey.
"Nah..." he thought, dismissing it. He smiled, and left.

Connor sat down, and sighed.
"Want some more pizza?" Amy began.
"I'm good, I... I'd better get ready for patrol."
"Connor, it's been a hell of a day!" she said. "You can take a night off."
"The city's still a crime-ridden hellhole." he shook his head. "I don't get 'nights off.'"
Sparky stretched. "I'll take Caldermouth."
"Wait." Amy sighed. "You've always found... big upheavals to be difficult. Maybe just take some time to process it?"
Connor's lip curled slightly. "The Skinners don't stop mugging people because an autistic guy feels sorry for himself."
"No, I--I'm not saying that." she shook her head. "Just... you're allowed to take some time for yourself too."
"No, I'm not." he sighed. "I have a responsibility."
She gave a sad look, as they rose and headed out. Then, she absent-mindedly chewed on her pizza.

Connor soared through the skies of Drackhill, riding atop the winds. He let an updraft give him some altitude, and then slowly dragged himself over the air.
He was tired. It was a miserable night. But it was his duty.
He came to a stop on a rooftop overlooking his flat's block. He looked over at the husk of a tower, and sighed deeply.
Then, he saw it.
Three heartbeats. They were on the first floor.
They looked... familiar.
"You've gotta be fuckin' kidding me..." he snarled, before moving.
