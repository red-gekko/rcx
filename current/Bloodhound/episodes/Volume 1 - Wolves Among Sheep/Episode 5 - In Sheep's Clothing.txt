SCENE - Chloe at Uni

	If you asked someone to draw a kindly professor, they would draw Isaac Lowenthal.
	He was an old man, probably in his late 60's - and clearly, one who enjoyed teaching enough to stick it out for this long.
	His hair was unkempt, like Einstein - and his glasses, slightly too big for his face, clashed in a funny way with his small bow-tie.
	Chloe smiled, tuning her hearing aids to the classroom soundfield system. Despite the groaning of a Monday morning, she was in her element. She was finally starting to feel a little less of the pressure she had back in Sixth Form - that is, she wore a grey pullover hoodie under a blazer, rather than a button-up. Baby steps, after all.
	"So, class - H. I. V." he said, underlining each letter. "A true scourge of modern medicine. Our greatest weapon - the vaccine - powerless! Or, so we think."
	He smiled, and looked over the classroom.
	"I want you all to try and figure out how we could make a vaccine for HIV."
	Chloe looked up, confused. The rest of the class began writing away, or talking to each other. Chloe nervously walked over to Lowenthal, who had sat down at his desk - clearly happy to have stumped the class.
	"Um, excuse me, Professor Lowenthal?" she asked sheepishly, covering her hands with the cuffs of her jumper.
	"Yes, Ms. Derrington?" he smiled warmly.
	"Um... well, I'm a little confused - HIV is so dangerous because it targets the immune system itself - a vaccine wouldn't do anything - would it?"
	"Indeed!" he chuckled. "Don't worry, Ms. Derrington - the point of this exercise is not to break biology itself. I just want to see how you all think!"
	Chloe smiled, and nodded. "Right, of course - sorry to bother you."
	"Not at all - it's what they pay me for!" he laughed. Chloe returned to her desk, and thought deeply - googling, writing notes, and chewing her pencil absent-mindedly.
	After 30 minutes had passed, Lowenthal stood, and clapped his hands together, startling her.
	"Okay, class! Let's hear it. Who'd like to go first?"
	The room fell silent. Finally, one man stood up.
	"I've got an idea." he said.
	"Excellent!" Lowenthal smiled. "Let's hear it!"
	"Okay, so I think we just do what we did with cowpox and smallpox- introduce Simian Immunodeficiency Virus into the host, and get them to base their immunity off of that." he said confidently.
	Lowenthal smiled, trying to avoid looking patronising. "Well, Mr. Darvill, that's an interesting idea. Unfortunately, current understanding is that SIV-infected bushmeat was the initial cause of HIV. So, by implementing your idea, we may end up just making a new strain of HIV instead. But, A for effort!"
	He was trying hard to seem supportive. "Anyone else?"
	"Um, I have an idea!" one girl raised her hand.
	"Go ahead, Ms. Ross!" Lowenthal smiled.
	"Well, how about we take HIV virions, and deactivate them, before injecting the dead virions?" she asked.
	"Ah..." Lowenthal pinched the bridge of his nose, trying his best to not seem rude. "Well Ms. Ross, that is the standard way to induce immunity via vaccination. Unfortunately, HIV does not work with that method. HIV is unique because, unlike many other viruses, it requires a very specific interaction with immune cells - particularly T cells - to trigger an effective immune response. Simply using a dead virus may not stimulate a strong enough immune response, especially one that targets the virus effectively. And, live HIV is incredibly mutable. Anyone else?"
	Chloe's heart raced. She knew her answer was solid, but the thought of being wrong in front of everyone made her stomach churn. Still, she raised her hand.
	"Ah, yes, Ms. Derrington?" Lowenthal asked, smiling.
	"Ah, well, sir... um..." Chloe fumbled over her notes. She could hear quiet snickering in the background, but steeled herself.
	"I was thinking... hIV envelopes have specific conserved regions, yes? Regions that are unlikely to mutate without making the virus unviable?"
	"Go on?" Lowenthal asked, intrigued.
	"Well, those conserved regions would have antigens, right? And, well, antigens have antibodies matching them. I'm thinking, we introduce only those conserved region antigens, along with a bunch of matching monoclonal antibodies. Doing so, hopefully the body could learn to produce those antibodies. If that worked, you would only need to take ART maybe, only weekly or monthly? That'd be a godsend for people in more developing countries. Of course, the challenge would be isolating the conserved antigens and ensuring the monoclonal antibodies are broadly neutralising across HIV strains..." she continued.
	Darvill muttered under his breath "That's not a vaccine."
	Chloe paused, trembling.
	"Ms. Derrington..." Lowenthal began, awestruck. "Absolutely brilliant!"
	Chloe breathed a deep sigh of relief, and allowed herself a smile.
	"Amazing!" he continued. "That is a very good idea. You not only provoke an immune response, but actively improve care outcomes for the people in most need."
	Chloe smiled, and returned to her seat. Once the lecture finished, she grabbed her bag and went to leave, along with everyone else.
	"Oh, Chloe?" Lowenthal asked.
	"Sir?"
	"Do you have a minute?"
	"Yes, of course." Chloe smiled, sitting down.
	"I've been giving it some thought - I think it's a waste having you in this classroom." he said.
	"I... sorry, sir, I know the objective was to come up with a vaccine idea rather than treatment, but I just thought--" Chloe began.
	Lowenthal raised his hand. "Oh no, dear - your idea there was brilliant. No, I fear *you're* wasted in this lecture hall! You're far beyond the rest of the class."
	"Sir?" Chloe asked, still timid.
	"Have you ever heard of a company called 'Vitalis?'" Lowenthal asked. "They're a medical research company."
	"I... yes, sir, I've heard of them. Their head office is in Belson, isn't it?"
	"I'm going to put in the good word, and see if we can get you a work placement there." Lowenthal smiled.
	"You will?" Chloe gasped. "Sir, I... Vitalis is such a big, successful company - are you sure I'd be any good there?"
	"Absolutely." Lowenthal nodded. "The placement would be for three days a week. Working there will count towards your academic credits here, as well. And, you'll get paid!"
	"Sir, I..." Chloe thought for a moment. "I'd be honoured." she smiled.
	"Excellent." Lowenthal smiled back. "Take care now, Ms. Derrington."

SCENE - The Vigilante

	That night, in Belson, a group of men in Skinner leathers were rifling through some boxes. A man in a military uniform oversaw them.
	"And this'll make us like you?" one of the men asked.
	"More than that." the uniformed man replied. Bulging black veins jotted his eye sockets. "It'll bring our leader's vision to this city."
	Suddenly, the sound of glass shattering was heard. One of the Skinners drew out his knife. "Who's there?!"
	Then the lights cut out.
	There was the sound of punching, before the man in the uniform pulled out a flashlight and gazed upon their assailant.
	Connor rolled his shoulders, tugging his red hood tighter, and his bandana upwards. He looked less like a traumatised mutant - more like an urban vigilante.
	His silver eyes shifted to red as he stepped forwards - the pitch-black warehouse bright as day.
	The uniformed man didn't hesitate. He came out, swinging. Connor bent backwards to dodge, but just barely, as the punch grazed his ribs.
	His eyes widened. This one was different - superhuman. Like the assassin in the club.
	The two collided, fists flying with such force that each swing sliced the air audibly.
	The man in uniform matched Connor's speed and ferocity, their blows rattling the room.
	Connor blocked a punch, the impact reverberating up his arm, and countered with a hook that landed squarely on the man's jaw. The man staggered, but didn't fall.
	Connor flinched, as the urges swelled once more.
	He pressed forward, throwing a barrage of strikes, each one more calculated than the last.
	The man in uniform grinned, his black-veined eyes glinting in the flashlight's beam. He ducked under a punch and landed a knee to Connor's stomach, sending him skidding back a few steps.
	Pain bloomed in Connor's gut, but he straightened quickly, his expression hardening. He was stronger. He grinned.
	"LISTEN AND OBEY!" the man shouted.
	Connor froze for a split second, before regaining his focus. "FUCK YOU!" he screamed back.
	The uniformed man lunged again, but this time Connor sidestepped and landed a devastating elbow to the man's ribs.
	The crack of bone was audible. The man grunted in pain, but Connor didn't let up. He grabbed the man's arm, twisted it, and drove his knee into the side of the man's leg. The sound of it snapping filled the room.
	The man collapsed to one knee, snarling in agony. Connor followed with a brutal kick to the head, sending him sprawling onto the floor.
	Connor planted a boot on the man's chest, pinning him. His hand darted down, grabbing the man by the throat. His voice was low and dangerous as he growled, "Who are you people, and what did you do to me?!"
	The uniformed man stared back, defiantly. "Fuck you, traitor."
	Connor growled, and tightened his grip. "I don't mind killing freaks like you. *WHO ARE YOU?!*"
	The man drew a knife, aiming for Connor's side. Connor grabbed the knife and drove it into the man's throat.
	He gasped, and reached out, before expiring.
	Connor sighed, and stood. He exhaled sharply, and pulled down his bandana. Turning to the boxes, he waved the flashlight inside.
	Syringes. Dozens of them. His jaw tightened. He grabbed one, lit a match, and threw it into the box, burning the rest.
	He felt his stomach grumble, and looked down at the warm body below. He felt his hunger-pangs intensify - the same way they did with the deer.
	Disgusted with himself, he went outside. There, the bike sat ready for him. He'd modified it a little - tuning up the engine, taking off the reg plates - but most of all, when he hopped on and turned his keys in the ignition, the headlights - replaced with thin striplights that looked like squinting eyes - blazed red in the night.
	He took a deep breath, centring himself. His gloves rippled as the blood slipped away.
	The engine revved, and his helmet morphed back over his skull.
	He looked at his reflection in the waterlogged street, and couldn't help but grin.
	His eyes turned crimson behind his tinted visor, and they shone through the plastic, like LEDs in a glass bulb. His jaw clenched, and the bike roared as he tore off.

	It was dark and cold, now. The rain was even heavier.
	Connor came into Riverside through the front door, and removed his mask, before sighing and pulling out a notepad. He crossed off an item on a list, stepped back, and sighed again, before pulling out his phone.
	"Tommy, it's me."
	"Anything?"
	"No, nothing. Managed to find whatever he was pushing on 'em, though - some kind of syringe."
	"Probably the steroids Stoker was freaking about." Tommy shrugged. "How was the fight?"
	"Not as bad as last time." Connor began. "Only killed one person - he was enhanced, like me - but weaker. I didn't have much of a choice."
	"You had *no* choice. What about the others?"
	"They're hurt, but they'll live. I've, uh... I'm getting better at holding back."
	"That's good."
	"Yeah." Connor nodded. "Also, I took one of the syringes with me."
	"What for?"
	"Well, I figured it'd be good to look into it - once I find someone who can. Either way, no good leaving that shit on the streets."
	"Fair enough. Anyway, I got you a lead."
	"What lead?"
	"Marsh Road, Belson. Warehouse 5-2-59/B. Stoker is meeting with these freaks."
	Connor's eyes flashed with anticipation. "I'm on it."

SCENE - Stoker's Wounds

	The Laughing Fox was, for the first time in a while, not heaving with human bodies. A stone's throw from the bar, Trey and Amy were hanging out in a pub, playing pool.
	Amy potted a stripe, and cursed.
	"Two shots for me, thank you very much." Trey grinned.
	"Eat shit." Amy smirked.
	Trey chuckled, and shot - immediately potting the black.
	"FUCK!" he exclaimed. Amy burst into laughter.
	The two chuckled, and sat down at their table.
	They sipped their drinks, and paused.
	"So..." Trey began, "Elephant that's not in the room."
	"He's not that fat." Amy grinned.
	Trey chuckled. "Still... I'm worried about him, Amy. He goes missing for a week, and suddenly he's in deep with the Hoods again?"
	"I know." she shrugged. "Still... working with Tommy helps focus him, I think."
	"Don't tell me you're changing your tune." Trey tilted his head. "You can't tell me you're okay with him being back in it?"
	"I'm not." she shook her head. "But he's been better since rejoining. I..." she hesitated, "I don't know what's been going on with him since he went missing, but he was struggling really bad, until he got back with the Hoods. So, I guess I'm not gonna look a gift horse in the mouth."
	"I'll message him. We'll throw a Halloween party at mine?" Trey asked. "Just because he's back in doesn't mean I'm gonna let him isolate himself."
	Amy smiled softly. "Yeah, that's... that's a good idea."
	Neither would admit it, but both were imagining what kind of trouble he was in.
	"Maybe he's delivering drugs..." Amy shuddered as she thought.
	"Maybe he's stealing from the Skinners..." Trey pondered to himself, his heart quickening.
	"Maybe he's killing." neither even dared to consider.

	In Belson, Stoker and a couple of his goons were meeting two men in military uniforms. Connor was hidden behind a couple of boxes, listening in.
	"You disappoint us, Stoker." one of the uniformed men began.
	"You've been taking from us." the other man snarled.
	"Listen, guys... this is all one big misunderstanding." Stoker grinned. "We're supposed to be partners, right? So if you're bringing guns and 'roids into my streets, I get a cut. It's just business."
	"'Roids?'" the first man chuckled, the veins around his eyes darkening.
	"You're on our turf, spook." a Skinner chuckled. "You pay your taxes, or get the fuck out."
	The man growled, pulling out a silenced pistol and shooting both goons in the head.
	"Oh, shit!" Stoker shrieked, before another bullet sank into his gut.
	"Keep him alive - the boss'll want a word." the second chuckled.
	Connor gasped, giving away his position.
	"Get him!" the first man barked.
	Connor didn't hesitate. He leapt over the boxes, moving with inhuman speed, and grabbed the armed man in a headlock. With a twist of his body, he pivoted, using his momentum to snap the man's neck in one smooth motion. The body crumpled, lifeless.
	The second man reacted quickly, grabbing a crowbar from a nearby crate and swinging it with brutal force.
	The first hit slammed into Connor's shoulder, rocking the bone slightly. Despite his assailant's superhuman strength, it didn't hurt as much as he thought it would.
	The second hit, however, was much more in line with expectations. It landed on the back of Connor's head. His vision cut out for a nanosecond, as blood shot out of his nose and mouth. He smacked into the ground, prone, before rolling onto his back.
	Another hit. This time, right in the face. It made a sickening crack as it landed on his brow, splitting the skin. Then, a new sensation overwrote the agony in his head.
	He felt his clothes shudder and shake, before reforming. They took the image of armour - thick, and calcified. It covered his face just in time for another smack - this one hurting much less than before.
	The suit then thinned, as the biomass forced itself back into his veins, invigorating him. His hand shot up, catching the crowbar before it could hit him again, and he sent a sharp kick to the man's midsection.
	He twisted his way up, moving more like a breakdancer than a street thug, and swung the crowbar in a wide arc, smashing it into the man's face. The impact sent him stumbling, blood pouring from his shattered nose.
	As the man stumbled, Connor felt his muscles shudder and tremble.
	Before he could recover, Connor swept the man's legs, sending him into the air. Then, he flipped the crowbar around to its sharper end, and drove it through the man's ribcage. It hit the ground as his body did, digging itself into the floor. His body jerked once, then went still.

	Connor stood over him, his breath ragged. For a moment, he shuddered, staring at the blood on his hands and the bodies around him.
	His migraine faded, replaced with a certain euphoria.
	Shaking it off, he turned to Stoker, who was slumped against a crate, groaning in pain.
	"Why are you meeting with these freaks, Stoker?" Connor asked, preparing to interrogate him. Thankfully, Stoker was a coward in pain, so he spilled.
	"Th-they've been bringing steroids into the city! They're trying to take over!" he relayed, panicking.
	"DO NOT LIE TO ME!"
	"I'm not! I swear!"
	Connor took a deep breath. Somehow... he knew Stoker wasn't lying.
	An idea.
	"Who do they work for?!"
	"I don't know!"
	The hairs on the back of his neck tingled slightly.
	A lie.
	Connor smacked the metal by the side of Stoker's head. It dented.
	"NEXT TIME, THAT'S YOUR JAW!"
	"Okay, okay! Donovan! He's a jumped-up little cunt - but he's got his hands on their drug. He's trying to take over the Skinners! They wanna kill me!"
	Truth. This one smelled of truth, somehow.
	"Why aren't they working with you? Why stage a coup?"
	"I don't know-AGHHH!"
	A strike to the jaw, and Connor snarled.
	"Lie to me again, and I'll start pulling teeth."
	"Alright, alright! I've lost the gang's respect, okay?! Ever since that little Barrett cunt stole my money and trashed my coke..."
	A grin cracked across Connor's face.
	"Where are they getting their supply from? Guns and drugs like that must be coming in from the docks - who?!"
	"Dobel! Christian Dobel. He's the connection, he's hired the Disciples to move the product. But there are others, too!"
	"Where can I find them?" he snarled.
	"I don't know... I don't... I need..." Stoker hyperventilated, before passing out.
	Connor sighed, and looked up.
	He whispered "Why am I going to do this?"
	Then his mind flashed to the men he killed - the ones who deserved it just like Stoker, but that he had no right to kill.
	He looked over at the fallen mutants, and then back down.
	A deep breath came out, as he pulled out the Coagulator.
	"Pretty good test run, I guess." he shrugged, before spraying the coagulating foam into his wounds.
	The foam hardened quickly, plugging Stoker's bleeding veins. The numbing cream within them soothed his pain, as Connor sewed up the larger holes. He grimaced at the reality - that being a hero instead of a monster sometimes meant saving creatures like *him.*
	Finally, he pulled out his phone.
	"999, what emergency service do you require?" the phone operator said calmly.
	"Hello, yes, I need an ambulance, I've had an accident and I'm bleeding out. I'm at Marsh Road, Belson, Warehouse 5-2-59/B. Goodbye." he said in an imitation of Stoker's voice, before hanging up.
	He looked at himself in a metal sheet, observing his new costume.
	It had changed from the street thug's gear to an edgy superhero getup. Instead of the chitinous exoskeleton or a thick jacket and hoodie, he was encased in a smooth black bodysuit - one that covered all of his skin and accentuated his muscles. His red hood had turned black, integrating into the suit's collar. His whole head was covered in a skintight black mask - and his motorbike visor had contracted into tinted lenses over his eyes. The mask's material moved with his face - letting him scowl, cock a brow, or even offer a gentle look.
	It completely masked his appearance - not even a close friend would recognise him. It was like someone had married spandex and kevlar, and moulded it into a ninja suit. The red eyes burned in the dark.
	"Okay..." he smirked. "This will come in handy."
	Then, he felt a final shudder - liquid oozed from his wrist. A pair of prongs formed out from behind the hands, and hardened into blades.
	He held his hand up as it caught the light, and smirked again.
	"But not as much as that."

SCENE - Meeting Ivan

	Outside now, Connor took a deep breath. But before he could exhale...
	*phit-WHACK!*
	He felt his leg buckle. He looked down and saw a bullet had torn right through his thigh and landed in the ground below.
	In less than a microsecond, he realised the direction of fire, and darted for cover behind a dumpster. A second bullet narrowly missed him, grazing his arm.
	Then, the pain hit. He clutched the entry and exit wounds, and his eyes flashed bright red for a second, before returning to their normal steel-grey.
	He stuck his head out, but immediately ducked as a bullet whizzed overhead. He spotted a crack in the wall he was covered by, and peeked through that.
	A sniper, perched on a rooftop above him. His outfit was similar to Connor's - that is, all-black, and perfect for stealth. He wore a bulky kevlar jumpsuit, and a sinister-looking respirator mask that completely concealed his face. Its smooth, skull-like shell was matte black, with a raised seam running down the centre of the forehead. Two sunken eye sockets housed a pair of bright red lenses. The lower half was an intricate assembly of overlapping metal plates and reinforced filters, with a large central breathing apparatus flanked by two protruding cylindrical canisters. Circular housings covered either side of his head, completing its imposing, almost inhuman appearance.
	"Tsentral'nyy, eto Verdikt." the sniper reported. "Kontakt - GROM-1. Vedu boy."
	Connor shuddered. No choice.
	He leapt out from cover, sprinting in a zig-zag pattern. The sniper's bullets whizzed past him, and one found its mark in his gut. It carved through his intestines, and tore out of his back, taking chunks of flesh with it.
	Undaunted, he sprinted on, the thrill of the fight masking his agony. He dived through a ground-floor window into the building the sniper had perched on.
	"Chyort voz'mi..." he heard him mutter. He sprinted up the stairs, the sniper sprinted down. They met in a stairwell.
	The sniper aimed his rifle, revealing an under-slung shotgun, and unloaded both barrels into Connor's chest.
	He flew backwards, crashing into the wall behind him. He struggled to his feet, his eyes flashing red again.
	"Bozhe moy!" the sniper shuddered, scrambling to reload.
	Before he could, Connor grabbed the gun, and effortlessly yanked it from his hands. His own fists morphed into claws, and he lifted the shooter up by the throat.
	"WHO ARE YOU PEOPLE?!" he screamed. "WHAT DID YOU DO TO ME?!"
	"Idi na khuy!" the sniper spat, kicking wildly.
	Connor tightened his grip.
	"Tell me who you people are, *right now* - or I'll pop your head like a grape."
	The sniper scowled. "I'll gladly die... before I give anything to your masters, fascist." he sputtered.
	Connor snarled, then released his grip. Dropping his bravado, the sniper choked and spat, catching his breath. He pulled off his balaclava, revealing black receding hair and a thick beard.
	"The fuck are you talking about? You're the ones who are working with these cunts!"
	"Ty chto?!" he pulled his head back incredulously. "Wait a minute... you're *not* with those freaks?"
	"I just killed three of them this evening, you prick!" Connor growled in his face. "And I'll happily kill a fourth, if you don't start talking!"
	Realisation dawned on his face. He grinned, and brushed himself off, before offering his hand out.
	"Ivan Sokolov. 'Verdikt' to my friends. I'm KGB - or what's left of it. Apologies for the... introduction, but we thought you were with them."
	Connor tilted his head, not taking the hand. He unmorphed his mask to reveal his face. "Why would I be with them? They shot me in the head and turned me into a monster!"
	"Da, and we thought they'd got your soul, too." Ivan nodded. "We know this... cabal, they have brainwashing technology. We thought they'd filled your head with all kinds of *khernya.* But if you're fighting them, it clearly didn't work on you. By now, they would have said your keywords."
	Connor shuddered. "They... they said 'Listen and Obey' to me a couple times. It makes me... remember things... but nothing more."
	Ivan stroked his beard. "Then their conditioning didn't take too deep a root. Good, very good. How's your leg? And your gut?"
	"I'll live." Connor chuckled grimly. "Shoot me again, and you're dead."
	Ivan laughed. "Fair enough, tovarishch."
	He paused.
	"You want to take the fight to these bastards?"
	Connor nodded.
	"Then follow me."
	"To where?"
	"I have a mission for you." Ivan began. "You, me, and a warehouse occupied by these freaks. We go in, loud - then we take the stuff they have."
	"What stuff?" Connor asked, raising an eyebrow.
	"Don't know - but secret, military shit."
	"How far's the drive?"
	"Half an hour. Why?"
	"Because some Russian bastard shot me a bunch, and I need some time to regenerate." Connor laughed.
	Ivan chuckled, and the pair headed downstairs.
	"You need a lift?"
	"I've got one." Connor motioned to his bike. Ivan nodded.
	Connor stood in the alleyway for a moment, deep in thought. His bodysuit shifted back to the red hoodie and black jacket, and his mask reformed into his bike helmet.
	"The KGB..." he muttered. "What the fuck..."

SCENE - Saving Sparky and Ivan

	"You know how to use?"

	Connor looked down, and his gaze was met by adorable puppy-eyes. He sighed, grinned, and gave Sparky a small chunk of food, which vanished in an instant.
	"Don't make a habit of this."
	"Can I have another?"
	"No."
	Connor finished his, then made Sparky a bodybuilder's shake.
	"Here - try this." he said, pouring it into a bowl.
	Sparky virtually inhaled it, then smiled.
	"I like that! Can I please have another?"
	Connor grinned, then made them both a shake each.
	"Now, that's enough for tonight." he said semi-firmly.
	"Okay. Can we have food tomorrow, please?" Sparky asked innocently.
	"Wha--Of course!" Connor nodded quickly, before guiltily realising. "Look, if you're ever genuinely hungry and not just being greedy, just tell me, and I'll get you food, okay?"
	"Okay! Thanks, Connor!" Sparky smiled warmly.
	Connor reached down and stroked him, before yawning.
	"We'd better get some rest. I don't know about you, but I'm knackered." he grinned.
	"Do I have to sleep in a cage?" Sparky asked, trembling slightly.
	"No way." Connor shook his head firmly. "You can sleep wherever you want."
	"Can I sleep next to you?"
	"Of course."
	He put down the laptop, and curled up on the sofa, next to his new packmate. The pair drifted into unconsciousness.
