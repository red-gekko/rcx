SCENE - It Wasn't Me/Blackout

	The morning sun was slowly starting to rise, now. It was the early hours, and Naismith groaned as he left his front door. He sat in his car, and took a deep breath.
	"You gonna sit there all morning, or?"
	"Detective." Connor chuckled, leaning forward. "Are we friends now? Or do you just say that every time you get in the car?"
	"Jimmy Boyle showed up in A&E the other night. Broken legs. Says he tripped and fell out of his balcony."
	"Yeah, and if he changes his story, he might trip and fall again."
	"Was it you who killed all those people?"
	Connor shot him a look - Naismith couldn't help but think he looked hurt.
	"Don't play silly buggers. Was it or wasn't it?"
	"It wasn't." Connor nodded firmly.
	"I knew it..." Naismith breathed out in relief. He started the car, and pulled off.
	"Is that praise, Detective?"
	"More like being happy knowing I'm not aiding and abetting a terrorist." Naismith leaned back.
	"Just a vigilante, then." Connor quipped. "Besides, you don't think it's fishy how city hall blows up, and Norris survives barely-scathed?"
	"I had my worries." Naismith shrugged. "It's election night tonight. Norris has managed to dominate the headlines. All those people... dead, for a boost in the polls?"
	"That's the Optimates for you." Connor growled. "But it's more than that."
	"How'd you mean?"
	"I'm getting closer." Connor nodded. "Gambit. The child trafficking. Hughes. Project Jotunn. ODIN. They need to distract me, and turn the public against me, all in one fell swoop."
	Naismith was quiet. Then, after a while, he went to speak - and stopped, as a clunking noise filled the air.
	"What was that?" he cocked an eyebrow.
	Connor sniffed, and sighed.
	"Shit..." he groaned, as the street lights went out. Then, the houses.
	"Blackout." Naismith sighed. "Don't tell me..."
	His phone buzzed, and he took it.
	"Breaking news." he read. "Mass power outages across the valley. Ah... you wanna see this."
	He played a video, and Connor's eyes flashed.
	"People of Drackhill!" the imposter began. "Forgive me for indulging myself, but some metaphors are just too good. You... you're all *humans. *Mortals.* The only man in this valley with *power...* is *me.*"
	"I don't sound like that. Do I?" Connor tilted his head.
	"*I* don't *know.* *You* don't *speak* with so much *gravitas,* do you? *I* think it sounds *stupid* when people *talk* like *that.*" Naismith snarked.
	"Oh, Jesus. Detective, I think we might actually be friends now. That sucks." Connor quipped back. "The speech patterns... they sound artificial."
	"Definitely a deepfake, then..." Naismith sighed.
	"I'd better get going." Connor nodded.
	"Wait..." Naismith began hesitantly. "I... I *cannot* believe I'm saying this., but... if you need my help exposing them..."
	"I do." Connor nodded. "Here's the plan. I'll keep working on who's behind all this. Any intel I find, you'll be first to know. And as soon as we can be sure who's behind this... we take 'em down."
	He handed over something - a burner phone.
	"You can contact me on this - and likewise."
	Naismith hesitated. He wanted to take it, but... once he crossed that line, he was no longer a victim of home invasions receiving tip-offs. He was involved.
	He steeled himself, and pocketed it.
	"I'll see you around, Werewolf."
	"In a while, Detective."

	Shannon was three hours deep into the deepfake files when the lights went out.
	Not just the basement lights. Everything. The hum of the servers, the glow of the monitors, the faint buzz of the radiator in the corner - all of it died at once.
	"The fuck?!" she gasped.
	Her laptop screen flickered, held on for a moment, then went black. The battery would last another few hours, but without the servers, she was blind.
	Then her phone buzzed - a text from Trey.
	"Power's out across the whole city. What the fuck is going on?"
	She gulped, then her brows creased. She rushed over to the emergency generator, and it choked to life.
	The servers flickered, along with the lights. In seconds, she was back on the laptop, and quickly started running diagnostics.
	Damn it. The whole valley was down.
	Every municipal system - traffic, water, emergency services - all gone.
	Or rather - every substation supplying power to those systems was down.
	She shook her head.
	"This doesn't just *happen...*"
	She pulled up her terminal, and started probing the network.
	There had to be a trace. A thread to pull. There always was.
	There. A compromised SCADA controller at a substation in the industrial district. The attackers had used a zero-day exploit - one she had kept in the back pocket for emergencies - to bypass the authentication protocols and inject malicious code into the system.
	She raced through her system, her hands dancing over the keyboard.
	"Vanguard, this is Base." she began. "I'm working on the blackout. I need you to get to your nearest substation, and see if you can access one of their terminals."
	"On it." Connor audibly nodded through his glide. "I'm close by anyway."
	She cracked her knuckles, and got back to typing.
	What to do, what to do?
	A bruteforce? No... that'd take too long.
	Ah... this'll be fun.
	She was used to playing this game as the attacker. But hell, she *loved* a challenge.
	She pulled up her copy of the exploit, and decompiled the SCADA firmware. Then, she opened up her text editor, and her fingers moved like a pianist.
	It took her fifteen minutes to write. Another five to test it in a sandboxed environment. Then she deployed it.
	It was pretty simple, all things considered - a script that would identify the malicious code the attackers used, isolate it, then restore the original firmware version.
	An ugly solution - nothing stopped the attackers from trying again. But, it'd have to do.
	The script ran. For a moment, nothing happened. Then, one by one, the substations began to come back online.
	Shannon allowed herself a small smile.
	"Hey, Base." Connor reported. "You're in."
	"Nice one. I'll see if I can get a copy of their virus."
	Then, her screen flickered again.
	"INTRUSION DETECTED."
	Shit.
	Someone was in her system.
	She slammed her fingers against the keyboard, pulling up her intrusion detection logs. There - a backdoor she hadn't seen before.
	Damn it. How did she miss that?
	Oh yeah, that's why. She didn't need it.
	She grinned, as she quickly executed the KGB's countermeasures. Her laptop screen spluttered, then went black. Then it came back to life, running an entirely different OS.
	She accessed the command line, and - there. The connection logs.
	She chuckled, before accessing the address.
	"Gotcha."
	It was an obvious proxy server - one in Brazil, by the looks of it. She impersonated a friendly device, and waltzed in like she owned it.
	Then she followed the link. And again. And again.
	Boom. Right there - a computer in---oh, shit.
	"Drackhill? Come on, guys - that's just sloppy."
	Ah - the location was anonymous. All she got was that it was somewhere in the valley.
	Oh well. No fun otherwise.
	There - the exploit they used.
	She copied the file, and pored through it.
	Every hacker had a signature. A way of writing code, a pattern of movement, a tell. And this one - this one was familiar.
	Shannon's breath caught. She pulled up the metadata from the deepfake analysis, comparing it to the code she'd just captured. The syntax was identical.
	The attack came from the same device as the deepfakes.
	Same person. Same software. Same everything.
	All she needed now was access.
	She ran a passive bruteforce - it'd be slow, but reliable.
	"Vanguard - this is Base. The others have gone to Raj's - power's back online. Meet up with us there."
	"Copy that." Connor smiled. "Nice work."

SCENE - Deepfakes
