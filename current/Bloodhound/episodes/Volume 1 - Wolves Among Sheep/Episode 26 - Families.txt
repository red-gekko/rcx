SCENE - Chloe meets Sveta

	The year was 2009. A 35-year-old woman stepped off of the bus, and walked towards a big, fancy house. Her shoulder-length black hair was tucked neatly into a ponytail, framing her pale skin and deep brown eyes that shone with quiet determination. Her right arm was bandaged, with another bandage poking out from her shirt onto the base of her neck. She took a deep breath and rang the doorbell.
	After a short moment, a middle-aged man answered.
	"Good morning, Mr. Derrington! I, uh, am here for job interview?" she asked, in a thick Russian accent.
	"Ah, right, hello! Ms. Popova, wasn't it?" the man smiled. He shook her hand. "Please, call me William."
	"Pleased to meet you, William." she nodded graciously. "Call me Sveta." William invited her in.
	"So, as we said on the phone, the job is primarily elder care." William explained. "My father, Oliver, suffers from Alzheimer's Disease. While you're working, your priority will be to take care of him."
	Sveta nodded deferentially. "Would I be able to see Mr. Oliver?"
	"Of course." William smiled, walking her to a downstairs bedroom. There, an old man in a wheelchair watched television.
	"Dad?" William said loudly. Oliver turned his face, and smiled.
	"Oh, hello!" he said jovially. "Who's this?"
	"Good morning, Mr. Derrington!" Sveta said, speaking slowly so that her accent was easier for him to hear. "How are you feeling today?"
	"Are you the new nurse?" Oliver asked, smiling. "Lovely to meet you. What's your name?"
	"You can call me Sveta." she smiled. "Can I check your blood pressure, sir?"
	"Absolutely." Oliver nodded. Sveta smiled warmly, and pulled a monitor from her bag. She fixed the strap to his arm, and looked at the screen.
	"Where are you from?" Oliver asked. William cringed slightly.
	"I'm from Russia." she replied, her smile faltering slightly. "I used to live in Moscow."
	"I went to Moscow not long ago... would have been in the 70s, I think." Oliver smiled nostalgically.
	"I would have been a little girl." Sveta smiled. "It was beautiful back then."
	"Where did you get those bandages?" he asked. William again cringed.
	Sveta paused for a moment, before answering. "Oh, just a small scrape. I'll be right as rain."
	She finished her checks, and rose. "From what I can tell, your father is in good physical health, all things considered. What is his current medication plan?"
	William smiled. "He takes insulin, hydroxychloroquine, alendronate, and hydrocortisone - all to deal with his VS--Ah, sorry, I should explain--"
	Sveta raised her hand. "No need. Everyone in Russia my age knows all about Volkov."
	"Oh really?" William tilted his head.
	"When they first discovered it in the 80s, there was a big scare in the Narodnyy komiss--Apologies." she cleared her throat. "In the Ministry of Health. There was a big push to get everyone tested. That's why we know about the carrier/active distinction."
	William raised his eyebrows, impressed. "Well then, I'll defer to your authority on the matter." he chuckled. "His is elder-onset, anyway. As you'll know from the medicine plan, it mainly manifests as endocrine and orthopaedic. My daughter has Congenital VS. She's good at looking after herself, though." he smiled proudly.
	Sveta bowed her head respectfully. "What are her symptoms, if you don't mind my asking?"
	"Mainly neurological. Epilepsy, partial deafness, short-sightedness... and a very strong stutter. And of course, the standard diabetes." he replied. "Anyway, my wife is currently away on business." he said, somewhat bitterly. "My children are in their rooms. Shall we?"
	Sveta nodded, and the two headed upstairs.
	"This is my son's room." William began. He knocked on the door. "Victor!" he called.
	Victor - a skinny boy of 12 years - opened the door. He sighed.
	Sveta smiled softly. Her little Alexei would've been around the same age.
	"What do you want?" he asked.
	"Victor, this is Svetlana. She'll be grandad's nurse. Svetlana, this is Victor." William sighed.
	"Pleasure to meet you, Victor. How are you today?" she asked, holding out her hand.
	"What? Do-you-speak-English?" Victor mockingly chimed.
	"VICTOR!" William hissed. "Go to your room!"
	"I am in my room." Victor snarked.
	"Then stay in it!" William snapped. Victor slammed his door, and William rubbed his temples.
	"I am so, so sorry... he's not normally like this, I swear."
	Sveta chuckled. "It's okay. My son was just as sarcastic."
	William smirked, reassured. "How old is he? Are you able to work overnight at all?"
	She took a deep breath - almost pained. "Um--It's not a problem, I... I can work whenever."
	He felt his phone buzz.
	"Ah, apologies - I have to take this." he said, rushing down the stairs.
	Sveta exhaled, and smiled. She looked over, and saw Chloe standing timidly in her doorway. She was 12 years old. Her freckles were even more prominent than the present day, and her face was still unscarred. She nervously held her elbows.
	"Good morning! My name is Sveta. What's yours?" she asked, kneeling down to her level.
	"I--I'm C-Chloe." she smiled shyly.
	"Lovely to meet you, Chloe. How are you today?"
	"I-I'm o-ok-okay." she stammered, blinking as she got the words out.
	"Are you doing anything interesting?" Sveta asked.
	"N-no. J-just h-homework."
	"Can I see?" Sveta asked. She cracked a cheeky grin. "I'm new here. I bet your school teaches you things I have no idea about!"
	Chloe smiled, and opened the door, revealing her homework.
	"Ah, chemistry." Sveta smiled warmly. "You are good with science, yes?"
	"A-a little." Chloe admitted. "I pr-prefer b-biology to ch-chemistry."
	"Wonderful." Sveta beamed. "I studied biology when I trained to become a nurse."
	"You're a n-nurse?"
	"Da, yes!" she smiled. "I was a nurse in the army back home."
	"Wow." Chloe looked up, amazed. "C-can g-girls join the army?"
	"In Russia, they can." Sveta smiled, crossing her legs to sit down comfortably.
	"Why d-did you come here?" Chloe asked, fascinated. "Was the army too b-boring?"
	Sveta chuckled softly. "Yes, very boring. I decided I'd have much more fun coming here to learn chemistry."
	Chloe giggled. She passed Sveta a periodic table.
	"C-can you test me? Ask me a n-number and I'll try to say the n-name." she asked.
	Sveta smiled warmly. "Of course! Let's see... 30."
	"Zinc." Chloe recited instantly.
	Sveta looked at her, impressed. "85."
	Chloe paused for a moment. "Oh! Asta-t-tine!"
	Sveta raised her eyebrows in amazement. "Okay... 12?"
	"M-magnesium!" Chloe giggled happily, then caught herself and looked down, cheeks flushed. "Sorry, I j-just... it's f-f--" she blinked, forcing the words out, "--fun."
	"Don't apologise, dorogaya!" Sveta brushed her shoulder.
	"Dorogaya?" Chloe tilted her head. Despite having heard the word for the first time, she didn't stutter.
	Sveta smiled warmly. "'Moya dorogaya' means 'my dear' in Russian."
	Chloe blushed. "How can I say 'Th-thank you' in Russian?"
	Sveta beamed. "You would say 'Spasibo' - and then I say 'Pozhaluysta' which means 'You're welcome.'"
	Chloe nodded. "Spa-Spasibo."
	"Pozhaluysta, moya dorogaya!" Sveta replied warmly. "Okay... 16?"
	"Sulphur! C-come on, that one's easy!" Chloe giggled happily.
	Sveta smiled warmly. "Okay then... 115!"
	Chloe thought long and hard.
	"Not B-Bismuth... not Uranium..."
	Then, her face lit up. "M-Moscovium!"
	Sveta laughed proudly. "You're a genius."
	Chloe played with her hair shyly. "Just a g-good mem-memory."
	Sveta smiled, and leant back. "I'll tell you something - I only picked it because it's my home town."
	"You're from M-Moscow?"
	"Originally, yes." she beamed.
	Chloe looked up in awe. Then, a knock on the door.
	"Sorry about that, Ms. Popova!" William entered.
	"No trouble at all, Mr. Derrington." Sveta nodded graciously. "Chloe here was teaching me all about the periodic table."
	Chloe blushed, and smiled.
	William beamed with pride. "Well, that settles it. Can you start working next Monday?"
	"Absolutely." Sveta smiled warmly. She rose, and picked up her bag.
	"See you on M-monday, S-Sveta!" Chloe beamed.
	"See you Monday, Chloe." Sveta nodded, smiling back.
	Chloe waved as Sveta reached the door, then hesitated. "Th-thank you for t-talking to me. Spa--spasibo."
	Sveta turned, softened by the earnestness in the girl's voice. "Vsegda pozhaluysta. That means, 'You are always welcome.', dorogaya."
	Chloe smiled. "V-Vsegda p-pozhaluysta." she repeated, pronouncing it surprisingly well. Her stutter softened as the words left her lips, slow and steady.
	Sveta smiled proudly, then stepped out, closing the door behind her.

	Connor's eyes moved rightwards, and rested on a large oil painting. There, a man with the same facial shape stood, dressed in a three-piece suit. A top hat rested on his head, while one hand held a cane, and the other rested in his tweed jacket pocket. A monocle fitted nearly over his left eye.
	"Boo." Chloe said, grinning. Connor turned, and chuckled.
	"Sorry." she laughed. "Couldn't resist."
	"You've been hanging out with our lot for too long." he smirked. "Who's he?"
	"My great-great-great grandfather." she nodded. "Lord Edmund Derrington."
	"Lord?" Connor tilted his head. "Damn."
	Chloe chuckled, and led him to a second painting. This one was of a tuxedoed man, stood next to a woman in an extravagant white dress.
	"Lord Anthony Derrington, and Adelaide, Princess Royal." she gestured. "My grandad's parents. This was painted on the night of their wedding."
	"That sounds like the most boring wedding night ever." Connor snarked.
	Chloe giggled. "That's the aristocracy for you. Honestly, I love it in Drackhill. There's so much more... freedom, I guess. Less pressure to conform."
	Connor nodded softly, and gently brushed her hand. She blushed.
	"I'm sorry about earlier... I just, I'm fed up with her. She acts like she's the mother from heaven and I'm the daughter from hell."
	"It's okay. Really, it is. Besides, like I said, my uncle's in prison. It's not like I can sit in judgement on other people's family dynamics."
	Chloe chuckled. "Well, at least your uncle sounds alright. Hey, speaking of... last night, you said there's the law, and then there's right and wrong. I... I didn't mean to imply that I was judging you and your uncle." she said, looking down shyly.
	"Oh? I didn't even think of that - it's okay." Connor smiled. Chloe looked relieved.
	"That's good." she smiled. "I was worried that it might come off as some... I don't know, like I was trying to insult you in the subtext? Which I wasn't, to be clear." she stammered.
	"Oh, god, don't do that - you wanna insult me, your best bet is just saying it openly. You know me and subtext." Connor chuckled. Chloe smirked.
	"Hah, forgot about that. I'll bear that in mind." she said. "To be honest, it's refreshing."
	"Oh?"
	"Well... I don't have to worry about subtext with you. I know that when you say something, there's no hidden meaning. And I don't have to worry about how you'll take something I say, either." she explained.
	"I guess being autistic as all hell has some advantages." Connor grinned.
	Both laughed, enjoying the moment.
	"Do I need to call your grandad 'my Lord,' or something?"
	She chuckled. "No. I mean, he *is* a Lord, but he never cared for it. He's on medical leave from Parliament, at the moment. When he... when the time comes, it'll pass down to my dad, and then one day, me. The Barony of Derrington is absolute primogeniture instead of male-preference, meaning I'm the expected heir."
	"Damn." Connor raised his eyebrows. "You gonna take it?"
	"I... I don't know how much of a say I get." she admitted. "But, if I get the choice, I'll pass. There are quite a few paths I'd be happy to see my life go down, but being even more enmeshed in the aristocracy is definitely not one of them."
	"Hey, I'll take it. Except, Baron Barrett sounds silly." he quipped.
	She giggled, and leaned back - relaxed, a little.
	"Hey, before you head off - there's something I wanna show you." she said. "It's a short walk away - I don't suppose you have any boots?"
	"Yeah, in the car." he nodded. "I'll grab Sparky, and meet you outside?"
	She smiled, and he headed into the garden. There, Sparky was basking in the cold sunlight.
	"Morning, buddy." he smiled.
	"Morning!" Sparky yapped. "Are we heading home, now?"
	"Yeah, in a minute." Connor nodded. "Chloe's gonna show us something, though."
	"Oh, okay." Sparky yawned, rising. Connor morphed on a pair of boots, and they headed into the driveway.

	The walk was fairly uneventful - the three were still tired, and enjoyed the cold air waking them. Chloe held Molly on a leash, while Sparky trotted to their heels.
	"Hah..." she chuckled. "Funny how the wolfdog is more well-behaved. If she was off lead right now, she'd be spending the next three hours stuck in a rabbit hole."
	"Ah, it's a size thing. Rabbits aren't nutritious enough for Sparky." Connor grinned. "If there was a horse here, then maybe."
	"What's a 'horse?'" Sparky tilted his head.
	"He doesn't chase horses, does he?" she turned quickly.
	"Hm? Oh, no - I was taking the piss." Connor shrugged.
	Chloe laughed, and they kept walking.
	It didn't take too long before they arrived at the pasture. She knelt down, and unleashed Molly, who was wagging her tail in anticipation.
	"Bucephalus!" she called out.
	Connor cocked an eyebrow, before a bellowing neigh cried out, and the thundering of hooves filled the air. A small shelter in the corner of the pasture shook with movement, and a massive black stallion charged out.
	"Oh." Sparky tilted his head. "That's a big deer."
	Connor stifled a chuckle, and bowed his head. Bucephalus raced over to Chloe, and pushed his head into her chest. She planted her boots in the mud as he pushed her backwards, before nuzzling.
	"Hey, Bucey!" she cooed. "How have you been, baby boy?"
	He nickered as he rubbed against her, and then took a look at Connor.
	"Hey, buddy." he smiled, approaching towards an angle. Bucephalus tilted his head to the side, keeping his eye on Connor. Connor averted his gaze, and raised his hand up softly, towards the horse's mouth.
	"This is Connor." Chloe said softly. "Say hi!"
	Bucephalus moved forward slightly, and nickered again as he sniffed Connor's hand.
	"Good boy." Connor whispered, gently rubbing the withers. Molly yipped at his heels, and then started sniffing Bucephalus's rear. Connor went to grab her, but relaxed as the horse seemed unbothered by the fuss - he wasn't much of a kicker, it seemed.
	"You're good with him." Chloe noted, beaming.
	"My cousin Mickey knew a fella with a very similar horse." Connor explained. "He's not a pure Shire, is he?"
	"Oh of course, I remember you mentioned spending time with horses as a kid." she smiled. "And yeah, well spotted. He's half Shire, half Arabian."
	"...either something terrible happened, or the bravest Arabian of all time managed to get his hooves on a stepladder." Connor grinned.
	Chloe burst into laughter, and Bucephalus whinnied too, almost mimicking.
	"He's a good mix." Connor cleared his throat. "He's got a Shire's build and temperament, but his facial expressions look more Arabian to me. I'm guessing he's a smart lad?"
	"Oh yeah, he's clever." she beamed. "He's a really good listener."
	"Do you ride him often?"
	"No, he's Victor's." she shrugged. "But he prefers me."
	Bucephalus neighed, as if in agreement. Connor chuckled, and scratched him gently by the nape of his neck.
	"I like the name." Connor smiled. "I'm sure good old Alexander would think he's a worthy bearer of the title."
	"Ugh, that might be the one thing you and Victor have in common - you both know who the original Bucephalus was." she grinned.
	"Hey, my dog's name is Spartacus, so I can't judge." he smirked back. "Does Victor know it means 'Ox-headed?'"
	She laughed. "You know, the name might be more fitting now. I've never met an animal as stubborn as ol' Bucey."
	The horse grunted in protest, and pushed his head back into her.
	"Hey! You know it's true!" she chuckled, pushing back against him. True to his name, he didn't budge.
	Sparky gently approached, and raised his snout upwards. The two animals sniffed each other, and Bucephalus moved his head away.
	"I think he likes us." Sparky shrugged.
	Chloe chuckled. "Anyway, there you have it. You've met the Derringtons, now."
	Connor smiled warmly. "It's been really nice. Hey... don't feel like you have to hide your family from me, or any of us, okay? None of us would've judged you over it."
	"I know." she nodded, hesitating. "I... judge *myself* over it. I hate the whole thing. The whole stupid system, the attitude, the ideology..."
	Connor reached his hand over Bucephalus's back, and placed it on hers. She blushed, and looked down.
	"None of that is *you,* okay?" he said quietly. "Yes, us down in Drackhill are more... well, we're not exactly huge lovers of the class system, or the aristocracy. But *you* are not part of that. As far as any of us care, you're Chloe - our mate, who shows up when we need her; who has a couple of vodkas and can start out-infodumping me; who's always looking out for all of us. *That's* the Chloe we know - and nothing I've learned in the last 24 hours changes that."
	Her eyes fluttered, and she looked into his eyes. She squeezed his hand, before grimacing.
	"Ew! Bucey! Way to ruin the moment!" she laughed, as a dollop of manure plopped onto the grass.
	They both burst into laughter, as Bucephalus trotted away. Then, they embraced.
	"I'll see you after New Year's, yeah?" she smiled.
	"Yeah. I'll see you then." he beamed. With that, they parted.
