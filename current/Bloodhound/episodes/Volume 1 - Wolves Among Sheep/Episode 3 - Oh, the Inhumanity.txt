SCENE - Beating the Alleyway Mugger

	Dark clouds had covered the skies, now. The sun crawled beneath the horizon, as Connor went out for a jog. He'd been running for hours, pushing harder, faster, waiting for the burn to come. But it didn't. His hearts pounded - not from exhaustion, but from something deeper. Restlessness. Fear.
	Connor closed his eyes as he ran. He didn't need them - he didn't need to *see* the city. He *felt* it.
	He wanted to pant. Wanted to struggle. Wanted to be weak again.
	Wanted to be *human* again.
	He imagined. Imagined what his heartbeat used to sound like.
	Thump-thump. Thump-thump. Thump-thump.
	His real heartbeats pounded in the walls of his skull, like drums.
	Thump-thump-thump-thump. Thump-thump-thump-thump.
	One-two-three-four. One-two-three-four.
	He tried to remember what breathing was like.
	His lungs filled as he gasped. His lungs emptied as he sighed.
	Not anymore.
	One set of lungs rose as another fell. Like a bird in flight, he felt the air flow seamlessly.
	He stopped in his tracks, and gripped his knees, tensing.
	The images flashed in his mind once more.
	Pain.
	Hate.
	Death.
	Then... a noise.
	His head turned sharply. His eyes ripped open.
	A faint sound.
	Impossibly distant, but clear as if it was right next to him.
	A scuffle.
	A voice. No... a cry.
	The sound of fists hitting flesh.
	The sound sharpened. A struggle. Someone being beaten.
	He scowled, as the black gel drizzled out of his face into a bandana. Before he could think, he was sprinting again.
	"Stop," he could hear Tommy say. "Not a soul."
	He stood in the cold, his hearts racing.
	No.
	Someone needed help. That was the important bit.
	The world blurred past him, and in what felt like seconds, he'd crossed half a mile. He stumbled to a halt, staring down the alleyway.
	A young man was crumpled on the ground, clutching his ribs, blood running from his nose. A larger man loomed over him, rifling through his bag, a baseball bat leaning against the wall within reach.
	"Get away from him." Connor barked, his voice a low growl that startled even himself. "Now."
	"Or what? Fuck off!" the mugger jeered.
	Connor didn't answer. He started walking, his hands tightening into fists.
	The thug grabbed the bat and swung, the metal whistling through the air. It cracked against Connor's head with a deafening clang, but Connor barely flinched.
	For a moment, the thug froze, staring at him with wide, disbelieving eyes. He swung again.
	The bat cracked against his skull, and Connor felt a dull throb, a brief sting. It was nothing. The impact sent a ripple through his body - not of pain, but of... excitement?
	The thug's eyes widened, his grip on the bat slipping. He staggered back.
	"Who the fuck are you?!"
	"My turn." Connor growled.
	He lashed out with a punch, holding back as much as he could. If he were still human, it would have been the equivalent of a light jab - the kind meant to distract, not to damage. The thug staggered, wheezing as the blow crumpled him like a folding chair. He gasped, clutching his ribs, and swung the bat again, this time with trembling hands.
	Connor caught it mid-swing, the force rattling his arm but not slowing him. He bent and twisted the metal, folding it in half.
	"Go!" he snapped, turning to the young man. "Get out of here. Now!"
	The victim hesitated for only a moment before scrambling to his feet and running, his footsteps echoing down the alley.
	Connor turned back to the thug, who was crawling backward, clutching his side.
	"I'm sorry! I'm fucking sorry!"
	Connor's lips curled into a smile. He lunged forward, grabbing the thug by the leg and twisting. The snap of bone was vile. The thug screamed, writhing in agony, and Connor grinned.
	The Rage slithered into his mind, from the depths of his subconscious.
	The realisation hit him like a cold wave. He was smiling. Enjoying it.
	He felt the black tar force itself from his palm. He looked down, and the freakish gel had formed a claw around his hand, ready to kill.
	Horrified, he released the man and stumbled back, his chest heaving. The thug's cries filled the alley, but all Connor could hear was his own twinned hearts, thundering like a drum.
	He yanked his red hood up, turned, and ran.

	Arriving outside his home, he saw a black SUV parked outside.
	The same make and model as the one used by the men that killed him...
	He darted into cover, hiding in an alleyway. He peeked out, spotting the driver open the door, hop out, pull out a cigarette, and lean back against the van.
	Wasting no time, he rushed over. Before the man could react, he wrapped his hands around his neck. He avoided squeezing too hard - he wanted answers, not another kill.
	"WHAT DO YOU PEOPLE WANT FROM ME?!" he screamed, smacking the man's head against the van.
	The man desperately gripped at Connor's arms, his face red, as spittle formed around his mouth.
	"ANSWER ME!" Connor demanded.
	"GET AWAY FROM HIM!" a woman screamed, hitting Connor as hard as she could. He barely budged, but let go in surprise.
	The man choked for breath. She caught him, helping to cushion his fall.
	"WHAT THE FUCK IS WRONG WITH YOU?!" she roared.
	Connor stood there, shocked.
	"S--sorry." he stammered. "I--I made a mistake."
	He turned again, and carried on running.
	He hated himself. Twice in a night, he'd hurt someone, and ran away.
	What did that make him?
	Coward?
	Monster?
	Freak?

SCENE - Deerstalker

	Back home, Connor was in his bathroom, freshening up. He looked at his reflection. He gently, gingerly placed his hand on the mirror, and sighed deeply.
	He then stared deeply into his eyes, like he was focusing. He looked closer and closer - trying to hunt for abnormalities.
	Whatever he was, it wasn't quite human. Was he an alien? Was his body even his own?
	As he dissociated, the world turned greyscale once more. He gasped, noticing the change - feeling the warmth in his eyesockets again, and seeing the glow in the mirror.
	Now focusing, and unburdened by the disorientation of his transformation, he took a closer look. The glow appeared limited to his irises - but it was bright. Bright enough to cast streaks across the glass like lens flares.
	It was a terrifying red. They glowed with a demonic quality, as though fire might erupt from them. Surrounding his eyesockets, ink-black veins like those of the men who tortured him jutted around.
	"Whoa..." he whispered, then gasped.
	The noises he made - he 'saw' the noise. The way the vibrations travelled through the air. The ripples coming from his mouth. Not in the mirror - he saw it come from the bottom of his field of vision, and cast outwards.
	He held his hand aloft, and snapped his fingers. Again, he 'saw' the sound come from it.
	"Super-vision?" he pondered aloud, mesmerised by the sight of his voice.
	Then, he closed his eyes, and shook his head. When he reopened his eyelids, the world looked normal again. He clapped his hands, but could only hear the noise.
	He looked back in the mirror, and confirmed they'd returned to their previous steel-grey, and the veins had faded back into his skin.
	Eager to explore this strange new perception, he closed his eyes. He focused his listening - picking up even the smallest sounds. His own heartbeats. The sound of pipes throughout his tenement block. People talking, walking, arguing, or even snoring in their own homes. Noises a human couldn't ever hear. And they were deafening. His ears would put a bat to shame.
	He covered his ears, and the sound died down. Then, another idea.
	He applied this same 'tuning' to his vision, instead. His vision had gotten better since his rebirth, but now it was stronger than even that. He could see tiny micro-fractures in the mirror. The imperceptible ridges within the ceramic sink. He looked down at his hand, and saw the fingerprints and follicles and pores with complete precision. His eyes would put a falcon to shame.
	Then, he shifted from his eyes to his nose and mouth. He sniffed the air like a dog, and instinctively, he could recognise chemical signals he'd never even perceived. The toothpaste in the tube on his sink was at least 4 months old. As for the water in the plughole, he could tell it was soft. There was a stagnant stench coming from down the pipes.
	He'd done a remarkable job of cleaning away the biley vomit and blood in the bathroom, but the lingering micro-stains railed into his nostrils. He closed his eyes, and instinctively was able to tap where the stains were, simply locating them within his mental map by the proximity of their stink. His nose would put a wolf to shame.
	Finally, he closed his eyes and ran his fingers over a piece of paper. He could feel the faint impressions of ink - and he could read it.
	Tunable senses. Useless, though, if activating them would just overwhelm him.
	That's when it hit him.
	He re-activated the synaesthetic 'super-vision,' and tuned his hearing back up. The noise was strong in his ears, but... bearable.
	Why wouldn't it be? He didn't need to 'hear' the sounds - now he could just 'see' them. Auditory input data was now being handled by optical processors. Like a load-balancing server.
	He looked around. Where he had heard the noises before, now he could see them. His hearing was strong enough that he could even see the heartbeats of the people on his floor.
	He looked at one heartbeat in particular - chosen at random - and focused his attention on it. Colour returned, but only on the rippling waves he could now see. They were bright red, like his eyes were now. He looked away, and looked back - the colour was still there, calling him.
	Now, he closed his eyes. Despite the thick skin covering his irises, he could still see rather clearly.
	At first, he assumed he was simply seeing through the skin, such was the power of his vision. But it was more than that - despite his eyes being closed, his other super-senses were defined enough to help him visualise his surroundings in lieu of sight. Even if you gouged out his eyes, the feeling of air disturbances on his skin; the tiny sounds of microscopic insects in his ears; the scent tags of chemicals in his nose. He could 'see' it all.
	The perfect hunting system.
	He shook his head, turning it all off.
	"Hunting system?" he whispered aloud. "What the fuck am I thinking?"

	It had gotten dark now. To clear his mind, Connor went out for a jog - in the woodlands on the northern side of Wyrmrock.
	He ran and ran and ran, like a dog off the leash. He leapt over a stream, hitting the bank on the other side with a notable thud. Undaunted, he kept running. Miles passed, as he effortlessly looped around the woods.
	He came to a stop, and caught his breath in seconds. He crouched low, and looked out into the darkness.
	There, he saw it. A deer - a red stag, to be precise. It had only just noticed him, and had gone still, waiting to see if he would chase.
	Something inside him pushed. Maybe his adrenaline, his super-senses, or just the thrill of his new-found powers. Maybe it was something darker. Inhuman. Feral.
	Maybe it was all of that.
	He smirked, and pounced.
	The stag bolted away, running as fast as its legs would take it. Ordinarily, no human could dream of keeping pace here. But Connor wasn't an ordinary human anymore...
	He gave chase, at points even running on all fours like an animal. His hearts raced, and his ears could sense the heartbeat of the deer. His super-vision activated automatically, and he saw the vibrations of his terrified prey. The heartbeat was, like before, highlighted in a blood-red glow, matching his demonic eyes.
	This was pure thrill. An almost divine experience of animalism. Predator and prey.
	Connor slowed down, toying with his quarry, letting the chase go on for longer. He climbed a tree, and leapt in front of the stag, causing it to panic and turn frantically. Connor grinned with savage excitement, and continued the chase, like a cat playing with a mouse.
	He continued to pounce from tree to tree, instinctively knowing exactly where to grip, before leaping down and landing right on the stag.
	The poor creature yelped as its legs gave out, and without hesitation, Connor gripped its antlers and twisted. The stag shuddered, then went still.
	In seconds, Connor descended on its body. His teeth ripped past its hide and pulled out chunks of hot red meat. Blood sprayed from the entry wounds, as he pulled his kill apart and savagely devoured it.
	This was true life. An animal, and its food.
	He tore out another chunk, and felt the abysmal hunger that tormented him since his transformation fade away.
	Killing, and eating. That was what the Demon in his skull demanded.
	He swallowed, and went for another piece, before he finally realised what he had done.
	He looked down at his bloodied hands, then at the half-eaten stag, before standing, spitting out a chunk of meat and fur, and vomiting.
	Then, he stood, vomited again, and cried. Oh, how he cried.
	He howled in despair and sorrow, and clawed at his face.
	He cradled the dead stag, and tried to say "I'm sorry," but could only manage mumbles between sobs.
	It was a truly pitiful sight. A bloodied man, inelegantly blubbering over his kill.
	The awful headaches he'd been feeling had gone, but something worse gnawed at his very soul.
	Horror. Fear. Rage. Hate.
	Inhumanity.

SCENE - Library/Meet-Cute
