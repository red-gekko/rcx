Army:
	The Army is the main fighting force of the military. While not as well equipped as the Knights or as augmented as the Rangers, they make up for it in good training and numbers.
	The Army is usually used for occupation, reconstruction, disaster relief, and conventional warfare - though they have the Marines, who use a scaled-down power armour; and the Scouts, lone operatives who specialise in black ops warfare. This allows an Army Group to perform operations usually reserved for Knights and Rangers, in the event that they are indisposed. In fact, both groups often recruit from the army.
	In peacetime, the army will focus on providing civil service and public works. Indeed, on many worlds, the army is a large employer simply because they're so organised, and after all, your average infantryman can always be put to work in all sorts of places.

Navy:
	If the Army is the military's fist, the Navy is its backbone that allows it to swing. Navy ships range from tiny personal shuttles all the way to million-man Star Castles. The Navy spends its time facilitating trade, handling troop transportation, and providing aerial and astral support.
	While the Navy has no dedicated combat staff beyond ship security, any given unit typically has combat staff seconded or permanently attached to it. Likewise, rare is the military group that doesn't have a naval unit attached to it. For this reason, the Navy is often seen less as an independent branch of the military, and more a service within the military that all other forces use. That being said, naval Security Staff are often given similar training to the Army, and the Navy enjoys a very close relationship with the Marines, with several Marine units seeing themselves more as Naval than Army personnel.
	In Peacetime, the Navy focuses on protecting merchant ships from pirates, and often even ferrying strategic goods between systems itself.

Knights
	The Knights are a feudal order. They focus heavily on elite training and augmentation. Knights are usually deployed as Orders, though when engaging in campaigns they will usually submit themselves to Army authority. Their 'Equivalent Ranks' indicate what rank they count as in Army hierarchy, so for instance only a Colonel or above could order a Knight-Militant with authority.
	Due to their elite status and lower numbers, Knights are typically deployed in assault formations, routing enemy forces to allow the Army to actually hold ground. Knights rarely engage in conventional warfare - though they typically have Levies, Army units subordiante to the Order, as well as Knights-Errant, their version of lone operatives and essentially lesser Rangers. Their Levies tend to have a disproportionate number of Marines - likely as a good recruitment pool.
	In Peacetime, the Knights typically focus on their training, attempting to recover any losses and expand their numbers. Once they reach a full Legion strength, they will begin recruiting to form a new Legion.
	Knights usually dedicate themselves to one or more of the High Gods, though the vast majority are devoted to Naraz and Skolazzar; hence their religious iconography and cybernetic augmentation.
	In terms of battlefield prowess, a freshly qualified Knight-Aspirant is equivalent to 10 normal Army soldiers. A Suborder of Knights could probably beat a Brigade of soldiers. This is partly down to their elite training, partly to their augmentation, and partly to their tactics. A single Knight is typically able to act as a largely independant agent - the reason a squadron leader in the Knights commands 3 times the number of an army Lance Corporal is because the Knight-Militant needs to do less 'commanding'. As such, in an open battle, the Suborder would likely focus on decapitation strikes to break down the enemy's communication, and then mop them up in their confusion.

Warmaster
	A special rank who is in charge of multiple forces, be they Army Groups, Naval Groups, or Knightly Orders.
Grand Warmaster
	An extremely special rank - the Grand Warmaster answers only to the Council. Uniquely, their commands can apply to Rangers.

Ranger ranks:
	Rangers are highly augmented and experienced lone operatives. They answer to the Rangers General, who in turn answers only to the Galactic Senate. In cases where the Senate authorises the foundation of a Grand Warmaster, they will submit to them, but it is expected that they will maintain operational independence.
	They typically recruit from Knight-Errants, as they are elite lone operatives. A KE becoming a ranger is a great honour to his Order.
	Sending a Ranger to deal with a problem is like sending a Brigade. Their elite skills and heavy augmentation allows them to wreak a great deal of havoc, while being lone operatives allows them to stay on the move and pick their battles.
	Becoming a Ranger has strict requirements. Typically, a Ranger-Initiate must have mastered at least one psychic discipline, and either undergone intense gene therapy or cybernetic enhancement (or for synth Initiates, advanced hardware upgrades). They must also be fully trained in both covert ops and at least one other conventional warfare school.
	Rangers do not command other units - they are usually outside the typical military structure. If the Army, Navy, and Knights are the Council's Right Hand, the Rangers are its left. That being said, it is unusual for even Initiates to not have Retinues - groups of staff from other units that an individual Ranger has recruited as aides and allies. Likewise, they will typically have Proxies - front-groups that they run from behind the scenes. Also, Rangers may sometimes work together in a Strike Team - gods help you if you've done something to get a Strike Team mobilised against you - the next step up is a full Army Group or Knight Order being deployed.
	Because of their familiarity working alone, it is rare for Rangers to work like this. A Ranger that has a higher rank than another can issue commands, but rarely will a Ranger actually 'manage' their subordinates. If you need someone to tell you what to do beyond the initial mission brief, you're not Ranger material. 
	Rangers rarely end up in peacetime scenarios - as long as there's trouble in the galaxy, a Ranger will get deployed. That being said, when not on mission Rangers tend to focus on training and recruitment, much like the Knights.
